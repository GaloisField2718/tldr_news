NEWS & TRENDS

 CARING VS. KNOWING (5 MINUTE READ) [6]

 While AI lowers software development costs, supporting a growing
audience demands continuous learning and quality standards. Developers
face challenges in managing user diversity and the risks of unchecked
customization. Ultimately, maintaining quality requires an ongoing
commitment.

 THE STAKEHOLDER WHO MATTERS MOST (6 MINUTE READ) [7]

 The industry overlooks the primary stakeholder: the user. Current
evaluation metrics measure machine functionality rather than whether
an agent actually reduces effort for humans. Consequently, companies
fail to instrument the complete customer journey, leading to hidden
inference costs and unmeasured user abandonment.

OPINIONS & TUTORIALS

 ‘NOT ENOUGH USAGE': YOUR BIGGEST CHURN REASON IS A BROKEN HABIT
LOOP (16 MINUTE READ) [8]

 "Not enough usage" is the primary driver of subscription app churn,
often accounting for 26% to 40% of cancellations. This churn reason is
frequently due to a broken habit loop rather than a pricing problem.
Using discounts to fix usage churn is ineffective because lowering the
price does not restore user value or engagement.

 NOT EVERY QUESTION DESERVES A STUDY (8 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Every research question should be assigned a specific price in
minutes, days, or an owned risk before any investigation begins. By
implementing a five-step routing discipline, product teams can
determine whether an answer already exists in organizational memory,
warrants a rapid study, or should be bypassed entirely with an
accepted risk written down.

 THE PROBLEM WITH PUTTING PEOPLE IN BOXES (6 MINUTE READ) [14]

 Personality labels can hide how much behavior depends on context.
Understanding what causes people to switch modes may tell you more
than knowing their “type.”

 AI ON TOP OF A DYSFUNCTIONAL SYSTEM (1): THE PRODUCT BACKLOG (14
MINUTE READ) [15]

 Applying generative AI to a dysfunctional product backlog masks
underlying flaws by polishing artifacts without resolving root causes
like missing customer evidence or decision authority. AI-generated
user stories obscure a lack of real product discovery. To remain
effective, product teams must maintain clear direction, authentic
authority, and feedback loops tied to real outcomes.
