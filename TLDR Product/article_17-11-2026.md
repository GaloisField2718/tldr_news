NEWS & TRENDS

 WHY THE ART OF PRODUCT IS GOING TO LOOK VERY DIFFERENT IN FIVE YEARS
(11 MINUTE READ) [6]

 AI coding tools and rapid prototyping are shifting product management
over the next five years. As software creation accelerates, PMs must
focus on strategic judgment, deep customer intimacy, taste, and
managing AI agents alongside cross-functional teams.

 WHAT IF THE TEAM CONSISTED OF TWO PEOPLE? (15 MINUTE READ) [7]

 As AI tools enable two-person pods to build features rapidly,
traditional multi-person practices like daily standups break down. To
harness this speed, organizations must replace command-and-control
structures with intention-based leadership, allowing small teams to
make autonomous decisions based on outcome-driven KPIs.

OPINIONS & TUTORIALS

 STRATEGIC BUILDING BLOCKS (5 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Product strategy involves building blocks across three scales: fast
moves, middle moves, and slow moves. Leaders must execute and sequence
moves deliberately at their scale, navigating visibility barriers that
obscure wider executive plans from fine-grained teams.

 THE TWO LAYERS OF PRIORITIZATION (4 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Prioritizing via headcount allocation sets fixed strategic capacity
for durable teams, avoiding subjective frameworks like RICE. By
assigning headcount based on strategic importance rather than
comparing all projects in one backlog, leadership enforces clear
strategy while reducing prioritization fatigue.

 DRIVING PRODUCT ADOPTION WHEN THE BUYER ISN'T NECESSARILY THE USER (6
MINUTE READ) [13]

 Enterprise B2B adoption often stalls due to misaligned incentives
between buyers, admins, and end users. Product marketers should
diagnose friction via targeted user interviews - offering clear admin
checklists, prescriptive first steps, and tracking actual usage rather
than relying on vanity metrics.

 DELIVER PRODUCTS WITH UNCLEAR SCOPE (9 MINUTE READ) [14]

 A software engineering manager outlines a framework for delivering
fixed-price projects with unclear scope to non-technical stakeholders.
By pairing a fixed architectural core with flexible modules and
combining waterfall milestones with agile feedback, complex systems
can be delivered reliably on time.
