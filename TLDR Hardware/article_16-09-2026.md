HEADLINES AND LAUNCHES

 STARSHIP FLIGHT 14 TARGETS FIRST FULL ORBITAL LAUNCH, NET SEPTEMBER
22 (3 MINUTE READ) [4]

 SpaceX has set Starship Flight 14 as its first attempt at a full
orbital mission, currently targeted no earlier than September 22 (an
earlier FCC filing had pointed to September 15), with Booster 21 and
Ship 41. The flight is expected to deploy next-gen Starlink V3
satellites, which SpaceX says carry over ten times the capacity of
current satellites for "fiber-like" speeds, marking the first time
Starship has released operational payloads in orbit rather than flying
a suborbital trajectory. Musk confirmed the upper stage won't attempt
a tower catch this time, that capability is being saved for Flight 15,
where he's given it a 50-60% success chance, while Booster 21 will
most likely attempt a soft splashdown in the Gulf of Mexico following
an anomaly during Booster 20's boostback and landing.

 AGILITY ROBOTICS UNVEILS DIGIT 5 HUMANOID BUILT FOR COOPERATIVELY
SAFE WORK AT SCALE (2 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Agility Robotics has introduced Digit 5, a next-generation humanoid
robot featuring a 50-pound payload capacity, swappable end-effectors,
and a rapid nine-minute recharge capability. The system incorporates a
holistic safety architecture powered by onboard sensors and advanced
safety controllers to autonomously slow down, avoid, or stop when
people are detected nearby.

ENGINEERING AND APPLICATIONS

 NVIDIA OPEN-SOURCES OSMO TO ORCHESTRATE TRAINING, SIMULATION, AND
ROBOT TESTING FROM ONE YAML FILE (6 MINUTE READ) [6]

 NVIDIA OSMO is an open-source, Kubernetes-native Physical AI
orchestrator that connects data generation, RL training, simulation,
evaluation, and hardware-in-the-loop testing across heterogeneous
compute. Developers describe the pipeline in one YAML file, while OSMO
schedules work across data-center GPUs, RTX simulation systems, cloud
infrastructure, and edge platforms including Jetson and Arm. The
system also exposes pipeline state, datasets, and available GPU
capacity to coding agents.

 OPEN SOURCE SAFETY CONSORTIUM FOR PHYSICAL AUTONOMY LAUNCHED (3
MINUTE READ) [7]

 Move fast and break things is great, until your robot hurts a person.
Creating robots that can safely operate around people is incredibly
important, but it is also incredibly time-consuming and expensive. The
Open Source Safety Consortium aims to fix this using open source. The
consortium is an industry collaborative initiative dedicated to
defining standardized safety frameworks, reproducible verification
procedures, and validation benchmarks for open-source physical AI
software stacks. The organization is attempting to bridge the gap
between open-source robotic software innovation and rigorous
industrial safety certifications. The hope is that future robotics
projects can use turn-key products and libraries to build safe robots
out of the gate.

 WAYMO OPENS FULLY AUTONOMOUS LAS VEGAS RIDES WITH ITS 6TH-GEN DRIVER
(4 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Waymo started providing fully autonomous public rides in Las Vegas,
expanding commercial deployment of its physical-AI driving stack. Las
Vegas is among the first cities operating primarily with Waymo's Ojai
vehicle platform powered by its sixth-generation Waymo Driver, across
a nearly 24-mile service area. More than 100,000 people had registered
interest.

 STRETCHABLE QLED HITS 16,000 PPI WITHOUT GOING DARK WHEN PULLED (2
MINUTE READ) [9]

 Older stretchable screens only flexed the wiring, so fixed pixels
thinned out and picture quality collapsed as you pulled. A
UNIST/DGIST/IBS team printed intrinsically stretchable quantum-dot
pixels at about 16,000 PPI and 53,300 nits that keep working at 1.65×
stretch, so wearable and e-skin displays can stay outdoor-bright when
bent.

 META SAYS ITS THIRD-GEN IN-HOUSE AI CHIP BEATS NVIDIA ON EFFICIENCY,
DEPLOYING BY EARLY 2027 (4 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Meta shares rose after Bloomberg reported the company plans to deploy
its third-generation MTIA chip, internally called MTIA 450 or Arke, in
data centers during the first half of 2027, with the next version,
MTIA 500 (Astrid), set to finish design in about a month and reach
data centers by the end of 2027. Twelve new sample chips arrived from
TSMC on September 1 performing within 2-3% of simulations, and Yee
Jiun Song, the VP overseeing Meta's custom silicon program, said each
generation delivers better performance per watt and per dollar than
current Nvidia offerings running the same AI models. Meta is
developing the chips with Broadcom on design and TSMC on
manufacturing, part of a broader push to diversify its silicon supply
and reduce reliance on Nvidia.

 ASTERA LABS' NEW LEO X CONTROLLER LETS MEMORY EXPANDERS PLUG DIRECTLY
INTO AI ACCELERATOR FABRIC NETWORKS (5 MINUTE READ) [11]

 Astera Labs' second-gen Leo 2 CXL memory controllers move from PCIe
Gen5/CXL 2.x to Gen6/CXL 3.2 and double memory channels from 2 to 4,
doubling both bandwidth and total capacity to up to 768GB of DDR4 or
4TB of DDR5 per expander, with new RAS features like hardware DIMM
testing and soft-ECC on top of hardware ECC, aimed at economically
reusing cheap, aging DDR4 pulled from retired servers rather than
buying expensive new DDR5 amid the ongoing memory shortage.

SERVER (3 MINUTE READ) [14]

 Micron demonstrated a 512GB DDR5 RDIMM, doubling per-slot capacity by
vertically stacking DRAM dies inside the package and connecting them
with through-silicon vias, the same die-stacking technique HBM uses,
letting a 24-slot dual-socket server hold 12TB without changing the
module count or footprint.
