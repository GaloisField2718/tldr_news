HEADLINES AND LAUNCHES

 MICROSOFT TAKES MAIA TO MULTI-GIGAWATT SCALE, MAKING IT THE DEFAULT
INFERENCE CHIP FOR ITS OWN PRODUCTS (4 MINUTE READ) [4]

 Starting in 2027, Microsoft will deploy Maia at multi-gigawatt scale
and make it the main inference system for its products. The
next-generation Maia 300 uses compute chiplets on TSMC's N2P process
and targets long-context, multi-step agent workloads. Digitimes
reports that the supply chain is already preparing for a surge in
server builds in 2027.

 INTRODUCING AMD HELION, AGENTIC AI COMPUTE AT RACK SCALE (10 MINUTE
READ) [5]

 AMD's Helion agentic AI rack is designed for flexible AI
infrastructure that caters to hyperscale deployments and enterprise
needs. It supports a range of configurations, including CPU-only and
GPU-integrated systems, aligning with diverse AI workloads. Helion,
part of an upcoming open blueprint by AMD for OCP, is set for release
in November.

ENGINEERING AND APPLICATIONS

 PHYSICAL INTELLIGENCE OPENS A BETA API TO FINE-TUNE AND HOST Π ROBOT
MODELS (3 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Physical Intelligence opened a beta API for its π robotics
foundation models: upload demonstrations, fine-tune on Pi-managed
compute, and run the result on your own robot through a hosted
inference gateway. Early partners spanned hardware from Actor's
autonomous wheel loader, running a commercial deployment with no
operator onboard, to Almond's dual-arm Axol, which learned to deburr
its own spacers in four days. Since most ran cloud-hosted inference,
network connectivity was a pain point for some.

 BUILDING A GPU COMPILER FROM SCRATCH, FROM PYTORCH TO CUDA (10 MINUTE
READ) [7]

 This deep dive uses the Emmy compiler framework to follow PyTorch
operations down to CUDA one lowering stage at a time: Tensor IR, Loop
IR, Tile IR, Kernel IR, and finally emitted code. It uses RMSNorm and
matrix multiplication as worked examples. It also covers operator
fusion, schedule selection, and online attention rewrites, so readers
can see what Triton-style stacks do under the hood.

 APPLE'S MINIONS POOLS IDLE MACS INTO A VIRTUAL GPU CLOUD (10 MINUTE
READ) [9]

 Apple Silicon Macs have powerful GPUs that sit mostly idle in normal
use. Minions combines them across machines into a virtual GPU cloud
for large ML inference jobs, with fault tolerance and flexible task
scheduling. The pitch is that teams can use hardware they already own
instead of waiting for expensive, centralized GPU capacity.

 ANTHROPIC RUNS A 26B MODEL ACROSS 15 $55 TV BOXES TO TEST IOT
SELF-REPLICATION (5 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Anthropic's Frontier Red Team ran Gemma4-26B-A4B across 15 off-brand
Android TV boxes with 4GB of RAM each, using pipeline parallelism at
0.39 tokens per second, to test whether small models could replicate
across cheap IoT hardware. A 9B model got inference running on 8 of 9
hardware types tested, and a Kimwolf-sized botnet would total only
about 40 H100s of compute. By their trend line, small models reach the
needed capability in simplified tests by year-end.

MISCELLANEOUS

 APPLE HOME COLLECTION BRINGS LG AND SCHNEIDER CAMERAS, A LOCK, AND
THERMOSTATS TO APPLE HOME (3 MINUTE READ) [11]

 Apple's Home Collection, built with LG and Schneider Electric, covers
cameras, thermostats, sensors, a doorbell, and a deadbolt. LG's lock
uses Ultra Wideband to unlock as you approach and works locally over
Matter and Aliro, while its indoor cam physically tilts away when
someone's home. Every device supports Thread, and cameras keep
recording to built-in storage if Wi-Fi drops. Prices run $199.95 to
$399.95, with availability starting in December.

 WHAT A 1GW AI DATA CENTER COSTS: ABOUT $30B, AND $17B OF IT IS CHIPS
(10 MINUTE READ) [12]

 An Abilene-scale build requires roughly $17B for chips and servers,
$3B for power, and only $2.4B for construction. Transformers,
generators, and cooling equipment face long lead times and rising
prices.
