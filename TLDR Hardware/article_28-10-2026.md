HEADLINES AND LAUNCHES

 ORACLE CLOUD BECOMES THE FIRST HYPERSCALER OUTSIDE GOOGLE TO OFFER
TPUS, WITH CAPACITY FROM 2027 (4 MINUTE READ) [4]

 TPU capacity will be deployed in OCI's liquid-cooled data centers,
tied in through Oracle's Acceleron SmartNIC fabric. Google says its
eighth-generation TPU delivers up to 12.6 PFLOPS of FP4 per chip at up
to 2x performance per watt, sustains over 97% goodput with optical
circuit switching, and cuts network hops by over 50% for up to 80%
better low-latency serving performance per dollar.

 COWBOY SPACE'S BIG IRON ROCKET TURNS ITS UPPER STAGE INTO AN ORBITING
MEGAWATT-CLASS DATA CENTER (3 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Big Iron is an 86 m stainless-steel, road-transportable rocket that
can carry 15,000+ kg to sun-synchronous orbit on nine 300,000 lbf
methalox ‘Clydesdale' engines. The upper stage can stay in orbit as
a data center with five racks of Vera Rubin GPUs, with the rocket skin
doubling as the radiator and ammonia propellant as coolant. Its first
flight is targeted for 2028, with up to 20,000 satellites planned
pending FCC approval.

ENGINEERING AND APPLICATIONS

 TESTING SILICON PHOTONICS AT SCALE (5 MINUTE READ) [6]

 The push for silicon photonics in data centers requires testing
infrastructure that matches evolving demands. Automated alignment and
integrated optical/electrical testing are critical for overcoming
challenges in coupling efficiency and throughput. Collaboration
between equipment providers, developers, and manufacturers is
essential to create a seamless production system for high-volume
manufacturing.

 ANDURIL AND QUALCOMM ANNOUNCE BLUECHIP, A HARDENED LAPTOP WITH ABOUT
90% US OR ALLIED PARTS (4 MINUTE READ) [7]

 BlueChip runs a Snapdragon X Series chip built at TSMC's Arizona fab,
with about 90% of its bill of materials from the US or allies and a
roadmap to 100% domestic. The display is one component still made only
in China. It's aimed at government and military buyers rather than
consumers. The chip is expected to cost about 20% more than typical
high-end PCs, and open for preorder in 2027. Anduril hasn't finished
contracting its suppliers.

RESEARCH AND PROTOTYPES

 FIRST 32-BIT MCU FAMILY BUILT ON 22NM PROCESS AND FEATURING MRAM (4
MINUTE READ) [8]

 Renesas has launched the RX74M and RX74N, the first 32-bit MCUs using
a 22nm process with embedded MRAM, reaching speeds up to 600MHz. These
MCUs offer significant performance improvements and support major
industrial networks like EtherCAT and TSN with integrated controllers.
They enhance security with hardware-based features and support
post-quantum cryptography, while a partner ecosystem aids development
efficiency.

 PROPOSAL READS AN ANDREEV QUBIT'S STATE THROUGH ITS SUPERCURRENT AND
A SQUID, WITH NO ANCILLA QUBITS (3 MINUTE READ) [9]

 A theory paper proposes reading an Andreev qubit without ancilla
qubits: its state shows up as an oscillating supercurrent in a large
superconducting loop, and a small coil coupled to a SQUID picks up the
magnetic signal. The method still disturbs the qubit, but within
standard error-correction thresholds. It's a proposal in Physical
Review B, supported by FAPESP, with no experiment yet.

MISCELLANEOUS

 INTEL TO ACQUIRE QUANTUM SOFTWARE MAKER CLASSIQ TO PAIR IT WITH
SILICON SPIN-QUBIT CHIPS (2 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Intel has agreed to acquire Classiq, pairing its hardware-agnostic
algorithm synthesis platform with Intel's silicon spin-qubit chips and
cryogenic control electronics, which Intel says it alone can make at
scale on conventional fab processes. The goal is a full-stack platform
that adds QPUs alongside Intel's CPUs, GPUs, and ASICs. Intel plans to
keep Classiq's multi-platform integrations.

 FUSION'S SUPPLY CHAIN COULD GROW FROM $500M TO OVER $7B BY THE FIRST
PLANTS, WITH THE TRITIUM FUEL CYCLE THE KEY HURDLE (4 MINUTE READ)
[11]

 The fusion industry is close to delivering a roadmap to commercial
power plants. The fuel cycle, especially tritium handling, is one of
the most important development areas.
