HEADLINES AND LAUNCHES

 A16Z LAUNCHES A $1.1B "MACHINE AGE" FUND TO BANKROLL AI'S PHYSICAL
INFRASTRUCTURE (2 MINUTE READ) [4]

 Andreessen Horowitz raised $1.1 billion for a new "Machine Age" fund,
a notable pivot for a firm best known for backing software, explicitly
aimed at chips, memory, interconnects, data centers, cooling, and
power-efficient edge devices rather than another model or app layer.
The firm frames the bet around needing faster, more efficient systems,
cheaper high-bandwidth memory, and better interconnects between nodes
to keep the AI buildout from being bottlenecked by physical
infrastructure, alongside broader materials, electrical, and real
estate build-out to support it all.

 CXMT SUES THE PENTAGON, ARGUES ITS DRAM IS CIVILIAN JEDEC SPEC, NOT
DEFENSE HARDWARE (3 MINUTE READ) [5]

 CXMT is suing the Department of Defense to overturn its January 2025
classification as a Chinese military-linked company, arguing it has no
ties to the PLA and that the designation lacks evidence and violated
due process. The company says the Pentagon's original case leaned on a
distributor's unauthorized "military grade" ad, later admitted
erroneous, and that its DRAM actually conforms to civilian JEDEC
commercial standards, lacking the temperature, packaging, testing, and
oversight requirements imposed on genuine Chinese military-grade
semiconductors.

ENGINEERING AND APPLICATIONS

 NVIDIA MOVES THE MEMORY CONTROLLER INTO THE HBM STACK (5 MINUTE READ)
[6]

 NVHBM is a design that moves NVIDIA's custom memory controller from
the accelerator into the high-bandwidth-memory base die. The change
frees compute-die area and lets multiple memory providers build
compatible stacks, with Amazon's Annapurna Labs as the first
collaborator.

 META TESTS ROBOTS THAT SWAP CABLES AND RESET SERVERS INSIDE ITS DATA
CENTERS (4 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Meta is piloting robots from Watney Robotics, Kinova, and ABB to
handle physical data center work, evaluating whether a Kinova Gen3
robotic arm can power-cycle servers, testing a separate robot for
swapping networking cables, and running ABB units mounted on wheeled
platforms to reseat components at its newer Prometheus campus in New
Albany, Ohio. It builds on simpler automation already in place,
including self-driving tugger robots that move heavy server racks and
wheeled inventory robots that scan equipment. One Meta worker
estimates a successful cable-swapping robot alone could replace up to
80% of that specific role's workload.

RESEARCH AND PROTOTYPES

 EPFL'S CHARGE-BALANCED GAN TRANSISTOR HITS 3,400 VOLTS, 5X TODAY'S
COMMERCIAL PARTS (3 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Pushing gallium nitride to higher voltages usually causes charge to
pile up in one spot when the transistor switches off, triggering
electrical breakdown. EPFL's POWERlab fixed this by engineering an
additional layer that produces a sheet of mobile holes nearly matching
the electron sheet GaN naturally generates, letting the two charges
cancel out so the electric field spreads evenly instead of spiking,
all from the material's own polarization rather than
temperature-sensitive doping. The result: diodes breaking down above
3.9 kV and transistors sustaining 3.4-3.5 kV, more than 5x the rating
of many commercial GaN devices, holding up at 125°C with under 15%
resistance drift during repeated high-voltage cycling.

 EX-DEEPMIND TEAM CUTS ROBOT TASK TEACHING FROM ONE DAY TO 10 MINUTES
(6 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Reimagine Robotics, founded by former Google DeepMind
applied-robotics lead Jonathan Scholz and other ex-DeepMind engineers,
is building robots that factory workers can post-train directly
through demonstrations and physical corrections. In one industrial
deployment involving recovery of critical materials from used hard
drives, Reimagine says the approach reduced the time needed to teach a
new task from one day to 10 minutes. The architecture shifts
adaptation from centralized robotics engineers toward workers
operating the robots in production environments.

MISCELLANEOUS

 LAMBDA RAISES $1B IN PRIVATE DEBT TO BUY GPUS MICROSOFT WILL LEASE (3
MINUTE READ) [10]

 Lambda raised roughly $1 billion in private short-dated debt,
arranged by JPMorgan, to buy Nvidia GPUs that Microsoft will then
lease, borrowing against Microsoft's willingness to keep paying rather
than its own revenue, letting Microsoft get the compute without
carrying the debt itself. It's Lambda's second such raise this month,
following a $917 million loan two weeks ago secured against a contract
with Nvidia, which is simultaneously its investor, supplier, and
customer, and the company is reportedly in talks for up to $3 billion
more ahead of a possible IPO next year

 SPACEX BUILDS ITS OWN TURBINE-BLADE FOUNDRY TO CUT GAS POWER DELAYS
BY 18 MONTHS (4 MINUTE READ) [11]

 Elon Musk has confirmed that a secretive SpaceX foundry in Bastrop,
Texas is meant to cast turbine blades in-house, tackling a
manufacturing bottleneck he says currently limits how fast natural gas
turbines can come online for AI data centers, up to 18 months faster
if it works. The blades sit in a turbine's hottest section at
3,000-3,600°F, roughly 800°F above the melting point of their own
alloy, survivable only through internal cooling channels, thermal
coatings, and a single-crystal casting process grown slowly in a
vacuum furnace, a technique just four companies worldwide have
mastered at industrial scale, and all are currently sold out.
