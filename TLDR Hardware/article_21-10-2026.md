HEADLINES AND LAUNCHES

 APPLE CONFIRMS OCT 28 EVENT FOR ITS FIRST TOUCH-SCREEN OLED MACBOOK
PRO (2 MINUTE READ) [4]

 Apple confirmed an October 28 event in Los Angeles where it is
expected to debut its first touch-screen Macs: 14- and 16-inch MacBook
Pros with tandem OLED panels, a Dynamic Island in place of the notch,
a lighter build, and M5 Pro and M5 Max chips. Apple is also expected
to launch an entry-level 14-inch MacBook Pro and iMac on the M6, its
first 2nm chip, in unchanged designs, plus an OLED iPad mini. Retail
availability is expected November 6.

 ANTHROPIC AND NSCALE REPORTEDLY NEAR A SECOND $45B COMPUTE DEAL (2
MINUTE READ) [5]

 Anthropic and AI infrastructure provider Nscale are reportedly close
to announcing a second $45 billion compute deal, just over a month
after their first in August, though talks are ongoing and details
could change. This would add to Anthropic's string of massive capacity
commitments, even as some investors worry about spending outpacing
profit. Nscale is also pursuing an IPO, but investors are pushing back
on its proposed $30 billion valuation.

ENGINEERING AND APPLICATIONS

 SPACEMIT ANNOUNCES THE V100 SERVER-CLASS RISC-V PROCESSOR (2 MINUTE
READ) [6]

 SpacemiT claims its V100 chip is the first chip fully compliant with
the RISC-V server specification. The chip packs 46 cores (6 XiangShan
Kunminghu V2R2 plus 40 SpacemiT X100) at 2.2-2.4GHz on 12nm with a
120W TDP, supports eight-channel DDR5-4800 and 52 PCIe 5.0 lanes, and
scales to two sockets and 92 cores. The specs trail Intel Xeon, AMD
EPYC, and Arm server chips, and performance is untested.

 NVIDIA VERA'S MEMORY SYSTEM DELIVERS 1.2 TB/S WITH FLAT LATENCY, BUT
CROSS-SOCKET LINKS LAG (5 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Chips and Cheese's teardown of Vera's fabric found a 1024-bit
LPDDR5X-9600 interface delivering 1.228 TB/s, with latency staying
under 200 ns even as bandwidth load climbs, plus a 164 MB shared L3
and field-serviceable SOCAMM2 modules. NVLink C2C moves up to 780 GB/s
between sockets, but cross-socket core-to-core latency is around 660
ns. Vera trails Graviton 5 on socket-level throughput but leads by
nearly 20% in DRAM-bound SPEC tests.

RESEARCH AND PROTOTYPES

 SPACEX FLIES ITS STARFALL RETURN CAPSULE FOR THE SECOND TIME ON A
32-PAYLOAD RIDESHARE (3 MINUTE READ) [8]

 SpaceX launched its Starfall return capsule for the second time on
the Bandwagon-5 rideshare, one of 32 payloads on a Falcon 9 booster
flying for the 19th time. Per an FAA assessment, the disk-shaped
capsule is about 10 feet wide and weighs 4,600 pounds, and can bring
2,200 pounds of payload home, aimed at microgravity research and
in-space manufacturing. It deployed last, 2 hours 48 minutes after
liftoff, alongside Varda's W-Series 7 capsule.

 HPE SPACEBORNE COMPUTER-4 WILL BRING AI AND HPC TO THE MOON ON
ASTROLAB FLIP (4 MINUTE READ) [9]

 HPE's Spaceborne Computer-4 will be the first HPC benchmark on the
lunar surface, launching in late 2026 aboard Astrolab's FLIP rover
under NASA's CLPS initiative. It will manage AI workloads such as
vision inference and LiDAR data processing in the Moon's
high-radiation, atmosphere-free environment. NASA and Deloitte are
joining this mission to enhance AI capabilities, including Federated
Learning and predictive cyber-analytics.

MISCELLANEOUS

 WHY PHYSICAL AI IS SO HARD TO ENGINEER (13 MINUTE READ) [10]

 This article presents views from Synopsys, Keysight, Renesas,
Arteris, Cadence, and Mixel on how physical AI changes chip design.
Engineers must handle unpredictable environments, safety and
serviceability, and split designs between devices that can rely on the
cloud and those that must run fully locally, such as vehicles. Several
speakers frame it as a sensing and imaging problem as much as a
compute one.

 AI HARDWARE DESIGN IS THE EASY PART, THE LOSSY PHYSICAL INTERFACE IS
THE BOTTLENECK (15 MINUTE READ) [11]

 AI will soon be superhuman at hardware design, but the
digital-to-physical interface is lossy. Building real parts means
navigating CNC/CAM translation, tolerance stacking, vendors, and
scale, while measuring reality is sparse, slow, expensive, and
sometimes qualitative. Demand is expected to shift toward suppliers of
machined parts, harnesses, and components, plus better simulation and
process-knowledge data.
