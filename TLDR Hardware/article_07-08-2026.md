HEADLINES AND LAUNCHES

 AMD TO BUY STARTUP TAALAS FOR NEW AI CHIPS (3 MINUTE READ) [4]

 AMD announced a definitive agreement to acquire Toronto-based chip
startup Taalas to expand its specialized AI inference portfolio.
Taalas develops custom silicon architectures that hard-wire individual
AI model weights directly into semiconductor metal layers during
manufacturing. AMD plans to integrate Taalas' low-cost,
ultra-efficient inference technology directly into its broader
accelerator roadmap alongside Instinct GPUs.

 ANTHROPIC IS BUILDING AN IN-HOUSE CHIP TEAM FOR CLAUDE (3 MINUTE
READ) [5]

 Anthropic has officially confirmed the assembly of an in-house chip
design team to engineer custom AI processors tailored for its Claude
platform. By co-designing hardware specifically alongside software
models, the AI startup aims to slash inference costs, boost execution
speeds, and optimize scaling for enterprise workloads.

ENGINEERING AND APPLICATIONS

 BLUE ORIGIN TRACES MAY'S NEW GLENN EXPLOSION TO A FAULTY BE-4 ENGINE
VALVE (3 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Blue Origin CEO Dave Limp said the May explosion that destroyed a New
Glenn rocket during a prelaunch engine test originated at the main
oxygen valve on one of its BE-4 engines, confirmed through hardware
recovery and inspection. The fix is straightforward: small
modifications to the valve that can be quickly retrofitted onto
existing engines, with the company aiming to return New Glenn to
flight later this year. The BE-4 also powers United Launch Alliance's
Vulcan rocket, so ULA has been collaborating with Blue Origin on the
investigation. The finding gives both companies a clear, containable
root cause rather than an open-ended engine redesign.

 PCIE GEN 6 STORAGE ARCHITECTURE SHOWCASED (3 MINUTES READ) [7]

 Microchip Technology and Micron showcased a high-performance PCIe Gen
6 storage system demonstration at the Future of Memory and Storage
conference. Combining Microchip's 3nm Switchtec PCIe 6.0 fanout
switches with Micron 9650 NVMe SSDs, the architecture achieves
transfer speeds of 64 GT/s per lane—doubling available bandwidth
over PCIe 5.0.

RESEARCH AND PROTOTYPES

 D-WAVE'S DUAL-RAIL QUBIT GATE HITS 99.9% FIDELITY, CUTTING
ERROR-CORRECTION OVERHEAD (4 MINUTE READ) [8]

 D-Wave published research in Nature demonstrating a new two-qubit
entangling gate for its superconducting "dual-rail" qubit
architecture, which is designed so the most common quantum errors are
also the easiest kind to detect and fix. The gate hit roughly 99.9%
fidelity with fast ~500 nanosecond operation times, while preserving
native hardware-level error detection, meaning errors get caught
directly by the qubit design rather than requiring extra correction
circuitry bolted on afterward.

 HOW TO SCALE AI ARITHMETIC EFFICIENTLY (4 MINUTE READ) [9]

 This technical analysis explores how arithmetic specialization is
essential for scaling artificial intelligence compute performance. As
traditional transistor scaling slows down, hardware designers are
replacing general-purpose IEEE floating-point units with specialized
arithmetic engines tailored to low-precision representations like FP8,
FP4, and Microscaling (MX) formats. By optimizing number format
combinations and operation depth directly within silicon arithmetic
units, modern AI accelerators drastically reduce energy consumption
per operation while maximizing throughput without swelling total die
area or power budgets.

MISCELLANEOUS

 NVIDIA AND GOOGLE ARE CROWDING TSMC'S N3 NODE, CREATING A RARE
OPENING FOR INTEL (5 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Nvidia's next-gen Rubin GPUs are moving from 4nm to TSMC's 3nm node
just as Google's TPUs, which are already on N3, plan to stay there,
while new AI CPUs from Nvidia, Amazon, Microsoft, and Arm are
converging on the same process simultaneously. TSMC has already hit
its planned N3 capacity and is still adding more, warning the node
could stay "very tight" for years, with three of its four Arizona fabs
reportedly already fully booked despite only one being operational,
driving reported price hikes of up to 25% for some customers. That
squeeze creates a real opening for Intel, whose Fab 52 in Arizona just
entered full production at 40,000 18A wafer starts per month, more
capacity than TSMC's original Fab 21 campus.

 BREAKING THE MEMORY WALL WITH CXL (4 MINUTE READ) [11]

 The Diligence Stack analyzes how Compute Express Link (CXL)
technology breaks the persistent memory wall in high-density AI
infrastructure. By utilizing PCIe physical interfaces to provide
memory-semantic access, CXL enables memory expansion and multi-node
pooling directly across server clusters. This architecture offloads
Key-Value caches and massive vector databases from constrained GPU
memory without severe latency penalties. Consequently, data centers
can eliminate stranded DRAM resources, drastically reducing hardware
costs, and scale memory capacity independently from fixed CPU channel
limits.
