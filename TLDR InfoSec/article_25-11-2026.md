ATTACKS & VULNERABILITIES

 HACKERS EXPLOIT CRITICAL ASP.NET ANTI-CSRF LIBRARY FLAW ALLOWING
UNAUTHENTICATED RCE (3 MINUTE READ) [4]

 Attackers are exploiting a critical insecure deserialization flaw in
Idunno.AntiCsrf, an ASP.NET WebForms library abandoned since 2008,
where one unauthenticated POST carrying a Base64-encoded
ObjectStateFormatter gadget chain achieves RCE as w3wp.exe
(CVE-2026-131914, CVSS 9.3). Mandiant found the bug and confirmed
in-the-wild exploitation, but no patch is coming for versions 0.9.1.0
and 0.9.1 Beta. Defenders should search Web.config files and bin/
directories for AntiCsrfModule references, replace the library with
System.Web.Helpers.AntiForgery or ASP.NET Core IAntiforgery, and
rotate machineKey and other secrets if IIS logs show unusual .aspx
POSTs returning 302 or 500.

 1.5 MILLION IMPACTED BY DATA BREACH AT SOUTH KOREAN ELECTRIC COMPANY
KEPCO (1 MINUTE READ) [5]

 KEPCO says an external attacker exfiltrated data from its KEPCO ON
customer portal and supplier relationship management platform. Records
for 650,487 customers include names, contact details, ID numbers, bank
accounts, card details, and billing information. Another 869,761 SRM
records expose supplier and employee contact and employment data.
KEPCO suspended affected services and blocked external access.

 TURNITIN DISCLOSES CYBERATTACK AFFECTING CUSTOMER-FACING SYSTEMS (2
MINUTE READ) [6]

 Turnitin, an education software provider used by more than 16,000
institutions and 71 million students, detected unauthorized activity
on some customer-facing systems and has engaged outside responders,
law enforcement, and regulators. The company hasn't disclosed how
attackers gained access, when the activity occurred, or whether
student or institutional data was taken. It has said only that the
affected systems contain no government IDs or financial data, and no
ransomware group has claimed responsibility. Until Turnitin discloses
the scope, institutions should watch for phishing attempts using
Turnitin as a lure and review their SSO and LMS integration logs for
unusual access.

STRATEGIES & TACTICS

 JUDGE THE CYBER APOCALYPSE BY THE DATA, NOT THE HEADLINES (15 MINUTE
READ) [7]

 CSET's Andrew Lohn finds that while the technical indicators favor
attackers, with monthly CVEs tripling since Claude Mythos launched,
slower patching, and exploitation now behind 31% of breaches, data on
real-world impact looks steady or better, since significant incidents,
healthcare breach victims, breach costs, and ransomware payouts are
all flat or down. He explains that fear of attribution and
retaliation, not technical capability, may have always limited
nation-states and criminals. He recommends tougher enforcement,
investment in attribution and resilience, and hardening critical
systems for a crisis where those limits fall away, but he concedes
that some datasets lag and the trend could still turn.

 AI AGENTS ARE CREATING A SHARED RESPONSIBILITY GAP (3 MINUTE READ)
[8]

 Shared responsibility models have been a staple framework for SaaS
and cloud services, delineating which aspects a provider is
responsible for and which a user is responsible for. Harnesses and the
MCP spec both put the responsibility and audit logging for sensitive
actions on the user. Agent identities also do not provide a silver
bullet. For example, Microsoft's documentation for Entra agent
identities also puts responsibility for sensitive actions back on the
user. Organizations must accept that responsibility cannot be
delegated and that agents require clear owners, least privilege, and
consistent logging.

 BRAKE CHECK: HIJACKING TRAILER BRAKES OVER BLUETOOTH (39 MINUTE READ)
[9]

 Researchers found that Elecbrakes EB2, VM1, and ELBC 2000 trailer
brake controllers accepted unauthenticated Bluetooth commands. A
nearby phone or laptop could write four bytes to activate, modulate,
or release trailer brakes without pairing, a PIN, or authorization.
The force byte controlled braking strength, and nearly every non-zero
opcode activated the brakes. Commands lacked replay protection, and a
persistent register retained the last command. The firmware-update
channel used Just Works pairing, CRC32 integrity checks, and an
app-distributed decryption key. Elecbrakes fixed EB2 and VM1 on July
6. ELBC 2000 remains unpatched. CISA assigned CVE-2026-96805,
CVE-2026-125273, and CVE-2026-124281.

LAUNCHES & TOOLS

 OPENAI AND CISCO INTRODUCE SHIRUDO (4 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Shirudo is an open-source eBPF runtime from OpenAI and Cisco that
deploys temporary "shields" to block a vulnerable code path on a
running Linux system without a restart or a patch. In a test, a shield
generated by Codex added an input check to Java's Base64 decoder and
stopped a Java4Shell exploit against a live Elasticsearch process.
Shields are cryptographically signed, checked by the eBPF verifier,
and recorded through TPM attestation, which makes Shirudo a promising
stopgap for teams with long patch cycles, though the case for
AI-generated shields so far rests on a single controlled demo.

 WHAT 5,400 MALICIOUS NPM PACKAGES REVEAL ABOUT SOFTWARE SUPPLY CHAIN
RISK IN 2026 (11 MINUTE READ) [13]

 A review of about 5,400 malicious npm packages from the past year
found that compromised legitimate packages made up only 7% of the set
but more than 99% of downloads, while the attacker-created packages
that make up most of the malware were rarely installed at all. After
npm revoked long-lived tokens, attackers moved to compromising CI
pipelines and publishing through OIDC trusted publishing, and 38% of
packages hid their C2 traffic in legitimate services such as
Cloudflare Workers and Discord webhooks. The registry took a median of
7 days to remove malicious packages, while attackers often deleted
their own within hours.

 HERE'S WHY DATA BREACH DISCLOSURE IS SO HARD: LESSONS FROM HUNDREDS
OF EMAILS WITH HACKED COMPANIES (13 MINUTE READ) [14]

 Troy Hunt reviewed 8.5 years of breach-notification emails sent to 73
organizations. Common responses included no security contact, denial
before reviewing evidence, open-ended investigations, legal control of
wording, and arguments that disclosure itself causes harm. In one
case, leaked records covered 16 user-system tables, including
identity-verification and tax documents. Effective organizations
replied quickly, investigated the evidence, notified customers, and
published updates.
