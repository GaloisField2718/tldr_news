 DEUTSCHE BANK TO LAUNCH INSTITUTIONAL CRYPTO CUSTODY (2 MINUTE READ)
[4]

 Deutsche Bank plans to launch an institutional-grade crypto custody
service pending regulatory approval, with a rollout expected later
this year. The service will let institutional and corporate clients
hold and transfer bitcoin, ether, and select stablecoins, with a
roadmap extending into tokenized financial instruments.

 TWO ROBINHOOD EMPLOYEES CHARGED WITH INSIDER TRADING ON HYPERLIQUID
(2 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Federal prosecutors charged two Robinhood employees with fraud for
alleged insider trading conducted on Hyperliquid, the decentralized
perpetuals exchange. The employees were allegedly trading perpetual
futures on tokens they knew were going to be listed. The activity
happened between 2025 and 2026 and netted the pair approximately
$50,000 each.

 CIRCLE LAUNCHES ARC MAINNET WITH BLACKROCK AND VISA AMONG VALIDATORS
(5 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Circle opened Arc's public mainnet Wednesday, an EVM-compatible Layer
1 with sub-second finality where gas is paid in USDC, launching with
Aave V4, Morpho, and Uniswap among more than 100 applications live
from day one. Founding validators including BlackRock, DTCC, ICE,
Mastercard, and Visa will join in phases, and Circle and DTCC plan to
tokenize DTC-custodied assets on Arc starting in the second half of
2027. Circle also completed the genesis mint of 10 billion ARC tokens
this week as a technical milestone, though a public token launch
remains undecided as the network plans a move from proof of authority
to proof of stake in 2027. Arc ships with Arc Studio, an AI coding
agent for smart contracts, and Arc App Kits, an SDK for payments,
swaps, onramps, and yield.

 X LAUNCHES US CASHTAG PROGRAM WITH BROKERAGE PARTNERS (3 MINUTE READ)
[7]

 X launched its Cashtag Partner Program in the US, converting ticker
symbols for stocks, ETFs, and cryptocurrencies into trade entry points
within the platform's timeline. Tapping a supported Cashtag surfaces a
price chart and related posts, then a "Trade" button routes users to
partner brokerages including Interactive Brokers, Moomoo, Gemini,
Kraken, and Coinbase. Execution occurs at each brokerage's own app or
website rather than within X itself, making this a referral and
discovery funnel rather than an in-app trading product. For Gemini,
Kraken, and Coinbase, the integration embeds a
social-discovery-to-onboarding pipeline directly inside X's financial
conversation layer.

 FALCON CARD REVIEW: AN EARLY SPEND PRODUCT, NOT YET A FULL NEOBANK (4
MINUTE READ) [9]

 Falcon Finance's virtual Visa debit card, live since July 20 for
KYC-verified users in 90-plus jurisdictions, lets holders spend USDf
minted against collateral including stablecoins, major crypto,
tokenized gold, and tokenized equities like Ondo's COINon and xStocks'
TSLAX without selling the underlying position. The card charges a $0
annual fee and no extra Falcon markup beyond Visa FX charges, and
counts spend toward Falcon Miles Season 2, but earns 0% on card
balance since yield stays in separately staked sUSDf. It's issued by a
third-party EMI rather than Falcon directly, remains virtual-only with
no published cashback or ATM fee schedule, isn't a bank deposit or
deposit-insured, and blocks Russia, Belarus, China, Singapore, and the
US.

 UNISWAP V4 HOOKS WERE A MISTAKE (5 MINUTE READ) [10]

 0x Protocol's review of 84,163 Uniswap v4 hooks across six chains
found only 19.4% safe, with 54.2% classified as malicious and 26.4%
probable malicious, a dataset drawn from routing ~70% of the firm's
$42.67 billion in annual volume through Uniswap liquidity. Malicious
hooks exploit the gap between quote-time and settlement-time
execution, spoofing aggregators and wallets to display one price while
delivering up to 50% less at fill. A single hook on Base extracted
$143,037 from users across 6,516 trades by charging an 18% median fee
on 60.6% of fills. The permissionless design that grants builders
custom AMM logic also removes routers' ability to trust quoted prices
without independent execution verification.

 L1 GAS IMPACT: SIX MONTHS OF MEASURED ETHEREUM L1 GAS (4 MINUTE READ)
[11]

 Six months of measured Ethereum L1 execution gas data show plain ETH
transfers consuming 13,815 million gas per day, roughly 1.6x more than
all Uniswap L1 activity, which ranked second at 8,491 million gas per
day. Among rollups posting to L1, Robinhood consumed 665 million gas
per day, approximately 2.3x more than Base (271M) and 3.1x more than
Arbitrum One (214M), making it the top rollup gas consumer by a wide
margin. Order book DEX Lighter's primary instance ranked above both
major rollups at 286 million gas per day across 41,184 daily
transactions, while smaller rollups including World Chain, Unichain,
and Ink each fell below 22 million gas per day. All figures are lower
bounds derived from verified direct activity measured through
mid-September 2026.

 ALL ABOUT X402 (8 MINUTE READ) [12]

 x402 is an open payment specification, originated at Coinbase, that
embeds pricing, authorization, and settlement into API workflows,
allowing machines and AI agents to pay for calls using stablecoins
without OAuth flows or account setup. As delegated purchasing
authority expands from narrow API credits to broader agentic
decisions, the protocol stands to reshape how APIs, data, and compute
are priced and traded between software systems. Cloudflare, AWS,
Google, and Fastly are each integrating agent payment capabilities
into their existing infrastructure businesses, competing on different
layers of the value capture stack. Outstanding questions around data
precision, service quality guarantees, trust mechanisms, and payment
rail neutrality will determine whether x402 matures into a unified
market or fragments across competing implementations.
