 DOJ SEEKS $1.17 BILLION IN USDC TIED TO BINANCE HACK (2 MINUTE READ)
[4]

 The DOJ filed a civil forfeiture complaint for about $1.17B in USDC
across 36 addresses on 18 blockchains, attributing the theft on
October 29 to North Korea's Lazarus Group/APT38, and separately asked
a judge to keep the assets restrained since the November 12 order may
lapse. Circle blocked the USDC but can't move the original tokens, so
it agreed to issue equivalent new USDC to the US if forfeiture is
granted. The DOJ says over $2.9B in crypto has been frozen, seized, or
returned since the theft. Binance puts losses at about $6.34B, says it
replenished them, and also won a $6.5B worldwide freezing order in Abu
Dhabi.

 CFTC STAFF SAY BTC AND ETH CAN COUNT TOWARD DERIVATIVES FIRMS' OPEX
(3 MINUTE READ) [5]

 A staff advisory says swap execution facilities, clearing
organizations, and designated contract markets may count BTC, ETH, and
payment stablecoins, subject to a haircut, toward the requirement to
hold a year of operating expenses. Crypto doesn't qualify as cash or
very liquid securities, so it can't count toward the separate
liquidity requirement, and the advisory isn't enforceable. The CFTC
also published a final rule creating a reporting framework for certain
event contracts, replacing no-action letters.

 PRIVY ADDS VERIFIABLE ENCLAVE ATTESTATIONS FOR WALLET OPERATIONS (2
MINUTE READ) [6]

 Privy added verifiable enclave attestations to its wallet
infrastructure, giving developers cryptographic proof that sensitive
wallet operations executed inside AWS Nitro enclaves. The feature
shifts the security model from operator trust to cryptographic
verification, letting any external party confirm that key operations
ran in an isolated, hardened compute environment. This closes a gap
common in MPC and embedded wallet providers, where users had to accept
security claims without cryptographic evidence.

 VISA LAUNCHES CLI AGENT WALLET ON TEMPO AND MPP (3 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Visa expanded its Trusted Agent Protocol (TAP) to agent wallets and
task marketplaces, and launched Visa CLI, an experimental agent wallet
for OUSD built on Tempo and the Machine Payments Protocol (MPP),
available to US users as a connector to major agent platforms. TAP
lets users attach a Visa card to their agent wallet, register that
agent in Visa's Agent Directory, and have the verified agent complete
paid tasks spanning video creation, music, gaming, surveys, and market
research, with earnings settled in OUSD. The expansion follows TAP's
October 2025 debut and existing pilots with Cloudflare and Akamai for
e-commerce authentication, plus a B2B pilot with Bill.com and
Billtrust for agent-verified invoice payments. Visa is positioning its
5 billion existing payment credentials as the accountability layer for
agent transactions and is soliciting integration partners among agent
wallet builders, personal assistants, and marketplace operators.

 TASK MARKETS: THE FINAL FORM OF AGENTIC COMMERCE (15 MINUTE READ) [9]


 Agentic commerce is converging on the task, a verified unit of
completed work. This article outlines three layers: single agent with
skills and MCP, pay-per-call, and task markets for complex work

 WHAT'S A BLOCK, AND HOW BIG CAN IT GET? (7 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Under Glamsterdam's 60M gas limit, skipped EIP-7702 authorizations,
not calldata, represent the worst-case block-size attack vector,
packing 17.0 bytes per 1k gas and producing 1.81 MiB EL blocks, about
2x what calldata can achieve. This creates a structural conflict with
FOCIL (EIP-7805): a transaction satisfying gas constraints may breach
the 8 MiB EL byte cap when appended, leaving builders unable to honor
attesters' inclusion expectations despite valid gas usage. The
proposed re-encoding patch fails in four edge cases, so the author
argues for pricing every payload byte in gas via EIP-8131 and EIP-8279
plus deposit-request metering, with a capped gas limit (256M without
those EIPs, 500M with them). This bounds worst-case envelope size and
gives the CL a deterministic maximum: max_envelope_size = C +
ceil(1.25 x gas_limit / 64).

 HOW CAN CRYPTO NEOBANKS WIN? (7 MINUTE READ) [11]

 Weekly crypto card spend exceeds $300M, up almost 200% year over
year, but the top 5 of the 20 largest programs hold 86% of the last 30
days' spend, with RedotPay alone over 46%. Users spend only $0.65 to
$0.75 per $1 topped up, treating apps as savings, and EtherFi and KAST
offer 7 to 9% APY on stablecoin deposits. After cashback, most
programs earn under 1% of spend while paying 2 to 3%, and card
infrastructure from providers like Rain is commoditized.
Differentiation will come from local rails, multi-currency settlement,
and owning the yield source, with EtherFi's Aave V4 loans over $47M
and Phantom earning over $20M in perps builder fees.

 XMR1 COLLAPSES 99% AFTER DEPLOYER KEY STOLEN IN WEB HOST BREACH (3
MINUTE READ) [12]

 XMR1, Wagyu's wrapped Monero token on Hyperliquid, fell from about
$500 to $1 in 30 minutes after an attacker extracted 5.2 million
tokens from the deployer wallet and sold 364,000 of them to 388 buyers
for $13.5 million, converting the proceeds to about 7,350 ETH. Wagyu
founder PerpetualCow attributed the breach to Icelandic host
1984.hosting, claiming the deployer private key persisted on a deleted
server whose logs the host retained, with 1984 confirming a broad
cyberattack on its infrastructure without naming Wagyu or disclosing
whether customer data was taken. The attacker seeded the exploit
wallet via Tornado Cash minutes before the drain, and the same
deployer account still controls ZEC1, a wrapped Zcash token carrying
identical centralization risk. PerpetualCow pledged direct refunds to
affected holders with no relaunch timeline given. The incident is
Wagyu's second reported breach in two months and follows the service's
appearance in ZachXBT's tracing of a $106 million theft routed through
the platform.
