 NEW YORK FEDERAL JUDGE DENIES NOVIG'S INJUNCTION BID (2 MINUTE READ)
[4]

 Judge Colleen McMahon denied the preliminary injunction Novig sought
against New York, finding that its sports event contracts are not
swaps under the Commodity Exchange Act because they don't depend on
events with a financial or economic consequence, and “bear more than
a passing resemblance to sports betting.” That lets the state
require Novig to get a gambling license or stop listing sports
contracts. Novig's operator, Ludlow Exchange, got CFTC contract market
designation in June, began serving New York customers in August, and
sued Attorney General Letitia James on August 5, arguing federal
preemption. The ruling follows a similar denial for Kalshi earlier
this year, and lawyer Daniel Wallach says all 10 Second Circuit
federal rulings in sports prediction-market cases have favored the
states, with state regulators possibly moving to enforcement in state
court.

 ALTA: AIRWALLEX CO-FOUNDER TO BUILD ONCHAIN FINANCE INFRASTRUCTURE (3
MINUTE READ) [5]

 Alta, operated by Nexus Networks, raised to build onchain finance
infrastructure spanning cross-border payments, embedded finance,
treasury management, and compliance controls. Founder Loong Wang
frames the addressable problem as $4T in trapped cash, $1.4T in
counterparty risk, and $135B in operational waste in global corporate
finance. The treasury product combines FX conversion at settlement
with onchain yield through tokenized money market funds, while the
compliance layer applies policy controls at the transaction level
across all product areas. A fourth vertical targets agentic commerce,
building payment rails for AI agents that transact autonomously.

 VISA ADDS MONAD TO STABLECOIN SETTLEMENT NETWORK (3 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Visa added Monad as a stablecoin settlement option for participating
issuers and acquirers, bringing its blockchain settlement network to
10 chains that also include Ethereum, Solana, Base, Polygon,
Avalanche, Stellar, Canton, Tempo, and Circle's Arc. The program
reached a $20 billion annualized settlement run rate as of September,
up 15x year over year, with 225 stablecoin-linked card programs live
globally. Monad is building an institutional privacy feature, expected
in the coming months, that keeps transaction details confidential from
public view while remaining auditable by regulators and sharing
liquidity with the public chain. The network's ability to settle on
weekends and holidays distinguishes it from traditional card rails,
which operate on a five-business-day banking schedule.

 BUILDING A TOKENIZED SECURITIES VENUE ON UNISWAP (5 MINUTE READ) [7]

 The SEC's September 2026 Innovation Exemption established Tokenized
Securities Venues (TSVs), a new venue category permitting AMMs on
public blockchains to trade tokenized NMS stocks without registering
as exchanges or ATSs, with compliant liquidity providers exempt from
dealer registration. Uniswap has processed over $20 billion in stock
token volume to date, with Robinhood Chain generating $900 million
across 130,000 wallets in three months through 40+ SPY-paired pools.
TSV compliance requires KYL and KYC identity gating, per-pool
disclosures, and price oracles tied to real-world exchanges, all
implementable via Uniswap v4 hooks without modifying the core AMM. The
exemption will run for five years and targets a $79 trillion US
equities market, where AMM settlement is atomic and final compared to
the T+1 cycle in traditional clearinghouses.

 IS HYPERLIQUID LOSING ITS LONG-TAIL LIQUIDITY MOAT? (6 MINUTE READ)
[8]

 Analysis of orderbook data from November 2025 to November 2026 across
38 comparable markets shows Hyperliquid's long-tail dollar depth fell
82%, versus 61% on Binance and 78% on OKX. After controlling for token
price declines, the HL-to-Binance depth ratio dropped 57.5% at 5 bps,
with $10k execution costs running 5.21 bps on HL versus 2.95 bps on
Binance. Wintermute's monopolization of read priority fees and L1
congestion at 25-40k TPS have disadvantaged smaller market makers,
pushing them to widen spreads and reduce depth in response to adverse
selection risk. The deterioration threatens Hyperliquid's founding
differentiation: deep liquidity in long-tail assets was the moat
separating it from centralized venues.

 DEFI MOATS IN AN AI WORLD (5 MINUTE READ) [9]

 AI tooling has collapsed the cost of launching DeFi products from
six-month, $3M builds to weekend projects, but Multicoin Capital's
Spencer Applebaum argues this commoditization strengthens incumbent
protocol moats rather than eroding them. Financial moats, including
liquidity depth, customer trust, and proprietary risk underwriting,
require years to accumulate and sit beyond what AI can replicate from
scratch. Multicoin's updated DeFi value-capture framework adds AI
agents as the next class of sophisticated users, gravitating toward
protocols with the deepest liquidity and most reliable execution at
scale rather than those competing on software features or UI. This
positions protocols like Uniswap, Aave, and Hyperliquid as primary
beneficiaries as DEX market share against CEXs hits all-time highs and
institutional adoption of tokenized assets accelerates.

 STOCK TOKENS ARE WHERE STABLECOINS WERE IN 2023 (10 MINUTE READ) [10]


 DTCC's new tokenization service is the biggest change in markets
since paper certificates went away in the 1970s, yet will be mostly
unused for a while. The market is tiny (about 0.004% of stocks and
ETFs, with $4.6B of tokenized stocks, nearly all wrapped tokens backed
by shares at one broker, Alpaca) and is similar to stablecoins in
2023, about 18 months before they took off. There are currently four
models: wrapped (xStocks, no shareholder rights), pooled (OKXICE,
where 56 of 62 targeted companies opted out), mirrored (DTCC, with 34
securities on Canton and Besu and one Canton transaction so far), and
native (Superstate and Figure, which need SEC rule changes). A
Warren-led Senate Banking Committee and faster settlement hurting
netting are potential headwinds. Banks that sit out tokenization risk
buying it later from Robinhood or BlackRock.

 BUILDING NETWORKS FOR AGENTS (4 MINUTE READ) [11]

 The intents model used by protocols like UniswapX, where users sign
outcome-specified orders and providers compete to fill them, offers a
template for agent-to-agent commerce networks that handle complex
multi-party preference matching across everyday goods and services.
Exchanges that currently require excessive coordination overhead
become tractable when each party's agent can programmatically bid and
accept offers within pre-authorized limits. Founders building these
networks face four concrete design problems: enabling agent discovery
of relevant suppliers, verifying supplier performance, pricing
preferences that are fuzzy and multidimensional, and preventing fake
demand or supply on either side. Crypto and stablecoins serve as the
natural settlement layer for machine-to-machine transactions in these
markets.
