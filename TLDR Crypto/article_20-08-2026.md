MARKETS & BUSINESS

 VISA SEARCHES FOR NEW STABLECOIN SETTLEMENT PARTNER (3 MINUTE READ)
[6]

 Mastercard's acquisition of BVNK, a stablecoin settlement
infrastructure provider holding active licenses in the US, Canada, UK,
and Singapore, forces Visa to find a new settlement partner, as
Mastercard would not continue supplying infrastructure to a direct
competitor. Visa must source a replacement with equivalent
four-jurisdiction regulatory coverage, a requirement that limits the
candidate pool to a small number of licensed providers. The deal
signals that traditional payment networks are treating regulated
stablecoin settlement rails as competitive moats, with Mastercard's
move simultaneously advancing its own stablecoin capabilities while
cutting off Visa's access.

 TEMPO: WHERE SPECIALIZED BLOCKCHAINS ARE HEADED (8 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Tempo is a payments-focused L1 incubated by Stripe and Paradigm,
assembled from Commonware's independently composable primitives
(consensus, data dissemination, and cryptography) rather than a fixed
framework like Cosmos SDK or OP Stack, with Threshold Simplex BFT
delivering 550ms deterministic finality and compact 240-byte
certificates verifiable without a full node. A payment lane enforces a
consensus-level gas ceiling on general traffic, guaranteeing payment
transactions dedicated blockspace regardless of NFT or DeFi
congestion, while a Fee AMM accepts any qualifying stablecoin and caps
standard transfer fees near $0.0006. The TIP-403 policy registry lets
stablecoin issuers propagate compliance rule updates across all tokens
using a given policy in a single operation, removing the per-contract
drift that complicates regulated asset management at scale. The
validator set is permissioned (Stripe, Visa, MoneyGram, and Zodia
Custody) with regulatory accountability replacing slashing, and Tempo
trades Ethereum security inheritance and IBC-style interoperability
for full institutional control over the execution environment.

 EVERYONE IS WRONG ABOUT TODAY'S SEC ANNOUNCEMENT (6 MINUTE READ) [11]


 The SEC proposed "Regulation Crypto Assets," its first crypto-native
rulebook, creating three legal registration paths for token launches
that include promises of future work, the ICO model that has operated
as an unregistered investment contract in the US since 2017. The three
paths are a startup exemption (up to $5M over four years with
plain-language disclosure), a fundraising exemption (up to $75M per 12
months with financial statements and ongoing reporting), and an exit
certification that removes investment contract status once promised
development is complete. This closes the final gap in the March
SEC/CFTC five-bucket token taxonomy, making US-based L1, DeFi, and
DePIN launches with explicit build commitments legally viable, though
tokenized equity and profit-sharing tokens remain bucket 5 and await a
separate framework. The proposal enters a 60-day comment period with
final adoption expected in 2027.

 COMPOUND FOUNDATION HIRES COINBASE AND ANCHORAGE ALUMNI (3 MINUTE
READ) [12]

 Compound Foundation named four executives to lead an institutional
expansion: Executive Director Aaron Schnarch (former Anchorage Digital
COO and Coinbase Custody CEO, with custody on 9 of 11 spot bitcoin
ETFs), COO Christopher Donovan (ex-NEAR Foundation), CPO Steven Liu
(ex-Maple Finance and Anchorage Digital), and CTO Leo Eikelman
(ex-Coinbase Institutional). The DAO approved a $52M two-year budget
via Proposal 582 in May with 1.88 million COMP in favor and zero
against, split $28M for operations and $24M for institutional growth,
with $38M held in a milestone-gated Program Reserve and $14M released
to the Foundation multisig. The initiative targets banks and asset
managers with permissioned vaults featuring KYC/AML controls,
tokenized equities as collateral, and portfolio-aware risk management.


 NEYNAR SEEKS BUYER FOR FARCASTER SEVEN MONTHS AFTER ACQUISITION (3
MINUTE READ) [13]

 Neynar is seeking a new operator for Farcaster, Clanker, and its
developer platform seven months after acquiring the assets from Merkle
Manufactory in January. Protocol fees collapsed from $35.43 million in
Q1 2026 to $376,740 through mid-August, with the trailing 24-hour
figure at $4,001, a 99% decline that has halted token buybacks and
dropped holder revenue from $3.98 million in Q1 to zero in Q3. Neynar
will return its remaining balance sheet and disband its team as it
fields buyers, with CLANKER trading at $12.29 against a $12.12 million
market cap. The collapse follows Farcaster's December 2025 pivot from
consumer social to wallet functionality, a strategic shift that failed
to arrest declining user activity after Merkle Manufactory transferred
the protocol, citing the need for new leadership.
