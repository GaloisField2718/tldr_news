 CIRCLE FACES UP TO 20% REVENUE RISK FROM AI AGENTS, MIZUHO SAYS (3
MINUTE READ) [4]

 Mizuho cut its Circle price target to $43 from $45 and kept
Underperform. That implies about 46% downside from Monday's $79.84
close, which was down 7% on the day. The analysts say AI personal
assistants are a “marked risk.” In their simulation, agents steer
USDC holders toward higher-yielding FDIC-insured options like SoFi
savings, and even crypto-only users toward other platforms. They
estimate USDC's market cap could shrink by $4B to $9B and reserve
revenue could fall 10% to 20% over the medium term. Only about 17% of
USDC sits on Circle's platform, which pays no yield, versus 30% on
Coinbase and 53% elsewhere. Under faster AI adoption, they project
2027 average USDC at $101B (from $108B), reserve revenue down 5% to
$4.1B, and adjusted EBITDA down 5% to $603M.

 HYPERLIQUID SAYS PAYWARD TO BE FIRST CFTC-REGULATED HIP-3 DEPLOYER (3
MINUTE READ) [5]

 Hyperliquid said it intends to make Payward the first CFTC-regulated
deployer on its HIP-3 markets. The upgrade would let deployers
restrict access through onchain allowlists, which could let regulated
operators run US perpetual futures on Hyperliquid's infrastructure.
Bitnomial, Payward's CFTC-regulated exchange and clearinghouse, would
be the deployer, and NinjaTrader Clearing would carry client accounts
if approved. The functionality is live on testnet, and the deployment
still needs regulatory approval. It is unclear how permissioning would
work or whether it would include built-in KYC. Payward first announced
the plan on September 16.

 CZ CONFIRMS BNB CHAIN NEW HIGH-SPEED PARALLEL L1 (2 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Binance founder CZ has confirmed BNB Chain is building a new
EVM-compatible parallel Layer 1 that will run alongside BNB Smart
Chain, connected through a native bridge with BNB serving as the gas,
staking, and governance token. The chain targets high-frequency
trading and real-time payments with 200-millisecond block times and
20-millisecond pre-confirmations. Planned features include native
account abstraction and onchain privacy pools. An internal
multi-validator devnet is already live, with public testnet targeted
for late 2026 and mainnet for early 2027.

 DIGITAL CASH ON SPARK: ZK-BASED PRIVACY FOR BITCOIN PAYMENTS (5
MINUTE READ) [7]

 Spark, a Bitcoin offchain payments protocol from Lightspark,
Flashnet, and Breez, exposes all user transactions to operators by
default, a privacy gap shared by Ark, Bark, and Wavelength. Zark
addresses this by layering a ZK co-signing protocol on top of Spark
leaves without modifying the base protocol, structuring user-to-user
transfer messages as ZKPs that prove payment validity without
revealing transaction details to operators, and using a 2-of-2
signature scheme pairing a Zark co-signer with Spark operators. The
main bottleneck is arbitrary-denomination payments through SSPs, where
each leaf swap must prove and verify independently, pushing settlement
times up to 10 minutes for complex combinations. The implementation is
a regtest proof-of-concept, with aggregate ZK proving across batches
identified as the path to practical settlement speeds.

 THE TOKENIZED CARRY TRADE (6 MINUTE READ) [8]

 The carry trade has existed across four onchain generations: 2020
incentive-subsidized yield farming, liquid staking carry via stETH on
Aave at 90-95% LTV, Ethena-style funding-rate carry, and tokenized
credit collateral backed by $14.4B in tokenized Treasuries, $8.1B in
tokenized credit, and $4.3B in active strategies. The carry math at
stage four: a 9% yielding asset at 75% LTV and 5% borrow cost produces
17% ROE at 3x leverage, but carry turns negative near 10% borrow
rates. The piece proposes a seven-factor collateral screen covering
yield spread, yield stability, price stability, price quality, issuer
redemption speed, secondary liquidity, and capacity, applied across
asset-backed lending, digital credit, reinsurance, and active
strategies.

 THE BIGGEST OPPORTUNITY IN CRYPTO: ONCHAIN OPTIONS (7 MINUTE READ)
[9]

 Onchain options represent 6.9% of Deribit's volume, but
infrastructure constraints around regulation, market-making, and RFQ
systems are now resolved, leaving distribution of retail taker flow as
the primary barrier to growth. Retail has been the growth driver in
each major options market: US contracts tripled from 4.19B to 15.2B
between 2017 and 2025 after Robinhood launched commission-free
options, 0DTE options grew from 5% to over 60% of S&P 500 index volume
in a decade, and India's NSE now processes 34.7B equity index
contracts per year, 89% of global volume. The Korea case study
illustrates the offshore spillover opportunity: after regulations cut
domestic retail participation by 85%, Korean retail shifted offshore
and now trades 3.6x more there than at home, positioning onchain
venues to capture demand displaced from over-regulated local markets.

 KEYSTONE HARDWARE WALLET DISCLOSES BREACH, DESTROYS 18,835 DEVICES (4
MINUTE READ) [10]

 Keystone disclosed a breach in which an attacker pivoted from a
compromised third-party monitoring tool to access 25,964 buyer
records, 12,873 incomplete checkout records, and the WebAuth device
authentication key burned into hardware during manufacturing. That key
cannot be erased from existing units and is the mechanism for
verifying device genuineness, meaning an attacker now has the
capability to produce counterfeit Keystones that pass authentication
checks. Keystone is destroying all 18,835 devices in current
inventory, suspending all unshipped orders, and halting production
under the compromised key until new-key devices are ready. User
assets, recovery phrases, and private keys were not affected. A
Shopify data-erasure tool failure caused customer records to be
retained beyond the company's 180-day policy, widening the exposure
window.

 FIREBLOCKS CUTS 7% OF WORKFORCE AFTER DENYING VISA ACQUISITION RUMORS
(2 MINUTE READ) [11]

 Fireblocks denied Visa acquisition rumors at a ~$2.5B valuation on
Thursday, then confirmed a 7% workforce reduction (60-70 jobs) the
following day. The rumored deal price represents a 69% markdown from
the company's $8B 2022 peak valuation, and this marks the company's
third round of layoffs in recent years despite having raised
approximately $1.04B in total capital. The sequence of events raises
questions about the state of institutional crypto infrastructure
valuations and whether Fireblocks is managing toward an exit at
materially lower terms than its last primary raise. On the same day,
American Express announced cutting roughly 5% of its workforce, with
its CFO citing AI-driven restructuring, adding to a broader pattern of
financial services firms shrinking headcount ahead of
automation-driven operating model changes.
