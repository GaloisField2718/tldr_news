 JPMORGAN EXPANDS ‘JPM COIN' DEPOSIT TOKEN TO ETHEREUM (2 MINUTE
READ) [4]

 JPMorgan's Kinexys brought its USD deposit token JPM Coin (JPMD) to
Ethereum for institutional clients. JPMD launched on Base in November
2025 and now supports peer-to-peer transfers across the EVM ecosystem,
including Base and Ethereum. It represents existing JPMorgan bank
deposits on a public chain. Kinexys says it has handled over $4T in
cumulative transactions, averaging $7B a day. The bank launched a
tokenized money-market fund on Ethereum in December and a second in
May.

 REVOLUT SECURES GIBRALTAR CRYPTO LICENCE TO SUPPORT INTERNATIONAL
EXPANSION PLANS (2 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Revolut got a Distributed Ledger Technology provider licence from the
Gibraltar Financial Services Commission for a new entity, Revolut
Global Digital Assets Limited. The entity will support its
international crypto operations, alongside its existing UK and EEA
(MiCA) entities. Revolut says it has over 80M retail customers and
over 16M crypto users. It did not say which markets the Gibraltar
entity will serve or when. Other recent moves are conditional OCC
approval for a US bank charter last month and the euro stablecoin EURR
rolling out in Denmark, Poland, and Portugal since August.

 WORLD LAUNCHES WORLD ID SUITE FOR BANKS (3 MINUTE READ) [6]

 World, co-founded by Sam Altman, launched a bank-focused World ID
bundle at Money20/20. It combines the World ID app, World ID for Zoom
(deepfake detection, now generally available after an April beta), and
World ID for Agents (AgentKit, which lets a person delegate Proofs to
an AI agent). World did not name a bank using it or give pricing. It
says over 18M people hold a Proof of Human.

 STRIPE LINK AGENT WALLET V0.27.5 (2 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Stripe released Link Agent Wallet v0.27.5 with two updates: expanded
purchase-type coverage across hotels, flights, subscriptions, and
recurring billing alongside standard ecommerce, and multi-currency
support for hundreds of Stripe-supported currencies paired with
geographic rollout to roughly six new markets beyond the US and Canada
this month. The wallet abstracts card types, tokens, and payment
protocols from agent code, letting agents submit spend requests
without handling payment-type differentiation. The updates extend
Link's reach from US-centric, single-purchase ecommerce to multi-leg
travel, recurring billing, and cross-border transactions across a
growing set of geographies.

 ETH IS ETHEREUM'S MOST IMPORTANT APPLICATION (6 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Gnosis is live with Ethereum's Fast Confirmation Rule (FCR), cutting
L1-to-Gnosis deposit times from ~14 minutes to 12-24 seconds, with
most consensus clients now shipping FCR behind an opt-in flag. AI
agents completed formal verification of the FCR in Lean in 4-5 days, a
task that used to take months, and the process caught a subtle
fork-choice bug in the spec itself. A Cambridge workshop of 100+
researchers from 70 organizations advanced post-quantum cryptography
and fast finality work, with Barnabé presenting a path to finality in
tens of seconds in the I* fork after Hegotá, down from the current
~15 minutes. Quick Slots (EIP-8198) joined heze-devnet-2 alongside
FOCIL, and Glamsterdam arrives on the Hoodi testnet on October 26.

 THE $280M COST OF ONBOARDING RETAIL TO HYPERLIQUID (5 MINUTE READ)
[9]

 A study of nearly 400,000 wallets trading Hyperliquid perpetuals
through six consumer apps found cumulative losses of $280M all-time,
with $126M accruing since November 2025. Fees drove slightly more than
half those recent losses: builder fees stacked on Hyperliquid's base
rate push per-$100 order costs to 6.5-14.5 cents, and small accounts
turning over their balance many times compound those costs fast. Invo
users show the extreme: $12.4M deposited against $5.8B traded, and on
BTC positions, fee drag alone exceeded net gains from closed trades.
Retention is poor across all six apps: about half of each month's
active traders are first-timers, and only one in seven new traders
remains active three months later, meaning growth has come from
continuous new-user acquisition rather than building a durable base.

 SINGAPORE GULF BANK ENABLES NATIVE USDT/USDC CONVERSION (2 MINUTE
READ) [10]

 Singapore Gulf Bank, backed by Singapore's Whampoa Group, now lets
retail customers convert between USD and USDT or USDC natively within
its mobile banking app, eliminating the need to route funds through a
separate crypto exchange. USD, USDT, and USDC sit in the same account,
with conversions available around the clock Monday through Friday. SGB
represents a growing class of Asia-based banks building stablecoin
access directly into core banking infrastructure.

 STABLECOIN SUPPLY STALLS, BUT PAYMENTS JUMP 45% AS VISA, MASTERCARD
AND STRIPE CONNECT CRYPTO TO CASH (4 MINUTE READ) [11]

 Stablecoin payment volume hit $482 billion through September 2026, a
45% year-over-year gain, while total supply held flat at $305 billion,
pointing to rising velocity rather than new issuance. Card spending
linked to stablecoins reached $3.9 billion, roughly 10x the prior
year, with sub-$50 purchases driving 84% of transaction count growth.
Mastercard deployed its BVNK-built Global Money Platform combining
fiat accounts, stablecoin balances, and card spending with Nuvei and
Marqeta as first customers; Stripe is expanding stablecoin card
coverage from 30 to 100-plus countries via a partnership with Plasma;
and Visa's Inyo Global became the first commercial user of Visa
Direct's USDC prefunding service for cross-border settlement.
Stablecoin card spending still totals roughly 0.03% of Visa's $4
trillion quarterly payment volume, marking the distance between
infrastructure buildout and adoption scale.
