 KALSHI HALTS UTAH OPERATIONS WHILE AVOIDING IMMEDIATE BAN IN NEW YORK
(3 MINUTE READ) [4]

 Kalshi paused operations in Utah, where it had 177,000 users, after a
federal judge let the state enforce its gambling laws. It is waiting
on a ruling on the CFTC's request to block Utah from enforcing against
it. In New York, Justice Melissa Crane denied the Attorney General's
request for a temporary restraining order, saying the questions around
derivatives and preemption are complex. Kalshi had warned the order
could shut it down nationwide. The CFTC and DOJ have also sued Utah,
Arkansas, Louisiana, and New York over state enforcement against
prediction markets.

 CFTC STAFF ADVISORY PERMITS CRYPTO ASSETS TO COUNT TOWARD FINANCIAL
RESOURCES REQUIREMENTS (2 MINUTE READ) [5]

 The CFTC issued a staff advisory on permitting derivatives trading
platforms and clearinghouses to count bitcoin, ether, and payment
stablecoins toward the one-year operating cost coverage component of
their financial resources requirements. Firms must still hold cash or
highly liquid securities to satisfy a separate liquidity requirement,
so crypto assets cannot fully replace traditional capital reserves
under the framework. Prior CFTC rules were silent on whether crypto
counted toward the operating cost threshold, leaving registered
entities without clear guidance on how to treat native asset holdings
on their balance sheets. The advisory, issued under Chairman Mike
Selig, reflects the CFTC's approach of using staff guidance to extend
crypto clarity in derivatives markets without full rulemaking cycles.

INNOVATION & LAUNCHES

 ETHEREUM FOUNDATION DOUBLES SECURITY TEAM FOR POST-QUANTUM OVERHAUL
(5 MINUTE READ) [6]

 The Ethereum Foundation's first PQAI post says AI has become a more
urgent risk than quantum computing, with models finding bugs in
production software faster than people can review them, and the EF has
doubled its protocol security team in response. After more than a year
evaluating signature schemes, the EF is advocating hash-based
cryptography to replace elliptic curves in three places: hash-based
validator keys for consensus, a migration path to hash-based
signatures for wallets, and hash-based blob commitments. Justin Drake
put forward December 2028 as a target at Devcon, with 2029 as the EF's
internal deadline and a timeline review in January, while Ethlabs
pulled its Quick Slots proposal out of Hegotá to free up engineering
time. The EF calls the upgrade harder than the Merge and warns that
BLS tooling, pre-quantum contracts, wallets, multisigs, and staking
pools all need changes.

 CROSS RIVER BANK ENTERS BITCOIN-BACKED LENDING WITH $250K LOANS (3
MINUTE READ) [7]

 Cross River Bank and LendingUSA launched a Bitcoin-backed lending
program on November 23, offering loans from $10,000 to $250,000 with
LTV options at 30%, 50%, or 70% of collateral value. Borrowers can
make interest-only payments or defer until maturity, though a BTC
price decline triggers additional collateral requirements and
potential liquidation of pledged holdings custodied by a qualified
third party. This marks Cross River's first entry as a Bitcoin loan
originator, adding FDIC-insured bank competition to an existing market
that includes Ledn, Coinbase through Morpho, Column N.A. via Strike,
and Fortis Bank through Unchained.

 BINANCE HIRES PLATFORMS FOR INDEPENDENT SECURITY REVIEWS (2 MINUTE
READ) [8]

 Binance engaged Google Cloud's Mandiant, blockchain security firm
SlowMist, and Verichains to run comprehensive, independent reviews of
its security procedures, validating current infrastructure and
assessing its security posture going forward. CSO Jimmy Su said
Binance is committed to acting on what the firms find, and co-CEO
Richard Teng called user asset security the exchange's "first
responsibility." The announcement covers only the engagement, with no
published scope, completion date, or commitment to release findings.
It comes 25 days after the intrusion on October 29 that took more than
$6 billion and that the DOJ attributes to North Korean actors, which
the release does not mention.

 UK BARS CRYPTO EXCHANGES FROM MARKET MAKING ON THEIR OWN PLATFORMS (4
MINUTE READ) [9]

 HM Treasury made S.I. 2026/1219 on November 18, adding Article 9UA,
which excludes dealing as a market maker on a qualifying cryptoasset
trading platform (QCATP) from the regulated activity of dealing in
qualifying cryptoassets as principal. The FCA's final rule CRYPTO
6.6.1R bars a QCATP operator from executing orders against its
proprietary capital on the platform it runs, and guidance CRYPTO
6.6.4G states the operator cannot act as a market maker there.
Operators can still use matched principal trading for client orders
under non-discretionary rules and deal as principal away from their
own venue, but must identify, monitor, and disclose market makers and
any liquidity incentive schemes. The rules take effect with the UK's
new crypto regime on October 25, 2027.

 MONAD'S FIRST YEAR: WHAT THE TEAMS HAVE BUILT SINCE MAINNET (10
MINUTE READ) [10]

 A one-year anniversary article crediting the teams behind Monad.
Consensus work progressed from MonadBFT to Cadence, with Quantum
Cadence making safety independent of signatures. Other work includes
BTX threshold encryption for an encrypted mempool, APES ordering
preferences, a zkVM, Private Settlement Domains, MIP-8 page-aware
storage pricing, and passkey-derived accounts. Ecosystem figures: 885
teams in the Metropolis hackathon and $82M of weekly tokenized
equities volume, up 90% week over week. Visa added Monad to its
stablecoin settlement program, Rain supports cards on it, and MetaMask
runs its Money Account earn and card program there.

 STELLAR'S BELL (PROTOCOL 30) BRINGS SMART ACCOUNTS AND QUANTUM-SAFE
SIGNATURES (8 MINUTE READ) [11]

 Stellar's Protocol 30 upgrade, Bell, is live on mainnet following the
validator vote on November 23. CAP-84 adds muxed contract addresses, a
new "W" format carrying a 64-bit ID, so one smart account can hold
funds for many customers in an omnibus model instead of one contract
per customer, and CAP-87 lets smart contracts verify NIST's ML-DSA
post-quantum signatures natively inside Stellar Core. CAP-88 moves
ledger close times to a millisecond clock, removing the one-second
floor so future upgrades can push block times below the current 5
seconds. Network transaction signatures stay Ed25519, and indexers or
explorers that decode raw ledger data must update their XDR
definitions because the ledger header format changes.
