 BINANCE SECURITY INCIDENT UPDATE #3: 90% OF STOLEN ASSETS REPLENISHED
(2 MINUTE READ) [4]

 Binance has reached 90% replenishment of the $6.3B drained from its
hot wallets on October 29, when attackers compromised a DevOps
engineer's laptop to access internal signing services. All top-10
assets (BTC, ETH, USDT, BNB, SOL, USDC, XRP, USD1, RLUSD, and ZEC) are
now 100% backed. The remaining 10% covers less-liquid tokens outside
the top 10 that are harder to source at volume. Binance funded the
replenishment through partial SAFU deployment, a portion of the
Futures insurance fund, and corporate treasury, and took a new Proof
of Reserves snapshot at October 31 00:00 UTC, with the ZK proof
expected by November 3. Withdrawal services are scheduled to reopen
November 1, with Binance absorbing all financial losses and users
facing no shortfall.

 CHAINALYSIS SAYS HACKER USING ‘OFFENSIVE AGENTIC TECHNIQUES'
ACCESSED ITS CUSTOMERS' NAMES AND EMAILS (4 MINUTE READ) [5]

 An unidentified AI-assisted hacker accessed “non-sensitive”
customer data, including user IDs, full names, emails, and role
labels. Chainalysis says it has found no sign that investigation
content, query results, plaintext passwords, MFA secrets, or session
tokens were accessed. It did not say when the intrusion occurred, how
many customers were affected, or how AI agents were used. It says the
breach is unrelated to the Binance hack. Separately, Chainalysis puts
the Binance theft at $6.4B, attributed to North Korean groups. It says
the attackers are using automation to cycle funds between addresses
and using JustLend on Tron to get around stablecoin freezes. About
$2.8B in stablecoins has already been frozen.

 EPHEMERAL ROLLUP: THE NEW "L1.5" OF ETHEREUM (6 MINUTE READ) [6]

 A proposed "L1.5" pattern for Ethereum separates stateless compute
from state by placing user secrets in wallets and commitments,
nullifiers, and verifiers on L1, so rollup-style execution can run
without any long-lived operator holding user state. Three forthcoming
EIPs enable this: EIP-8141 frame transactions let contracts self-pay
fees after verifying a proof, EIP-8250 keyed nonces repurpose privacy
nullifiers to reject double spends, and EIP-8288 aggregates proofs so
the block carries a single recursive proof checked once per node. Any
prover can take an L1.5 job (a stateless function over L1 state and
user inputs) so long as its output is checked by L1, its fee is paid
without linking payer identity, and submission requires no dedicated
sequencer. Target applications include gasless payments via
threshold-blind-signed tickets, fair exchange for private RPC and PIR
queries, and stealth privacy pools, with a three-step migration path
for existing L2 apps: move owned state into wallets, enable contracts
to issue their own VERIFY-frame transactions, and open proving and
submission to any party.

 STRIPE POWERS STABLECOIN DEPOSITS IN WALMART'S ONEPAY (2 MINUTE READ)
[7]

 Stripe's Bridge platform now powers USDC deposits into OnePay,
Walmart's consumer financial services app, letting select US customers
fund checking accounts from external wallets or exchanges across 12
chains, including Base, Solana, and Ethereum. Bridge handles deposit
address generation, compliance checks, and USDC-to-USD conversion,
with balances credited within moments and no conversion fees at
launch. The integration targets gig workers and cross-border earners
who receive USDC and want to access those funds through a mainstream
banking product without holding a separate stablecoin balance. OnePay
describes this as the first of several stablecoin-enabled features
planned for the platform, signaling a broader buildout of crypto rails
inside Walmart's fintech stack.

 BINANCE $6.3B HACK: WOULD YOUR PERP DEX BE SAFER? (8 MINUTE READ) [8]


 On October 29, a hacked DevOps laptop gave attackers access to
Binance's internal signing system, which approved an EIP-7702
delegation handing hot wallet control to the attacker's contract,
draining $6.3B with no private key compromise required and $3B since
frozen. The same vector cost Bitget $350M a month earlier, both
exploiting signing systems configured with blocklists rather than
allowlists that permit only known-good transactions. Hyperliquid holds
$5.6B of its $6.5B USDC in a Coinbase-controlled treasury address
moved by unsigned system transactions, meaning a single key or
validator compromise reaches the bulk of user funds. Lighter ($1B,
Ethereum) uses ZK proofs to verify withdrawals with a 14-day
forced-exit escape hatch, the only DEX among four analyzed where
server compromise alone cannot drain balances, though its 4-of-7
security council bypassed upgrade-notice requirements five times in
October.

 NON-USD STABLECOINS ARE ONE OF THE MOST UNDERRATED SEGMENTS (3 MINUTE
READ) [9]

 A key neobank use is avoiding FX fees, since paying in another
currency often costs over 1%, and local stablecoins with
multi-currency settlement can remove that. The main problem is
fragmentation, with many issuers per currency, such as six for the
real, three for the yen, and three for the Swiss franc, plus several
for EURC-type euro coins. Questions remain around how many guarantee
1:1 backing or can be verified in real time, and the industry may
benefit from less issuer competition.

 THE LENDERS BEHIND CURATOR VAULTS (5 MINUTE READ) [11]

 RWA.xyz's analysis of $6.1B in Morpho variable-rate stablecoin
lending identifies supply, not borrowing demand, as the binding growth
constraint, with five curators (Steakhouse, Sentora, Gauntlet,
SparkDAO, and RockawayX) overseeing 80%+ ($5.0B) of deployed capital.
Of the $2.6B traced to specific funding sources, platform earn
integrations and Sky-authorized protocol balance sheets each account
for $1.1B, with issuer reserve lending via Ethena and similar
protocols contributing the rest. Platform earn integrations offer the
most expandable near-term growth vector, while credit limits cap
protocol balance sheet allocations and redemption liquidity
requirements constrain issuer reserve portfolios. For tokenized
collateral, transfer restrictions, redemption terms, and
secondary-market liquidity under stress set the ceiling on lender
capacity.
