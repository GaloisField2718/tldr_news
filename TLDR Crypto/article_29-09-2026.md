 BITGET STARTS PHASED WITHDRAWAL RESUMPTION FOLLOWING $388 MILLION
EXPLOIT (3 MINUTE READ) [4]

 Bitget began restoring withdrawals in stages after an exploit on
September 24 drained ~$388 million from its hot and warm wallets, the
largest reported crypto theft this year. BTC withdrawals resumed
September 29 at 8 AM UTC, with ETH following September 29, USDT on
September 30, and all remaining assets, fiat, and P2P by October 2.
Bitget said an attacker exploited a vulnerability in a third-party
security product to obtain internal credentials and send fraudulent
withdrawal commands, bypassing risk controls. Private keys, cold
wallets, and user balances weren't affected. Losses will be fully
covered by Bitget's 5,500 BTC User Protection Fund. Bitget said it
suspects the attackers were "sophisticated" and "state-backed,"
pointing to North Korea.

 STRATEGY PROPOSES DAILY DIVIDENDS TO BRING STRC BACK TOWARD $100 (3
MINUTE READ) [5]

 Strategy is seeking shareholder approval on October 28 to implement
daily dividend accrual across its four US-listed preferred stocks
(STRF, STRC, STRK, and STRD), a timing-only change that leaves
existing dividend rates and total payouts unchanged. STRC has traded
below its $100 stated value since May, fell to $71 during bitcoin's
June correction, and has failed to recover to par despite a 12% annual
yield and roughly $1 billion in preferred share repurchases. Daily
accrual, paid each subsequent business day, is designed to attract
income-focused investors by eliminating reinvestment gaps between
distributions and dampening price swings around payment dates.
Strategy holds 846,000 BTC acquired at an average cost of $75,416 per
coin, meaning bitcoin price moves feed through to these preferred
instruments, and the June drawdown in BTC explains STRC's drop below
par.

 THE CRYPTOGRAPHIC WORLD COMPUTER (6 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Vitalik Buterin's new paper reframes Ethereum's post-Hegota
trajectory as a shift from blockchain to "cryptographic world
computer," a hybrid architecture combining traditional consensus with
modern cryptography to support properties no pure blockchain can
deliver. The roadmap covers FOCIL for censorship resistance, EIP-8288,
Lean consensus, state management improvements, and advanced mempool
upgrades including transaction privacy. The paper also raises
obfuscation as a longer-term primitive that could expand what on-chain
computation can offer. Buterin structures the argument through a
section-by-section comparison of the Bitcoin whitepaper against
Ethereum's 2015, 2025, and 2030 states to show how far the design
goals have diverged from their origins.

 SOLANA UPGRADE THAT COULD CUT FINALITY FROM 12.8S TO 150MS (4 MINUTE
READ) [7]

 Solana's Alpenglow upgrade has moved to public testnet, replacing
TowerBFT with a new consensus protocol called Votor that reduces
transaction finality from 12.8 seconds to 150 milliseconds. Votor
replaces on-chain vote accumulation across 32 slots with direct
validator-to-validator messaging that settles in one or two rounds,
requiring no changes to wallets or user-facing transaction flows. The
rollout requires Agave 4.3 validator software, while Firedancer and
Frankendancer clients do not yet support the upgrade, and a tentative
mainnet date of September 28 has not been confirmed. The finality
reduction would cut the hold periods exchanges impose before crediting
deposits, the wait bridges require before releasing cross-chain funds,
and the confirmation delays merchants currently face.

 WHY ETHEREUM'S L1 STATE OWNERSHIP IS NON-NEGOTIABLE (3 MINUTE READ)
[8]

 Offchain computation with onchain proofs creates a cryptographic
bulletin board, not a world computer. Ethereum Foundation researcher
Barnabé Monnot draws the line at state ownership: Ethereum's network
effects derive from its liquidity and accessible onchain state, which
allow permissionless actions like liquidations without dependence on
brittle third-party APIs or centralized servers. Composability scales
with how granular and accessible onchain state is, and offchain state
resolution fractures that permissionless composability. The Ethlabs
vision follows: L1 as liquidity hub and secure asset layer, with L2s
as differentiated state neighborhoods that reinforce and extend L1
network effects while absorbing the offchain economy.

 WHY AI AGENTS COULD MAKE BANK RUNS FASTER AND MORE VIOLENT (2 MINUTE
READ) [9]

 The real story isn't an imminent agentic bank run but the collapse of
cheap bank funding broadly, which will compress return on equity for
banks and brokers like Schwab and IBKR that rely on idle sweep-account
cash. When agentic bank runs do happen, they'll be faster and more
violent than human ones like SVB's, because AI agents have much lower
behavioral diversity than humans.

 DEFENSE SECRETARY PETE HEGSETH HOLDS BITCOIN IN A COINBASE WALLET (2
MINUTE READ) [10]

 Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth disclosed Bitcoin holdings of
$16,000-$65,000 in a Coinbase wallet via his 2025 OGE financial
disclosure, split across two capital gains entries. The filing shows
no Bitcoin transactions above $1,000 during 2025, indicating Hegseth
held a passive position throughout the year. His exposure is modest
compared to President Trump's June filing, which reported over $50
million in Bitcoin and $1.2 billion in crypto-related earnings. The
disclosures from multiple senior US executive branch officials mark
Bitcoin ownership as established at the cabinet level, with Coinbase
serving as the disclosed custodian for at least one sitting secretary.


 CIRCLE'S TAZAPAY ACQUISITION IS REALLY ABOUT BUYING AN OFF-RAMP FOR
USDC (5 MINUTE READ) [11]

 By tracing on-chain flows behind Circle's September 8 announcement to
acquire Tazapay, a Singapore B2B cross-border payments firm, it seems
about a third of Tazapay's payout business is already funded in USDC
(up from under 10% in June), with monthly redemptions into Circle
hitting $562 million in the first 25 days of September. Circle isn't
buying a future USDC channel but a client that already burns over half
a billion dollars of USDC monthly through Circle Mint plus the fiat
off-ramp rails on the other side, since Tazapay's product is
one-directional (stablecoin in, fiat out) with almost no on-chain
revenue (2 basis points). The real business is the FX spread at the
off-ramp, not the blockchain layer itself.
