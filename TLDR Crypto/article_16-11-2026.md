 HOUSE OVERSIGHT CHAIR LOOKS TO BAN GOVT OFFICIALS' PREDICTION MARKET
BETS (2 MINUTE READ) [4]

 Chair James Comer released findings from a May investigation covering
1,700 pages of documents and five briefings from prediction market
firms, and urges legislation restricting what bets federal employees,
lawmakers, and staff can place, up to an outright ban. The probe
follows an Army soldier's Polymarket bets on Maduro's capture and a
White House teleprompter operator profiting on Kalshi. Kalshi and
Polymarket say they have tightened controls, and the Senate passed a
resolution in April banning members from such trading.

 KAVA LEND LOSES ABOUT $3.8 MILLION TO HACK, KAVA TOKEN DOWN NEARLY
40% (3 MINUTE READ) [5]

 An attacker drained about 55.2M KAVA (~$3.76M at pre-attack prices)
by exploiting a dormant BUSD market, shrinking its net supply to a
tiny nonzero figure so deposit values inflated ~243,000-fold, then
borrowing KAVA against it 127 times. About 49.2M KAVA was bridged to
BNB Chain through LayerZero and sold for about $2.1M. The attacker had
been preparing since November 8. Kava says it is “not an infinite
mint” and is contained to Kava Lend, has paused the Axelar bridge,
and promises a full incident report. KAVA was down about 39% at
publication, and exchanges paused deposits and withdrawals.

 CHATGPT WALLET LAUNCHES FOR AGENTIC COMMERCE (6 MINUTE READ) [6]

 OpenAI's ChatGPT Wallet, launched Friday, lets OpenAI's always-on
agent check out on merchant sites using your own credit card. At
checkout, the card network generates a one-time substitute card number
capped at the approved amount, so the agent never holds your real
card, and you keep your points and dispute rights. Issuers gain
visibility versus wallets like Stripe's Link, which charge your card
in a second transaction, and merchants may pay lower interchange and
see who is buying. Users review and approve each purchase with Face
ID, and it is limited to US ChatGPT Pro users 18 or older with Amex,
Mastercard, or Visa credit cards only. Under the hood, it uses a
vault, enrollment in the networks' agent programs (Visa Intelligent
Commerce, Mastercard Agent Pay, and Amex ACE), and two sets of
headers: Web Bot Auth signatures and user signals in the Forwarded
header, with OpenAI working with fraud providers like Forter,
Riskified, Sardine, and Signifyd to honor them. Drawbacks are sites
still blocking agents, the per-purchase approval, partial card
coverage, and slow, token-heavy browser checkout.

 TEMPO CONFIGURABLE ACCOUNTS: PQ + NATIVE MULTISIG AS DEFAULT (3
MINUTE READ) [7]

 Tempo's Configurable Accounts (T14) will deploy to mainnet on
December 1, introducing post-quantum signature groundwork and native
multisig as default account primitives rather than optional add-ons.
The upgrade's core claim is friction elimination: both capabilities
existed on prior systems, but Configurable Accounts makes them cheap
enough to use without weighing cost against benefit. Paradigm frames
T14 as a near-completion milestone in "Pragmatic Native Account
Abstraction" for the Ethereum ecosystem, with one remaining component,
Attestable Accounts, expected in the coming weeks.

 HOW WE MADE $6M+ EXPLOITING A SINGLE ONCHAIN EDGE (10 MINUTE READ)
[9]

 A trader and collaborators extracted $6M+ from two Robinhood Chain
listings by monitoring two public data sources: Wintermute's on-chain
market-making contract whitelists and BitGo's GitHub repository
(Robinhood's custody partner). For cashcat, a "hoodeth:cashcat" string
in a public BitGo pull request on July 27 and Wintermute's WETH pool
approval on August 3 at 17:48 UTC gave days of advance notice. The
group accumulated over $1M at around 4% of supply and realized $4M+
when Robinhood listed three days later. A parallel pipeline applied to
PONS produced another $2M, with the method requiring no privileged
access, only structured monitoring of public contract approval events
and custody provider repositories.

 THE ANTIFRAGILE INTERNET (10 MINUTE READ) [10]

 October 2026's incident cluster (PHP2Shell exposing 5M+ hosts,
Railway decrypting secrets across 100K workspaces, Binance losing
$6.3B, and eMudhra issuing a signing key set to "4") frames the
argument that AI has collapsed the cost of exploitation to near zero.
There are three agent-specific threat vectors: agents as weapons,
agents as targets, and agents as impostors. USV's investment thesis
closes on crypto: blockchains proved strangers can transact without
mutual trust, and the firm expects decentralized reputation, PAP/A2A
agent-identity protocols, and permissionless rails to embed in
mainstream apps as a two-lane internet takes shape.

MISCELLANEOUS

 NEXO REPORT SHOWS GOVERNMENT PAYMENTS DOMINATE ITS CARD BY VALUE (1
MINUTE READ) [11]

 Alex highlights Nexo's card report: 91% of government payments exceed
€1,000, 55% fall between €5,000 and €10,000, and nearly 12%
exceed the €10,000 daily limit. He estimates the average government
payment at about €6,400 against €80 for the average card
transaction, and notes millennials make up about 42% of them. Since
Nexo markets borrowing against crypto to avoid capital gains tax, he
wonders how many of these were paid in credit mode, effectively tax
planning with a card.

 THE CURRENT STATE OF CRYPTO MARKETS (2 MINUTE READ) [12]

 There's a scary macro backdrop of cyberattacks and a stock dip.
Keeping this in mind, the Binance hack was a major bear signal because
stablecoin supply fell more than 5% afterward. The breakout into the
80s was a bull trap. Losing the $74K range low was the real bear
signal, and although the $60K mark should hold, a break would ensure
capitulation into $40K like in 2022.
