 KIM JONG-UN'S SISTER REJECTS NORTH KOREAN LINKS TO $6B BINANCE HACK
(3 MINUTE READ) [4]

 Kim Yo-jong, a senior North Korean official, rejected the FBI's
assessment, saying the FBI is trying to brand the country a “cyber
criminal state.” Three firms, including TRM Labs, and the FBI over
the weekend attributed the hack to North Korean actors. TRM says the
attacker tested code on three wallets, then used EIP-7702 to turn a
Binance hot wallet into a sweeper, with 50 pre-signed authorizations
so one would stay valid. Earlier Monday, Binance published its first
proof of reserves and said it replenished about 95% of affected assets
with its own funds.

 $146M DRAINED FROM TWO EXCHANGES IN 24 HOURS EXTENDS FOUR-DAY HACK
STREAK (3 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Mercado Bitcoin, Brazil's largest centralized exchange, lost $40M in
an exploit on November 2, while an unnamed EU-based exchange lost
$106M the same day, bringing the combined single-day custodial loss to
approximately $146M. Both incidents fall within a four-day window that
also includes what market observers are calling the largest exploit on
record, raising questions about a coordinated attack vector or shared
infrastructure vulnerability across venues. The cluster puts renewed
pressure on CEX hot wallet exposure and cross-jurisdictional key
management practices.

 CONSENSYS LAUNCHES CROSS-LEDGER SETTLEMENT AND ROUTING STACK (2
MINUTE READ) [6]

 Launched at Devcon in Mumbai, “Sovereign Network
Interoperability” lets banks settle across independently run
blockchains without moving assets off their native ledgers. It covers
securities settlement, collateral mobility, wholesale and
cross-currency payments, and funds servicing. A Cross Ledger
Interoperability Protocol built on zero-knowledge research verifies
cross-ledger state cryptographically, with no bridges. An institution
sets its requirements and approved counterparties, and the software
picks a route, coordinates execution, and tracks the result. EVM
networks are supported now, with Canton planned, and it will also
support the Ethereum Economic Zone standard and IBC. The launch
follows Consensys' October 8 ClearToken partnership and September
split of MetaMask from its institutional business.

 INTRODUCING SILVER: GATTACA'S NEW ETHEREUM CONSENSUS CLIENT (6 MINUTE
READ) [7]

 Silver is an open-source Ethereum consensus client built from
scratch. It recorded median block-processing latency of 6.5ms (5.2x
faster than next-best) and p99 of 9.5ms (9.6x faster) across 20,300
canonical mainnet blocks benchmarked against Prysm, Lighthouse, and
Grandine from October 17-20. The performance gains stem from Gattaca's
thread-per-core, share-nothing Flux runtime with six CPU-pinned tiles,
a new p2p layer supporting up to 2 Gbit/s gossip traffic, and a
formally verified BLS/KZG crypto library delivering 2x+ throughput
over existing implementations. Gattaca's roadmap targets EIP-8198
(Quick Slots for shorter slot times) and EIP-8292 (post-quantum
attestation), with plans to publish the underlying libraries so other
client teams can adopt them. Silver is currently in alpha, with audits
and final testing still pending.

 THE BALANCE OF POWER (7 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Stripe's Link Balance converts user deposits to USDC on its Tempo
chain, holds them in Privy non-custodial wallets, and liquidates back
to a Lead Bank virtual card at checkout, letting merchants receive a
standard Visa payment while Stripe accumulates stablecoin float. The
three-step roadmap moves from card-funded to bank-transfer-funded
balances, then to Stripe's Machine Payments Protocol, which routes
consumer-to-merchant flows on Tempo with no Visa involvement, no
interchange, and instant settlement. With 350M Link users, $1.9T in
annual merchant volume, and a free open stack of Tempo (rails), OUSD
via Bridge (money), and MPP (protocol), Stripe is commoditizing each
payments layer while concentrating network effects at the wallet.
Autonomous agents route to whatever rails are cheapest. Stripe is now
using this against Visa interchange, but future entrants could apply
the same logic against Stripe's own network moat.

 THE NEXT FRONT IN THE PERSONAL AGENT WARS: CONSUMER FINANCE (8 MINUTE
READ) [9]

 Dedicated finance AI agents will displace general competitors like
Meta's Muse and Instinct for money management, because their
incentives run in opposite directions: general agents monetize via
transaction fees and advertising, rewarding spending, while finance
agents succeed when users save more. Licensing and fiduciary
obligations compound the divide, as general agents lack the compliance
infrastructure to move money, recommend investments, or manage debt.
Crypto rails resolve the infrastructure gap: agent-owned wallets
interact with any protocol without logins, programmable conditions
enforce user limits at the wallet layer, and stablecoin settlement
runs 24/7 with every action auditable on-chain. The projected end
state pairs a life-concierge general agent with a finance CFO agent
coordinating via agent-to-agent messaging, with US stablecoin
legislation now providing a regulated on-chain rail from launch.

 AGENTIC PRODUCTS ARE GETTING AHEAD OF THEMSELVES ON ACCESS (4 MINUTE
READ) [10]

 Teams are racing to give agents access through virtual cards, API
credentials, prepaid balances, and tokens without addressing what
happens once they're inside. The harder problem is whether an LLM
understands what a person asked for (intent), what the system allows
(execution), and whether the two match. A payment can clear and still
be the wrong purchase, and access doesn't equal authorization, which
doesn't equal intent. Cards and API wrappers won't solve the problem,
so teams need to understand the whole transaction lifecycle. AI makes
software easier to generate but not easier to architect, and
infrastructure has to be deliberate.

 COINBASE Q3 EARNINGS (2 MINUTE READ) [11]

 Coinbase posted Q3 GAAP net income of $719M, but adjusted net income
came in at $78M after stripping out $641M in unrealized gains on
crypto held as a treasury asset. CFO Alesia Haas confirmed the GAAP
figure reflects mark-to-market appreciation, not operational earnings.
Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong filed a 10b5-1 plan to sell up to 8.17M
COIN shares (~$1.31B) through October 2027, with co-founder Fred
Ehrsam and COO Emilie Choi entering similar plans for up to 3.87M and
1.56M shares. The pre-scheduled filings insulate executives from
insider trading claims and represent a combined potential disposal of
over 13.6M shares.
