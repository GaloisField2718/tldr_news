 MORGAN STANLEY EXPECTS TO SUPPORT TOKENIZED EQUITIES IN Q1 2027 (4
MINUTE READ) [4]

 Amy Oldenburg, Morgan Stanley's head of digital asset strategy, said
the bank now expects to support tokenized equities on its alternative
trading system in Q1 2027, later than the H2 2026 timeline it gave in
March, because client demand depends on products that do not exist
yet. She pointed to DTCC's tokenization service launch at the end of
October and the GENIUS Act taking effect in January as milestones, and
said the tokenized RWA market will “without question” land in the
$3 trillion to $10 trillion range by 2030. The bank is also building
abstracted wallet infrastructure, runs a Digital Asset Lab, and holds
a conditional OCC approval for a digital trust.

 FORMER CFTC ACTING CHAIR SAYS THE CFTC CAN REGULATE ALMOST EVERYTHING
(4 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Caroline Pham, now MoonPay's chief legal officer, said the CFTC does
not need Congress to regulate crypto and called its proposed
Regulation CAM and CTX the “absolutely the kind of protection”
retail crypto traders need. She argued tokenized deposits, not
stablecoins, fit wholesale markets because atomic settlement of one
trading day would need about $15 trillion in cash that does not exist,
and expects NYSE and Nasdaq to list “real” tokenized shares with
meaningful volume within two years.

 THE STONKS EXCHANGE ACQUIRES STOCKIFY (4 MINUTE READ) [6]

 The Stonks Exchange acquired Stockify, combining Base's primary token
launch venue ($STONKEX) with a protocol that auto-distributes
tokenized equity dividends via a no-claim mechanism. Under $STFY's
unchanged mechanics, a 3% ETH trading fee routes 90% of proceeds to
purchase tokenized stocks sent to eligible wallets with no staking or
claiming required. The deal adds $STONKEX to Stockify's dividend index
at equal weight alongside NVIDIA, Meta, Apple, Alphabet, and SpaceX,
giving STFY holders direct exposure to the combined entity's growth
through existing payout infrastructure. Base now hosts 130+ tokenized
stocks targeting 250 by month's end, and the merged team aims to
supply liquidity and holder depth for newly listed assets as the chain
accelerates its push to become the primary onchain equities venue.

 BRO-AMM: A BONDED RECONCILIATION ORACLE AMM AS A LOW-RISK DEFI
PRIMITIVE (5 MINUTE READ) [7]

 AMM liquidity providers on Ethereum lose money to LVR (loss versus
rebalancing), with MEV bots capturing most arbitrage profits from
price gaps between pools and external markets. BRO-AMM addresses this
by combining oracle-verified post-trade reconciliation windows,
mandatory trader collateral, and reputation-tiered fees to compensate
legitimate arbitrageurs while penalizing excess extraction. Six-month
ETH/USDC simulations show 68-79% reductions in arbitrage losses on
Ethereum and 49-55% on Arbitrum, with Ethereum depositors shifting
from net losses to profitability. The proposal depends on trusted
oracle data and remains simulation-only, pending live validation.

MISCELLANEOUS

 OVER HALF OF TOKENIZED STOCK TRADING HAPPENS OUTSIDE US MARKET HOURS
(2 MINUTE READ) [10]

 The IMF's Global Financial Stability Report found that more than half
of tokenized equity trading occurs outside US market hours, with 80%
of transactions in the five most liquid tokenized US stocks (Tesla,
Nvidia, and Google among them) sized below one share. These tokens
carry roughly 1.5x the volatility of their traditional counterparts
while showing considerably lower liquidity, raising structural
questions about price formation and investor protection. The tokenized
equity segment totals $2.3 billion within a broader $65 billion
tokenized real-world asset market, and the IMF data positions the
segment as a retail-driven, offshore-hours venue rather than a
functional extension of regulated equity markets.

 EMPIRE MARKET CO-CREATOR SENTENCED TO 40 YEARS OVER $430M DARK WEB
BAZAAR (3 MINUTE READ) [11]

 Raheim Hamilton, co-creator of Empire Market, received a 40-year
federal prison sentence and a $5 million fine on October 9 for running
a dark web marketplace that processed over 4 million transactions
totaling $430 million between February 2018 and August 2020. The
platform accepted only cryptocurrency and encouraged vendors to use
tumbling and mixing services, with drug sales accounting for roughly
$375 million of volume. Prosecutors seized approximately 1,230 Bitcoin
and 24.4 Ether from Hamilton. Co-creator Thomas Pavey faces forfeiture
of roughly 1,584 Bitcoin, gold bars, and real estate before his own
sentencing later in October. The platform shut down in August 2020 in
an alleged $30 million exit scam, and the combined recovery of
2,800-plus Bitcoin across both defendants demonstrates that chain
analysis pierced mixing-service obfuscation at this scale.
