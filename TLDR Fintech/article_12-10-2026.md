NEWS & TRENDS

 REVOLUT CEO NIK STORONSKY SAYS COMPANY WANTS TO “DOMINATE” THE US
(10 MINUTE PODCAST) [6]

 Revolut CEO Nik Storonsky says the fintech plans to build a full US
digital banking offering, including credit cards and loans, as it
competes with incumbents such as JPMorgan and American Express. He
also expects AI and automation to reshape banking, with Revolut
preparing to test AI agents that can shop and transact on behalf of
customers.

 NETFLIX SETS NOV. 19 RELEASE FOR FTX DRAMA “THE ALTRUISTS” (2
MINUTE READ) [7]

 Netflix will release “The Altruists,” a dramatized series about
the collapse of FTX, on November 19, starring Anthony Boyle as Sam
Bankman-Fried and Julia Garner as Caroline Ellison. The show focuses
on their relationship and the effective altruism movement surrounding
FTX, turning one of crypto's biggest scandals into a scripted
Hollywood drama.

 OPENAI'S ANNUALIZED REVENUE REPORTEDLY CLOSER TO $50B, NOT $70B (2
MINUTE READ) [8]

 OpenAI has reportedly told investors that annualized revenue is
approaching $50 billion, well below the roughly $70 billion figure
previously circulated by investors attempting to compare the company
with Anthropic. The discrepancy also highlights differing revenue
definitions between the two companies, while raising more scrutiny
around OpenAI's economics as it invests heavily in infrastructure and
prepares for a potential IPO in 2027.

DEEP DIVES & REPORTS

 YOU NEED A NEW CFO (8 MINUTE READ) [9]

 AI is changing finance software from tools that help teams manage
work into systems that increasingly do the work themselves, pushing
CFOs toward a new role as builders of the company's operating system.
The AI-native finance organization is smaller and higher leverage,
with continuous forecasting, finance engineers, agent-driven
workflows, and stronger audit controls, while the CFO focuses more on
designing what people versus software should do and less on manually
collecting and reconciling data.

 WHERE WILL BANKS MAKE MONEY IF IT ISN'T FROM PAYMENTS? (6 MINUTE
READ) [10]

 AI agents and programmable money are pushing financial value away
from transaction processing and toward orchestration, identity,
liquidity, compliance, and software. McKinsey estimates agentic AI
could put $75 billion to $160 billion of payments revenue at risk by
2030, while Capgemini says $230 billion is already exposed across
areas like FX spreads, correspondent fees, and float income. The
emerging battleground is increasingly about who controls the
intelligence that decides how money moves, whether that is a bank,
fintech, software platform, or AI agent.

 CHASE ADDS PAYROLL TO LARGER BUSINESS BANKING OFFERING (3 MINUTE
READ) [12]

 Powered by Gusto, the service lets eligible small businesses handle
payroll, tax filings, and employee payments inside Chase Business
Online, with next-day processing available to qualifying checking
customers. The integration brings another essential workflow into
Chase's platform while leaving the specialized payroll calculations
and filings to its fintech partner.

 EXPERIAN UNVEILS NEW CONSUMER REPORTING AGENCY FOR CASHFLOW
UNDERWRITING (3 MINUTE READ) [13]

 Experian is building an FCRA-regulated layer that lets lenders
incorporate permissioned bank-account activity into credit decisions,
potentially helping thin-file and credit-challenged borrowers qualify.
Experian says these insights can increase approvals by up to 25%
without changing risk tolerance, while bringing consent, data
aggregation, standardized reports, analytics, and its existing scoring
tools into one ecosystem. The move puts Experian into closer
competition with Plaid, Prism Data, Mastercard Finicity, and Nova
Credit, even as Plaid remains one of its data partners.

MISCELLANEOUS

 NFL TELLS SUPREME COURT PREDICTION MARKET SPORTS CONTRACTS ARE
GAMBLING, NOT SWAPS (3 MINUTE READ) [14]

 The NFL urged the Supreme Court to review whether sports prediction
contracts such as Kalshi's should be regulated as gambling by states
or as swaps under exclusive CFTC jurisdiction, arguing that Dodd-Frank
was not intended to federalize sports betting. The dispute has
produced conflicting appeals-court rulings, while Kalshi argues its
markets are federally regulated and protected by existing integrity
controls, setting up a potentially important Supreme Court test of
prediction-market regulation.

 AMEX DINGED BY FED, OCC OVER ANTI-MONEY LAUNDERING FAILURES (2 MINUTE
READ) [15]

 American Express National Bank was ordered to pay a $350 million
penalty after the OCC found major deficiencies in its anti-money
laundering program, including weak risk assessments and systemic
failures in suspicious activity monitoring that regulators said
involved roughly $13 billion of suspected money laundering activity
over 10 years. The Federal Reserve separately issued a
cease-and-desist order covering broader enterprise-wide AML issues,
while Amex said the penalty will not affect its 2026 or 2027 guidance.
