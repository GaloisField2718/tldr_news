NEWS & TRENDS

 STRIPE VALUATION SET TO TOP $200B IN NEW TENDER OFFER (2 MINUTE READ)
[4]

 Stripe is discussing a new employee tender offer that would value the
payments company at more than $200 billion, with the transaction
expected to close early next year. The annual liquidity program lets
employees sell shares without requiring Stripe to go public, while
marking another step-up in the company's private-market valuation.

 CROWDSTRIKE TO ACQUIRE ZSCALER FOR $43.9B IN ALL-STOCK DEAL (3 MINUTE
READ) [5]

 CrowdStrike agreed to acquire Zscaler for about $43.9 billion,
combining CrowdStrike's endpoint and workload security with Zscaler's
zero-trust network platform as AI agents create new security risks
across cloud traffic. The combined company would start with more than
$10 billion of ARR, target roughly $1 billion of annual revenue
synergies, and expand CrowdStrike into a network security market it
says adds more than $185 billion of addressable opportunity by 2029.

 GTCR EXPLORES ROUGHLY $50B TAKE-PRIVATE OF GLOBAL PAYMENTS (2 MINUTE
READ) [6]

 GTCR confirmed it is working on a potential take-private of Global
Payments, where it already owns a 16.35% stake received through the
Worldpay sale. A deal could reportedly value Global Payments at around
$50 billion and would likely require additional partners, as GTCR
appears to be betting that the market is undervaluing the combined
Global Payments-Worldpay business or its integration potential.

 WHY MOST AI SERVICES STARTUPS WON'T CREATE VENTURE-SCALE RETURNS (7
MINUTE READ) [7]

 AI-native services can produce venture-scale outcomes, but only in
categories where AI is core to delivery, adoption is slow enough to
create a temporary advantage, and pricing does not quickly deflate as
competitors adopt the same tools. In sticky markets such as
accounting, cybersecurity, and wealth management, the stronger model
may be an AI-enabled roll-up that acquires existing firms and
transforms them with a proprietary agentic platform, rather than
building a new services business from scratch.

 DEEL LAUNCHES STABLECOIN SPENDING CARD ACROSS 26 MARKETS (3 MINUTE
READ) [9]

 Deel launched a Stripe-powered Visa card that lets eligible
contractors spend DLUSD balances directly without first transferring
funds to a bank, with merchants receiving local currency as usual. The
rollout covers 26 markets across Latin America, the Caribbean, and
Africa, builds on more than $45 million already held in DLUSD, and
extends stablecoins from payroll storage into everyday spending.

 PAYWARD'S KRAK PLANS EARLY 2027 LAUNCH OF US CREDIT CARDS WITH
BITCOIN CASHBACK, INCLUDING ONE SECURED BY USDC (4 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Krak's secured card would let customers pledge 350–5,000 in USDC
while earning up to 4% APY on that collateral, connecting stablecoin
balances to everyday consumer credit. First Electronic Bank will issue
both planned cards, with Cardless managing the programs and rewards
paid in dollars or bitcoin.

MISCELLANEOUS

 WORLDREMIT FOUNDER LAUNCHES ODOLA TO BUILD A BROADER BANKING PLATFORM
FOR MIGRANTS (2 MINUTE READ) [11]

 Odola Group is a fintech aimed at serving migrant workers with a
fuller financial account rather than a standalone remittance product.
It plans to let users receive salary, spend through a Visa debit card,
and send money abroad from the same account, targeting a global
remittance market approaching $1 trillion annually.

 ADYEN CO-FOUNDER PIETER VAN DER DOES TO RETIRE AS CO-CEO (3 MINUTE
READ) [12]

 Adyen co-founder Pieter van der Does will retire as co-CEO at the end
of 2026, with Ingo Uytdehaage becoming sole CEO after serving
alongside him since 2023. Van der Does will remain an advisor. Adyen
says the leadership transition does not change its financial
objectives as it enters its next phase.
