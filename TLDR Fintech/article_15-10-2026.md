NEWS & TRENDS

 BUMBLE IN ADVANCED TALKS TO GO PRIVATE AT $1B VALUATION (3 MINUTE
READ) [8]

 Bumble is reportedly in advanced talks to be acquired by Ithaca
Holdings in a deal valuing the dating-app company at roughly $1
billion, well above its $389 million market value before the report.
The potential take-private comes as Bumble struggles with falling
paying users, declining revenue, and weaker engagement among younger
consumers, highlighting broader pressure across the online dating
sector.

 JPMORGAN BLOCKS OUTSIDE AI AGENTS FROM DIRECTLY ACCESSING CUSTOMER
ACCOUNTS (5 MINUTE READ) [9]

 JPMorgan is blocking third-party AI agents such as Muse and Instinct
from directly moving money or paying bills on behalf of retail
customers, citing security, rogue-agent, and liability risks, while
still allowing limited read-only access through open-banking
connectors. At the same time, the bank is building its own agentic
tools, including Smart Cash, which can forecast cash flow and move
funds to improve yield, suggesting banks may prefer controlled
in-house agents over unrestricted external access.

 RAMP INTRODUCES GLASS THAT MAKES COMPANY KNOWLEDGE REUSABLE ACROSS AI
WORKFLOWS (4 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Ramp's Glass is an internal AI workspace designed to capture and
reuse the best prompts, workflows, context, and operating knowledge
created across the company instead of leaving them trapped in
individual chat histories. Glass connects to systems like Slack,
Notion, Linear, and Gong, supports shared sessions and reusable
skills, routes work across models, and tracks cost by project. Ramp
says the system cut its own AI bill by 30% while scaling workflows
such as feature-request capture from roughly 300 to 20,000 per month.

 RAMP'S SALES PLAYBOOK: MAX FREEMAN'S HIRING SECRETS (1 MINUTE READ)
[11]

 Ramp SVP of Sales Max Freeman describes GTM as an operating system
built around talent density, rigorous hiring, and automation. Ramp
recruits unconventional sellers such as investment bankers and reps
from high-growth, low-NPS companies, pairs that talent with structured
onboarding and quota design, and uses internal engineering tools to
automate prospect research, outbound, and pipeline workflows so reps
spend more time selling.

 THE DEATH OF THE INTERFACE: WHAT HAPPENS TO MONEY WHEN APPS,
WEBSITES, AND BUTTONS GO AWAY (5 MINUTE READ) [12]

 AI agents will replace much of today's app and website interface,
which means payments must also become “headless” and usable
directly by software agents. The opportunity is in combining
stablecoins, real-time settlement, embedded wallets, fiat
connectivity, and card networks behind APIs that let agents hold and
move money globally while enforcing permissions, limits, and liability
rules.

 MARK ZUCKERBERG AND ALEXANDR WANG DISCUSS META'S MUSE WONDER AND THE
FUTURE OF AI (134 MINUTE PODCAST) [13]

 John Collison sat down with Mark Zuckerberg and Alexandr Wang to
discuss Meta's new frontier model, Muse Wonder, and the broader race
to build leading AI systems. The conversation spans model development,
Meta's willingness to invest $100 billion, agentic commerce,
OpenRouter, new computing interfaces beyond smartphones, and
Zuckerberg's view that AI will support multiple major winners rather
than a single dominant platform.

 WHY IS MONZO SO MUCH CHEAPER THAN REVOLUT? (8 MINUTE READ) [14]

 Monzo raised $500M at a $4.5B valuation in late 2021, near the peak
of fintech VC funding. Its last employee share sale last year nudged
that to $5.9B. That's a 31% gain in three years, while annual revenue
grew from £154M to the current £1.7B. Over the same period,
competitor Revolut went from $33B in 2021 to $115B in a secondary sale
this July, a 3.5x increase. That prices Revolut at roughly 19x its
2025 revenues vs. Monzo's 3x revenues.

 CARTA RECEIVES CSSF AUTHORIZATION IN LUXEMBOURG (3 MINUTE READ) [17]

 Carta received Specialised PSF authorization from Luxembourg's CSSF,
allowing it to provide licensed fund administration and transfer
agency services for structures including SCSp, RAIF, SIF, and SICAR.
The move extends Carta's private-capital ERP into one of Europe's
largest fund domiciles, with fund administration, KYC, investor
workflows, and private credit loan operations running on the same
platform across global fund structures.

 SPLITWISE LAUNCHES FREE BILL-SPLITTING TOOL (2 MINUTE READ) [18]

 Splitwise, the platform that makes it easier to share expenses with
friends and family, today announced Itemize, a new, collaborative way
to split a bill from a photo. The free in-app feature allows one
person to take a picture of a restaurant or bar receipt and quickly
calculate each person's total, with proportional tax and tip included.
Everyone at the table can claim their individual and shared items to
keep the split fair, even if they don't have the Splitwise app
installed.

 AIRTABLE LAYS OUT POST-ACQUISITION ROADMAP AFTER MAJOR TEAM CUTS (6
MINUTE READ) [19]

 Following its acquisition by Bending Spoons, Airtable is moving to a
leaner operating model while refocusing the product around three
priorities: faster improvements to the core platform, deeper AI and
agent workflows, and stronger enterprise controls. Near-term plans
include monthly customer-requested improvements, better performance
and scale, upgraded Omni, Airtable Canvas, a stronger MCP,
bring-your-own agents, agent permissions, audit logs, and tighter
governance so Airtable can serve as a shared system of record for both
people and AI agents.

 JPMORGAN CEO DIMON WARNS CUSTOMERS OF ‘LIABILITY TRANSFER' WITH AI
(4 MINUTE READ) [20]

 Autonomous money management could weaken banks' grip on deposits as
software moves funds in search of better returns. JPMorgan is
restricting outside agents' access while piloting Smart Cash, its own
tool for optimizing balances across internal accounts. The bank says
productivity experiments are promising, but benefits from generative
and agentic AI aren't yet visible in quarterly earnings.

 AGENT PAYMENTS ARE REINVENTING A PROBLEM THE CARD INDUSTRY ALREADY
SOLVED (10 MINUTE READ) [21]

 For as long as card payments have existed, a human has been at the
keyboard or the terminal, tapping a card, typing a PIN, and clicking
buy now. That assumption is ending. A new class of AI agents is being
built to shop, negotiate, and pay on our behalf. Your assistant
reorders groceries when the fridge runs low. A travel agent books the
whole trip the moment prices dip. A procurement bot restocks a
warehouse without anyone signing off on each order.
