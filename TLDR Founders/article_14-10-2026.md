HEADLINES & TRENDS

 THE “ALIEN” ECONOMICS OF AI MONETIZATION (8 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Two-thirds of the companies on the Forbes AI 50 list use some form of
usage-based pricing. AI's variable costs and unpredictable demand are
causing today's leading companies to operate in ways that would seem
alien to traditional SaaS businesses. These companies are iterating
much faster on pricing, prioritizing market share over ARR, and
pursuing multiple go-to-market strategies at once.

 WHY THERE WILL BE MORE PRO RATA CONFLICTS (2 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Early-stage funds increasingly exercise pro rata rights across
multiple rounds as they grow, driven by outliers offering significant
upside potential. The rise of secondary markets and financial vehicles
like Fund of Funds and SPVs supports smaller funds in monetizing pro
rata shares. Faster round pacing within a fund's investment period
further contributes to potential pro rata conflicts.

 ARR VS GAAP REVENUE | SPOTTING “ARR” LIES (8 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Businesses need to stay honest about what ARR actually is. If ARR
doesn't ultimately lead to revenue and cash, then it's a fake metric.
Most companies need to redefine or evolve their definitions of ARR for
the AI era. ARR should turn into GAAP revenue, and it shouldn't take a
really long time for that to happen.

MISCELLANEOUS

 OK, I MIGHT HAVE BEEN WRONG ABOUT BUILDING WITH A LEAN TEAM IN THE
AGE OF AI (1 MINUTE READ) [14]

 Chatbase is now at 40 people, and it plans to double its number of
employees in the next 6 months. Building is now much easier, and the
opportunity cost of not building more is huge. Companies that want to
build more, grow more, and achieve more still need to hire because
humans are still needed in many parts of the building loop. While this
might change, it doesn't make sense to act like it isn't true right
now.

 CAPITAL STACKS FOR ATOMS (28 MINUTE READ) [15]

 Capital allocation has shifted from bits-based businesses to
atoms-based businesses over the last few years. Investors have been
directing capital to overcome the physical-world bottlenecks of
inference. There's an emerging cohort of atoms-based companies in the
fields of energy, manufacturing, space, robotics, and beyond. These
hard asset businesses are increasingly built and valued on bundles of
cash flows rather than revenue multiples. This requires an entirely
different approach to capital formation across the company lifecycle.
