 85% OF AEO IS WON OFFSITE (16 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Most AEO is won offsite. Companies are wasting AEO budgets on content
that sits on their own websites. This post presents a step-by-step
playbook on offsite AEO. The secret weapon is creators, an underpriced
asset in AEO. The post also discusses how to measure AEO beyond vanity
metrics.

 UNGLAMOROUS (7 MINUTE READ) [11]

 This post contains a list of 21 founders and the unglamorous work
they did to bring their visions to life. The Pebble founders worked on
their own factory line, Wakefit's founders acted as their own delivery
men, and Stripe's founders did their IT support in person. The
founders on this list spent time doing something that looked small:
they shilled their own products, sent DMs nobody answered, and
pretended to be their own users.

 7 WAYS TO HANDLE YOUR AI GROSS MARGIN PROBLEM (8 MINUTE READ) [12]

 Charge AI customers by the token, and they'll ask whether you're four
times as good as the model you're marking up. An investor in more than
700 companies walks through how AI startups should price and who pays
the token bill. The advice is to charge for a unit your product
controls, like a personalized email from an AI SDR. Keep measuring the
token cost behind each one, since two support resolutions can bring in
the same revenue and cost wildly different amounts. Vanta's CEO hears
that markup question, so Vanta absorbs its token costs and takes lower
margins to get more people using its agent (GitHub just started
billing for the tokens it uses).

 WHY WOULDN'T THE LABS DO IT? (13 MINUTE READ) [19]

 Startups once feared Google entering their market, but today AI labs
pose broader threats as their models cover most knowledge work. The
labs' extensive capabilities can displace AI companies either through
direct competition or by developing superior models. Successful tech
ventures should aim to operate outside these labs' immediate focus
areas, like in physical operations, proprietary data, or regulated
workflows.

 SAAS S&M DOES NOT GET MORE EFFICIENT WITH SCALE (3 MINUTE READ) [20]

 Most founders expect each sales and marketing dollar to buy more
revenue once the company gets bigger. A VC firm checked 158 SaaS IPOs
and found the median fell from $0.71 of new annual revenue per sales
and marketing dollar the year before listing to $0.66 in the listing
year. The 20 most recent IPOs had nearly twice the revenue at listing
and still landed at $0.66, so the firm treats $0.50 to $0.70 as a
normal range for a company that goes public. Some of the strongest
companies listed well below it (Palantir came in at $0.33 and
Snowflake at $0.57).
