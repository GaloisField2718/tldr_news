 $5T OPPORTUNITY: AI ROLL UPS (21 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Buy a business that already has customers, the licenses, and the
trust, and then change how the work gets done with AI agents. Around
$5 trillion worth of businesses in the US will change hands by 2035.
Most of these belong to boomers heading into retirement. Many of them
have nobody lined up to buy them. This guide walks readers through how
to find the right business, buy it, and run it with agents.

 AI FOR BOSTON COLLEGE'S INVESTMENT COMMITTEE (15 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Pat Grady frames AI as a technology wave that is bigger, faster, and
fundamentally different because progress increasingly comes from
computation. His reflection for Boston College's Investment Committee
examines how that shift is reshaping technology and investing.

 THE RISE OF THE NEO NEO LAB (6 MINUTE READ) [7]

 The last generation of enterprise software was built around
proprietary data and workflows. The next will be built around the
intelligence derived from them. Frontier labs will continue pushing
the ceiling of general intelligence, but enterprises will take that
intelligence and teach it what only they know. Companies with enough
proprietary data, expert judgment, workflow volume, and distribution
will have the raw materials to produce proprietary intelligence of
their own.

STRATEGIES & TACTICS

 THE IRREPLACEABLES. THE EMPLOYEES TO NEVER LET GO. EVEN IF YOU HAVE
TO INVENT A ROLE (8 MINUTE READ) [8]

 You will always regret letting one of the truly irreplaceable people
on your team leave. This usually happens with scale. As things change,
you need larger teams and different processes, and new executives may
not think these employees are so irreplaceable. However, they are the
ones who truly know how and why everything works and are often the
best to help figure out how the next move can work. Top performers are
the people you'd rehire into their role - irreplaceables are the ones
you would rehire into a role that doesn't exist yet.

 GUSTO'S COFOUNDER SAYS AI MADE YOUR REVENUE WORTH LESS TO AN ACQUIRER
AND YOUR SPEED WORTH MORE (3 MINUTE READ) [9]

 An acquirer may love your product and still decide it's faster to
build its own. Gusto's cofounder explains that AI has shortened the
time the company expects internal development to take, making
acquisitions harder to justify. His example is hypothetical. If buying
a company saves 18 months' worth of $50 million to Gusto, a business
with $5 million in revenue might fit at a 10x multiple. One with $50
million in revenue probably won't. Your revenue can raise your asking
price without increasing how much development time the buyer saves.

MISCELLANEOUS

 THE CAUTIOUS TALE OF CAL AI (4 MINUTE READ) [14]

 Before Cal AI reached a reported $50 million in annual recurring
revenue, one founder's mom had to co-sign the company bank account.
Two of the four founders were still in high school. People now ask how
they assembled that team, but the answer involves a cousin's YouTube
recommendation, a cold DM, and meeting a future cofounder while
working at another startup. There was no recruiting process to copy.
The advice is more realistic: look for someone who is available and
can learn, and expect to offer enough money or equity to make joining
worthwhile.

 VALUE ACCRUAL FLYWHEEL (5 MINUTE READ) [15]

 Software accrues value by creating dependencies that compound over
time and raise switching costs. These dependencies compound internally
as the product embeds deeper into workflows and externally through
network effects and ecosystem adoption. Dependencies inside critical
systems get a value multiplier because ripping them out threatens the
business itself. Resulting revenue from these embedded software
systems funds new features that create new dependencies, creating a
Value Accrual Flywheel.
