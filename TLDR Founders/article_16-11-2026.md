HEADLINES & TRENDS

 DOES MORE SOFTWARE MEAN MORE BUSINESSES? (5 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Subscription companies are adding less new revenue than they did a
year ago. The surge in filings to start software companies hasn't
produced more employers. The drop is steepest at the cheap end, for
products under $50 a month. Sellers of bigger contracts should check
where their buyers' budgets went, because OpenAI and Anthropic have
more than tripled their revenue this year.

 NO SHIRT, NO SHOES, NO SERVICE FOR BOTS (5 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Amazon and United make most of their profit on extras like sponsored
listings and seat upgrades, which is why both now block AI shopping
agents that skip past the extras. Shopify takes the same cut no matter
who clicks buy, so it opened checkout to agents in September. Stores
that live on upsells can respond by raising base prices or charging
agents a fee at checkout.

 BUILDING STORY FACTORY (14 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Story Factory is a way for marketing and design to transform content
production in the AI era. The agentic creation studio turns real
front-end code and design tokens into production-grade marketing
films, 3D motion, stage visuals, decks, and social assets, in minutes,
in any language, without screen recordings. It is still in pilot. This
post explains the engineering decisions behind the tool.

 LESS TIME ON CODE, MORE TIME ON ARCHITECTURE (8 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Clay relies on AI review agents to catch routine bugs in most pull
requests, freeing engineers to focus on decisions that are risky or
hard to reverse, such as changing a data schema or deciding which
service owns a piece of logic. For complex changes, engineers discuss
the design before reviewing the code in detail. If a large pull
request arrives without that context, reviewers ask for a short design
write-up and pause the review until the team has discussed it.

 DON'T NEGLECT THE M&A PATH (13 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Most leaders are too focused on the IPO path and neglect the M&A
option until it's too late. Almost all companies that have the
privilege of an exit will do so via an acquisition. It's important to
start early to improve your chances that M&A remains an option. This
post is the first of a three-part series that covers preparing for
M&A, the M&A process, and diligence and closing.

MISCELLANEOUS

 MOST OF AI MOATS ARE JUST FUEL (12 MINUTE READ) [16]

 In the age of AI, context and know-how are losing their status as
moats, becoming more like fuel for initial growth. Moats are defined
by their ability to provide lasting protection without ongoing
resource inputs, unlike fuel, which only provides a competitive edge
when continuously added. Reinforcing loops, such as network and data
effects, can evolve into moats by reducing dependency on fuel, making
businesses harder to challenge.

 ASK CLAUDE WHICH OF YOUR SHIPPED WORK MOVED A NUMBER (2 MINUTE READ)
[17]

 Building with AI feels productive because something ships every day.
AppSumo's founder asked Claude to check the work and learned that only
4 of 67 shipped changes had moved a number. Tests with clear winners
had also sat waiting on the founder's decision. You can run the same
audit with the prompt in the post, which asks Claude what number each
change was meant to move and whether it did.
