HEADLINES & TRENDS

 SOME FINAL WORDS ON FOURSQUARE (9 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Foursquare was acquired about a month ago, marking the end of a
17-year story. The acquisition was a $0 outcome for equity holders.
This post captures the ending from a founder's point of view and
details lessons learned from starting, scaling, and losing the
company. In the end, the company was unable to survive a leadership
change, leading to employee frustration and the zero-dollar outcome
for equity holders.

 YOUR PRODUCT IS NOT THE CENTER OF THE UNIVERSE (4 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Classical traits of product-centric thinking include expecting people
to come to you to get work done, building integrations so other
products plug directly into you, asking people for context and
preferences they've already shared somewhere else, and trying to own
AI access by building a custom agent inside a product. This
product-centric view has an expiry date. Coding and personal agents
can now work across several tools, and now visiting each product one
at a time is starting to feel like friction. The products that will
thrive will put the user, along with their agent of choice, at the
center.

STRATEGIES & TACTICS

 WE STOPPED REVIEWING CODE AND STARTED VERIFYING INTENT (7 MINUTE
READ) [7]

 When AI writes most of the code, human review is best spent on what
the change should do. Scaling diff review to match AI output doesn't
work. Review intent before code is generated, and verify each
criterion with evidence. Turn recurring review comments into
invariants, and keep humans on the decisions and on ownership of the
rules. This should result in code review no longer being the
bottleneck.

 TOP AEO AUTOMATIONS YOU (OR YOUR TEAM) SHOULD BE RUNNING RIGHT NOW (7
MINUTE READ) [8]

 The companies winning at AEO right now run it as a closed loop.
They're building agents that find the questions buyers ask, test what
assistants say back, chase the citations they're missing, and fix
answers that are wrong, then test again. World-class AEO means
building closed-loop agents for everything that can be automated. This
post lays out in detail five of the best agents running in the market
right now.

 Interactive apps built in the Gemini app can now be exported directly

MISCELLANEOUS

 PIVOT! (8 MINUTE READ) [12]

 Many founders fear the word 'pivot', but if you're on a path that
isn't leading to a great outcome, you must make bold moves. Admitting
that the current strategies aren't working may be the only way
forward. A pivot is a chance to redefine a company and refocus
everyone on a winning strategy. The real risk is trying to make
incremental change in a deteriorating business.

 DON'T DO "DRIVE-BY RECRUITMENT" (2 MINUTE READ) [13]

 Low-effort cold emails from founders are a huge bear signal on the
company. Founders who send them are deeply uncalibrated about what it
takes to recruit good people. This is especially true if they go cold
immediately after a rejection. Recruiting is sales - these things are
a relationship game. Next time a founder says that it's impossible to
hire good engineers, consider that they might just be terrible at it.
