HEADLINES & TRENDS

 WHAT ON EARTH IS GOING ON WITH AI PRICING AND PACKAGING? (10 MINUTE
READ) [6]

 Lovable's growth lead says even her company's pricing is hard to
understand. Its $25 monthly plan buys 100 credits, but customers have
to start building to learn what those credits accomplish.
General-purpose AI makes this harder because two requests for the same
outcome can incur very different costs, especially when an agent
retries its work. Credits help combine those costs into a
subscription. The missing step is translating the allowance into work
a buyer recognizes, as audio tools do with minutes and scraping tools
do with pages.

 VALUATIONS NEED A CORRECTION (10 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Startup valuation reports only include companies that managed to
raise money. As fewer seed companies reach Series A, that group
becomes increasingly selective. A model correcting for this bias
estimates that new seed rounds value companies at four times the
average existing seed startup. Higher round prices can therefore
accompany a tougher fundraising market. Before using a recently funded
competitor to price your own round, compare your growth and unit
economics with theirs.

 INVESTORS TURN ON DATA CENTER BUILDERS AS RISING INTEREST RATES
DAMPEN THE AI TRADE (7 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Rising interest rates and market skepticism challenge AI
infrastructure firms like CoreWeave, which heavily rely on debt for
growth. CoreWeave's bond trading reflects distress, and its founders
selling $3.3 billion in stock since the IPO adds to investor unease.
Meanwhile, AI token spending growth plateaus as new enterprise
customer acquisition slows, amid efficiency improvements and cost
reductions.

STRATEGIES & TACTICS

 APP STORE OR WEB CHECKOUT: THE MATH AT 15% (10 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Moving a small app's checkout to the web saves less than the headline
App Store fee suggests. At Apple's 15% rate, a $9.99 subscription
leaves $8.49 in the app versus about $9.33 after Stripe card and
billing fees. With equal prices and renewals, losing roughly 9% of
buyers erases that gain before extra operating costs. One
small-business app in Adapty's review rolled back its test after
revenue per paywall viewer fell 9%. Judge a checkout experiment on
revenue per viewer through at least one renewal, using your actual
fees.

 NEGOTIATION & MULTI-TURN GAMES (5 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Negotiation works best as a collaborative process rather than a
zero-sum contest. Understanding each side's priorities and constraints
creates stronger, lasting outcomes across hiring, venture investments,
sales, and acquisitions.

 G2'S 2026 AI AGENTS REPORT: ONLY 9% OF BUYERS THINK SEAT PRICING IS
FAIR, 57% WANT USAGE-BASED, AND 74% WILL SHOP YOU AT RENEWAL (6 MINUTE
READ) [11]

 G2's 2026 AI Agents Report reveals 91% of B2B buyers find per-seat
pricing unfair, with 57% preferring usage-based models. Buyers expect
stronger agent capabilities, with 74% open to switching vendors at
renewal and 72% valuing a robust ecosystem. 46% find agent running
costs higher than anticipated, demanding real-time dashboards and
spend caps for better cost control.

MISCELLANEOUS

 HOW AGENTS UNLOCK NEW MARKETS (9 MINUTE READ) [16]

 Suppose a supplier turns down an order for 5,000 parts because it can
only ship 2,000 immediately. The buyer can wait a month for the rest,
but nobody asks. Agents representing both sides could work out a split
delivery and save the order. Walmart already uses AI to negotiate
individually with smaller suppliers. Those suppliers used to get
standard terms because the savings were too small to justify
negotiating each deal.

 WHAT'S STILL THERE IF EVERYONE HAS YOUR CODE? (11 MINUTE READ) [17]

 A competitor can copy a workflow tool without getting the network
that makes it valuable. SnapDocs began by helping offices manage
mortgage signings, then gave notaries and attorneys their own profiles
so they could work across customers on one platform. It also learned
which notaries might hold up a closing. A founding-team member says
the network and data let it charge five to ten times legacy
competitors' prices. If a rival copied your software, would customers
still pay more for the people or transaction history on your platform?


 DELTA'S BISCOFF COOKIES WERE SUPPOSED TO BE TEMPORARY. THEN
PASSENGERS FELL IN LOVE (7 MINUTE READ) [18]

 A peanut shortage gave an unfamiliar Belgian cookie access to Delta's
passengers in 1986. The airline says the replacement was supposed to
be temporary, but passengers began calling headquarters to ask where
they could buy Biscoff. Lotus answered with mail order in the 1990s,
then entered major US grocery stores through Kroger in 2006. Delta now
serves 70 million packs a year. For a product customers need to try
first, look for a business that already serves them and needs
something inexpensive to hand out, then give those customers somewhere
to reorder.
