 REPLIT'S CEO ON BUILDING A COMPANY THAT CAN RUN ITSELF (23 MINUTE
READ) [4]

 Amjad Masad started Replit in 2016 with his wife, designer Haya Odeh.
Replit began as a website where beginners could run code in a browser
without having to install anything. Today, it features an agent that
can design, write, test, deploy, and host applications from a prompt.
This post contains an excerpt from an interview with Masad where he
discusses the way AI is changing work, AI job losses, the future of
software, and more.

 THE SAAS EXTINCTION TEST (9 MINUTE READ) [5]

 The rumors of the demise of modern software are greatly exaggerated.
However, the nature of what makes software valuable is quickly
changing. SaaS companies can no longer survive by being a slightly
better UI for a human workflow. Defensibility remains at the
intersection of data and risk.

 NEW GRAD HIRING: WHY SLOPE, NOT EXPERIENCE, IS WORTH PAYING FOR (8
MINUTE READ) [6]

 Founders used to pay a premium for experience. AI made that premium
pointless, but that doesn't mean that talent got cheap. The market is
currently mispricing new grads as a discount option, when they're
actually the best deal on the table. That mispricing won't last.

 AIRTABLE'S ROAD NOT TRAVELED (13 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Airtable's no-code advantage eroded as coding agents let operators
build directly on Postgres, while organizational drift and incremental
AI features slowed adaptation. Selling the legacy business and
spinning out Hyperagent creates a clean break, preserving upside
without dragging old architecture.

 SHOULD YOUR EARLY INVESTORS ALWAYS FOLLOW ON? (5 MINUTE READ) [8]

 A seed investor can love your company and still skip the next round.
The math may leave no other choice. A fund's $100,000 at a $2 million
valuation can return 100 times at a $200 million exit. If it puts its
remaining $500,000 in at a $10 million valuation, the company needs a
$1 billion exit for that new money to do the same. The fund may prefer
five early bets. A Series A fund that invested early for access and
then passes at Series A is a different signal. Ask what earns the
second check before taking the first.

 HOW TO GROW YOUR MARKETPLACE TO 500,000 USERS (7 MINUTE READ) [9]

 PromptBase has 500,000 users and roughly 300,000 listings. It started
with no users, two listings, and a bad website. The founder became
seller number one, uploaded the first prompts himself, and found the
next sellers in Reddit threads. He messaged them after their first
sale, fixed bugs quickly, and shared their listings. Storefronts and
lower fees gave sellers a reason to bring buyers, while every prompt
became a page Google could find. The marketplace grew by hand before
it grew through sellers and search.

 THE BIG MANAGEMENT LIE: OVERPROMISING (6 MINUTE READ) [12]

 Overpromising career rewards to retain high performers can backfire,
damaging trust and causing significant personnel issues. Despite
well-intentioned optimism, unexpected circumstances often prevent
managers from fulfilling these promises. Managers should instead focus
on controllable actions, provide disclaimers, and avoid guaranteeing
outcomes to maintain credibility and trust with their teams.

 I LAUNCHED A FAKE DEODORANT BRAND TO SEE IF AI WOULD NOTICE (8 MINUTE
READ) [13]

 Someone spent $11.25 and less than an hour making up a deodorant
brand. Three weeks later, ChatGPT started mentioning it. By day 30,
the fake brand ranked first in four answers and once beat MAGS Skin, a
real product with clinical testing and a National Eczema Association
seal. No fake reviews, Reddit posts, or press. It did not take over
deodorant search. It worked for only two narrow questions about
sensitive skin. Claude, Gemini, and 13 broader searches ignored it.
Native still won 99 of 180 answers. The fake brand found two questions
the real brands had left open.
