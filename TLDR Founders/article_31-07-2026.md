HEADLINES & TRENDS

 BIG-WORLD INTUITIONS (6 MINUTE READ) [4]

 It's best to follow universally sound principles and heuristics as
they're robust to uncertainty. Behaving in a way that's usually for
the best avoids issues with misreading the situation. Most of the
time, for most people, following generally good heuristics is good.
Play fair, mind your own business, cultivate your own garden, and do
your best, and it'll mostly work out for the best in the long run.

 TUNE-UP (3 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Two-week sprints once felt radical. Bring that cadence to an
AI-native team now and you might get smoked. 37signals still runs
six-week product cycles, but it just cut the two-week cool-down
between them to a one-week "tune-up." The team says a substantial
feature that may have taken four weeks, with a real risk of six, can
now ship in one.

STRATEGIES & TACTICS

 DEAR SAASTR: HOW DO YOU STEAL CUSTOMERS FROM THE LEADER IN THE SPACE?
(3 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Show up in places where your competitors are. Most customers will buy
the top brand, but not all of them, and being known will help you if
your competitor ever makes a mistake. Help customers switch vendors
for no additional cost. Making the process effortless will relieve any
concerns about the cost of switching.

 SAAS BEST PRACTICES WE LEARNED FROM SAAS IPO'S (6 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Successful SaaS IPOs emphasize a land-and-expand model, creative
pricing tied to value, and the importance of annual or multi-year
contracts. Companies like GitLab leverage open-source and freemium
strategies to build user bases before sales involvement. For sustained
growth, SaaS firms should eye enterprise customers, maintain over 110%
net dollar retention, and align go-to-market strategies with buyer
behavior.

 DON'T HIT THE NOS TOO SOON: A FOUNDER'S GUIDE TO PR (7 MINUTE READ)
[8]

 Lester Chen advises founders to view PR not just as a distribution
channel, but as a test to gauge the external validation of their
startup's story. Engaging early with professional PR services can
prevent common mistakes, allowing founders to focus on tailored
messaging for their specific audiences. The direct approach, favored
by many now, involves founders engaging directly with their audience
through personal channels, though it requires comfort with public
engagement.

 HOW TO GET YOUR LAUNCH TRENDING ON X (FULL GUIDE) (9 MINUTE READ) [9]


 Successful X launches combine market-specific research, a memorable
claim and demo, coordinated creator waves, and rapid comment-driven
engagement. Founders then convert attention by replying, DMing
sharers, delivering lead magnets quickly, and sustaining one launch
narrative for 48 hours.

 STACKED PULL REQUESTS ARE NOW IN PUBLIC PREVIEW (3 MINUTE READ) [10]

 GitHub Stacked Pull Requests break large changes into small,
reviewable pull requests so developers can independently review and
check each pull request, then merge everything together in one click.
It helps developers introduce smaller individual changes while
shipping larger features. Stacked pull requests help teams keep large
changes moving, maintain quality across every layer, and merge one,
some, or all. The feature is built into GitHub, so existing reviews,
checks, and merge requirements will work.

 The Gemini Live API enables low-latency, real-time voice and vision
interactions with Gemini. It processes continuous streams of audio,
images, and text to deliver immediate, human-like responses. The API
creates a natural conversational experience for users. It can be used
to build real-time agents for a variety of industries.

 DOMO'S UNUSUAL $400M ACQUISITION (5 MINUTE READ) [12]

 Usually, an acquisition means selling the company. Domo is selling
almost everything that makes it Domo and keeping the public company
alive. After it broke a debt covenant, its lender gave it until July
31 to sign a deal. Progress Software is paying $400 million for the
software, customers, and IP. What remains will have about $246 million
in cash and $900 million in tax losses it can use against future
profits. Progress gets the business. Domo's management gets another
shot at buying something else.

 HOW MERCOR BUILT A $20B MARKETPLACE WITH $71 AND A GOOGLE FORM (10
MINUTE READ) [13]

 Mercor says it crossed a $2 billion annualized revenue run rate after
doubling in four months, making it one of the fastest-growing AI
companies. It's always good to see what a company like that actually
looked like in the beginning. In this case, it was a software agency
finding engineers through IIT coding clubs, Facebook ads, and
hackathons with a $71 prize pool. When clients asked to hire those
engineers, the founders ran the marketplace through WhatsApp, Google
Sheets, and PandaDoc. GPT-4 scoring and the AI interviewer came later,
once that manual process started to break.
