HEADLINES & TRENDS

 THE RISE OF THE SELF-DRIVING COMPANY (5 MINUTE READ) [4]

 Companies pay a lot to keep work moving between people, in meetings
and status reports. At Replit, agents are taking on much of that.
Designers now ship product changes themselves instead of handing ideas
to engineers, and support uses agents to look into customer problems.
Engineers wrote nearly three times as much code in the first half of
2026 while review times, rollbacks, and incidents held flat. At most
companies, agents still run in chat windows and personal accounts with
whatever logins someone handed them, and nobody can see what they did.


 PAYMENTS MOATS (8 MINUTE READ) [5]

 As AI makes software easier to reproduce, defensibility increasingly
rests in regulatory approvals, banking relationships, and accumulated
operational knowledge. Records of agent decisions and human
corrections can become an advantage that competitors cannot recreate
simply by copying the product.

STRATEGIES & TACTICS

 THE SIMPLEST, HIGHEST-ROI PLACE TO PUT AN AI AGENT IS YOUR CONTACT ME
FORM (9 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Inbound qualification is a good place to start with agent deployments
because the job is bounded. The agent just needs to answer questions,
qualify, route, and book. There are clear success metrics: meetings
booked and revenue closed. The downsides of implementing AI in inbound
qualification are low because the alternative is a form that sits in a
queue.

 BETTER AI DIDN'T CHANGE HOW I WORK - HUMANS DID (5 MINUTE READ) [7]

 One AI startup's head of product tried Claude Cowork in January, and
it couldn't do the job. A month later, a newly hired product manager
showed the head of product how he had Claude Code agents doing his
work. Watching a direct report work that far ahead did more than any
product demo. That month, the head of product built a first agent to
draft the weekly update for leadership. By April, Cowork was running
nine jobs. Keep a list of what AI can't do yet, and retry the top
items every couple of months.

 Startups can now spend their Google Cloud commitment on the full

execution, skills, and web search are included, and new features

 REPLIT MONITORING AGENT: YOUR VERY OWN SOFTWARE ENGINEERING TEAM
(TOOL) [9]

 Replit's Monitoring Agent is an on-call engineer for your published
app. It watches for errors and slow responses, investigates each
alert, tests a fix, and emails you a summary. It can also republish
the fix on its own if you let it.

 Vercel Connect CLI now lets users search over 1000 services directly
from the terminal using filters like category or connection method.
The update supports JSON formatting for scripts and agents, enhancing
integration flexibility. Users need CLI version 64.1.0 or later to
access these features.

MISCELLANEOUS

 THE FORGOTTEN INDUSTRIALIST (17 MINUTE READ) [11]

 AI founders should use George F. Johnson's “Square Deal”
framework: share productivity gains among employees, customers, and
the business. That means giving employees meaningful ownership,
passing savings on to customers, and reinvesting in growth. Lincoln
Electric offers a proof point: the company pairs profit sharing with
stock returns more than twice the S&P 500's since its 1995 IPO.

 TOKENS OVER HIRES (12 MINUTE READ) [12]

 Some startups now buy engineering in tokens instead of headcount. Two
founders spend about $28,000 a year on AI, less than one engineer
costs. In their first month, they merged 657 pull requests. Their
biggest bill is about $1,500 a month for an AI code reviewer, so
neither founder has to read every first draft. Their real limit is
attention. While one founder was reviewing other work, a small
database problem grew until it took the product down. So their next
engineer will own one piece of the product, agents included.
