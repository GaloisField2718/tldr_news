HEADLINES & TRENDS

 THE HARNESS MARGIN OPPORTUNITY (3 MINUTE READ) [8]

 The right harness can cut the cost of the same result by 71% without
a loss of accuracy. This presents an opportunity for the next
generation of software applications. State-of-the-art models can be
expensive to use, but a good harness can reduce costs enough for them
to be a viable option. An effective harness requires deep customer
understanding, a collection of relevant evals, and a factory for
automating hill-climbing.

 THE NEW KINGMAKERS (2 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Revenue scale is no longer sufficient when raising a round. What
actually matters is how many top AI-native companies use, love, and
embrace your products and profess undying loyalty to you. AI-native
companies are the hardest customers to win, so they are the leading
indicator. Revenue can be bought, but loyalty from a kingmaker can't
be manufactured.

 PULLEY, A CARTA RIVAL, IS SHUTTING DOWN (2 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Pulley raised more than $50M to compete with Carta. Now it's shutting
down and directing customers to that same competitor. The cap-table
startup closes December 8, giving customers about twelve weeks to
move. A former employee speculated that spreadsheets, increasingly
improved with AI, were a bigger competitive problem than Carta itself.
If you use Pulley, start checking the migration details now,
especially which records transfer automatically and what you'll need
to export yourself.

STRATEGIES & TACTICS

 INSIDE 12 MONTHS OF AI SEARCH EXPERIMENTS (8 MINUTE READ) [11]

 HubSpot spent twelve months testing what gets a company found in AI
search. Adding an llms.txt file did nothing, while 92% of its highly
specific industry and use-case pages eventually earned citations. A
page-speed overhaul increased AI crawler visits by 1,600%, and
qualified leads from AI search rose 1,850% over the year. The
experiments give teams somewhere concrete to start. Track whether the
content brings qualified leads, though, because more crawler visits
alone won't tell you whether it's working.

 WHY WOULD SOMEONE PAY FOR YOUR APP WHEN AI DOES IT FOR FREE? (16
MINUTE READ) [12]

 Your customer can ask ChatGPT for a lesson. Getting them to practice
again tomorrow is a different problem. This article suggests comparing
your app's main use case with what someone can do in a blank chat
window, then looking at what's missing. Does your app remember their
progress, organize what comes next, or give them someone to practice
with? Those are reasons to keep using it even when the answers are
available elsewhere. If you're struggling to explain why someone
should pay, try the comparison yourself.

 WHY DOES YOUR TEAM THINK YOU'RE A WORSE MANAGER THAN YOU DO? (4
MINUTE READ) [18]

 You suggest a new feature during a meeting. A week later, someone has
built it, and the project you actually needed is behind. You thought
you were sharing an idea, but they thought you were giving them a
task. This is one reason founders and their teams can disagree about
how well things are managed. Tell people whether you're asking for
opinions or assigning work. When you do add a task, explain how it
ranks against what they're already doing.

 HOW TO RAISE VENTURE CAPITAL CHAPTER 1: THE SPEED RUN (13 MINUTE
READ) [19]

 One investor wants an answer by Friday. Another can't meet until next
month. You can end up choosing before you know who else is interested.
This fundraising guide explains how to avoid that by building
relationships early, then scheduling your pitch meetings close
together so investors can reach decisions around the same time. Aim to
finish the raise before you're down to six months of cash. Even after
you accept a term sheet, investors still need to complete their checks
and finalize the paperwork before the money reaches your account.
