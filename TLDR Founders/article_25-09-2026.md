 WHAT IT'S LIKE TO WORK AT AN AI-NATIVE COMPANY (18 MINUTE READ) [6]

 AI-native company Lovable operates with a flat structure, minimizing
traditional titles and meetings, while fostering high autonomy and
rapid adaptation. The dynamic environment encourages employees to
automate tasks and focus on high-impact, individual contributions,
resulting in blurred roles and responsibilities. AI agents, paired
with human "agent parents," support daily operations, highlighting the
shift towards leveraging AI in company operations.

 THERE ARE NO MORE MOATS. YOU NEVER HAD ONE (12 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Companies are buying more software than ever, but there's no loyalty
anymore. They leave the moment something better ships. Migrations are
now free, and contracts are monthly. Companies need to have the best
product for the week, and then do it again next week. It's no longer
about protecting a moat, but going out on the attack.

 THE SAASTR AI GUIDE TO BUILDING A TOP-TIER INBOUND AI AGENT: 17,000
CONVERSATIONS, ~600 MEETINGS BOOKED, AND 60% MORE NEW BUSINESS (10
MINUTE READ) [9]

 SaaStr's inbound AI agent conducted 17,000 conversations and booked
600 meetings, boosting new business by 60%. Key improvements included
replacing long forms with real-time qualification and direct meeting
booking on high-intent pages. The self-serve agent enhanced this by
creating personalized, trackable documents and employing heat mapping
for deeper insights, supported by a lightweight, custom booking system
on their site.

 WHY VENTURE DUE DILIGENCE GOT WORSE FOR DEEP TECH (24 MINUTE READ)
[16]

 Venture due diligence for deep tech has worsened, driven by
deployment pressure, herding behavior, reliance on lead investors'
reputations, and the removal of independent expert review layers.
Deals in frontier tech sectors like AI and quantum often close rapidly
without thorough technical vetting, exposing investors to unverified
risks. Limited partners are advised to mandate independent technical
feasibility reviews before deep-tech investments to enhance diligence
and protect their capital.

 WHAT IT TAKES TO BUILD GREAT PRODUCTS NOW (13 MINUTE READ) [17]

 Getting there fast doesn't mean you've built something great. Teams
need to decide whether a direction is the best, whether the product
fits the team, and whether the product is worth building. The teams
answering those questions well are strong in speed, direction, and
differentiation. They'll keep moving through the final stretch, weigh
many directions before betting on one, and shape the result into
something only they would make.
