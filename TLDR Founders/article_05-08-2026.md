HEADLINES & TRENDS

 CROSBY: THE INNER WORKINGS OF A NEOFIRM (26 MINUTE READ) [4]

 Crosby is an AI native law firm. It employs lawyers from elite law
firms as well as engineers from top startups. The firm takes on
commercial contracts like Non-Disclosure Agreements and Master
Services Agreements, the basic units of doing business. This article
looks at how the firm operates and how it uses AI to complete tasks in
minutes that would otherwise take a human hours.

 TIME HAS STARTED SERVING ADS TO AI AGENTS (6 MINUTE READ) [5]

 More bots than people visit Time on most days now. So it started
selling ads to the bots. Every article gets a stripped-down markdown
twin with one sponsored FAQ telling crawlers what a brand wants them
to know. Ally Bank and the Project Management Institute are among the
first buyers. Time charges more than it does for a human ad because an
answer engine could repeat the message across thousands of answers.
The catch is that nobody knows whether models will absorb it, ignore
it, or treat the whole page as cloaking.

STRATEGIES & TACTICS

 SPEED ABOVE ALL ELSE (4 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Don't let people slow you down. Build and operate with strong
edge-case detection, great testing frameworks, pre-mortems, and
intense heads-down focus. Write down a plan, test it in your head, and
sprint. Things are often possible in insanely fast timelines.

 HOW WE BUILT A SOFTWARE FACTORY TO DRIVE ASTRO'S GITHUB ISSUE COUNT
TO ZERO (8 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Astro handed more than 200 open GitHub issues to four agents that
separately reproduce, diagnose, verify, and fix each report. The
backlog is now around 30, and the team expects to reach zero for the
first time in Astro's five-year history. The four-agent split matters
more than the agent count. No agent owns an issue from start to
finish. Each leaves evidence for the next one, GitHub labels hold the
state, and the original reporter tests a preview package before a pull
request opens.

 THE PHYSICS OF ENTERPRISE SALES (4 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Two college dropouts sold a $100,000 contract to a public company
from one cold LinkedIn message. They did not have a product yet. They
had not even decided exactly what to build. An enterprise does not buy
anything. One person with an urgent priority does. Use the first call
to test the pain and your rough solution. Use the second to get the
yes and map the path through legal and procurement. Group demos and
pilots only matter if your champion needs them.

 INSIDE UNILEVER'S 300,000 CREATOR NETWORK: THE LOGISTICS BEHIND THE
HEADLINE NUMBER (7 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Unilever activated 50,000 creators during the World Cup and reached
more than 600 million people. The hard part was not finding them. It
was vetting, contracting, briefing, and reviewing them without getting
50,000 versions of the same post. Its full network now has 300,000
creators, from big long-term partners to small local accounts that can
move around one cultural moment. AI can handle more of the paperwork.
It can also push every brand toward the same safe creators and the
same safe work. Scale fixes logistics faster than it fixes taste.

TOOLS & RESOURCES

 INTRODUCING ATLAS, YOUR AI COWORKER (7 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Atlas is an AI agent that works alongside teams in Slack. It can
answer questions, prepare workers for customer calls, summarize
information, investigate regressions, and more. Teammates can add
context, correct an answer, and continue the work together. Atlas
learns organizations' shorthand, priorities, processes, and people
from the conversations already happening in Slack.

 INTRODUCING WEB SEARCH ON AMAZON BEDROCK FOR FOUNDATION MODEL
GROUNDING (10 MINUTE READ) [11]

 Web Search is now generally available on Amazon Bedrock. The
server-side tool grounds model responses in current web knowledge. It
is backed by a web index that spans billions of documents and is
refreshed continually. Web Search on Bedrock offers zero data egress,
so users' data never leaves their AWS environments.

 CANVA IS ONE PERCENT OF THE WAY THERE (9 MINUTE READ) [12]

 One of the phrases most heard inside Canva is 'we're one percent of
the way there'. The phrase was repeated when the company was just a
few hundred people, and it continues to be repeated now that it has
five thousand employees and a quarter of a billion users. The phrase
keeps employees hungry, but there is also a lot of truth to it. When
the ambition is large and genuine, the target never stands still.

 THE ENDGAME OF VERTICAL INTEGRATION (7 MINUTE READ) [13]

 Anthropic's vertical integration, co-designing models with harnesses,
pressures agent labs to evolve beyond domain-specific harnesses. The
move aims to optimize intelligence per dollar in enterprise AI,
challenging agent labs to match margins by integrating and enhancing
their own model-harness combinations.
