 PARALLEL VS. SERIAL (3 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Serial processing is better for tasks that must be completed in a
specific order and best performed by the CPU. Parallel processing is
better for tasks that can be divided into many parts and done
simultaneously and is best performed by a GPU. Many problems benefit
from the fusion of both approaches, where the main problem is broken
into many smaller independent problems solved in parallel. The
solutions of the smaller problems can then be integrated serially to
create the complete solution.

 THE LEAD INVESTOR PROBLEM (12 MINUTE READ) [10]

 A founder can fill a calendar with investor meetings and still have
no round. "Interested," "keep us posted," and "we could participate"
sound positive, but none means an investor will set the terms, anchor
the valuation, do the deepest diligence, or give everyone else a
deadline. A lead does those jobs. Keep likely leads separate from
likely followers, then ask directly whether each fund would actually
set the terms. Ten more meetings with followers still leave the lead
column empty.

 PREVENTING CHURN IN SET-AND-FORGET PRODUCTS (8 MINUTE READ) [11]

 Backups and monitoring can be working perfectly even when nobody logs
in. One company with hundreds of thousands of customers searched its
first 30 days of human activity for something that predicted who would
still pay 12, 18, or 24 months later. It found nothing. The
integration was doing the work, so the useful signals were whether
data was still moving, volume was falling, or errors were rising. A
cancellation may arrive months after the product stopped working,
which means the real churn date happened much earlier.

 WE SPEND $45,000 ON DOING MORE WEIRD EVERY MONTH (5 MINUTE READ) [17]


 PostHog sets aside $45,000 every month for marketing ideas that may
fail. Anyone can pitch one in Slack, and a two- or three-person
"Council of Weird" scores the cost, effort, and whether people
actually want to make it. Some bets did almost nothing. Billboards got
zero comments, Easter eggs were found by about three people, and a
documentary got 2,000 views. Most startups can steal the setup for far
less money by fixing what they are willing to lose each month and
judging several experiments together.

 BUILDING A DIVERSIFIED STARTUP PORTFOLIO (5 MINUTE READ) [18]

 Diversifying startup investments by sector, stage, and geography can
mitigate risks associated with market volatility. Sector
diversification helps balance industry-specific cycles, while stage
diversification mixes potential high-reward early-stage investments
with more stable later-stage companies. Geographic diversification
protects against regional economic fluctuations, though it can be
challenging to access international deals.
