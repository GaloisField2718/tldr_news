HEADLINES & TRENDS

 COMPUTE SCARCITY IS PERMANENT. BUILD A LADDER (12 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Processors are currently scarce because of the same bottleneck: the
high-bandwidth memory and the advanced packaging that binds it to the
die are capacity-limited across the entire industry. This can't be
fixed with a bigger cloud budget or a patient retry loop. This
constraint isn't clearing anytime soon and is likely a permanent
condition. Businesses need to build for this reality rather than
waiting for the shortage to end.

 THE RISE OF THE 1-PERSON GROWTH ENGINEER (10 MINUTE READ) [9]

 No industry leader has created a program or certification for AI
fluency yet. It's difficult for individuals and hiring managers to
understand whether they're doing cutting-edge work or are part of the
status quo. Building a portfolio of proof is really important at this
stage. Do the work in public, talk about what you're building, and
collect third-party proof and outcomes wherever you can get them.

 99% OF MY WEBSITE TRAFFIC IS BOTS (27 MINUTE READ) [10]

 PatronView is a donor-research database with 1.5 million profile
pages built from public records. In one week, its server served 1.28
million pages while analytics counted only 5,977 pageviews. That is
214 invisible loads for every visible one. Claude-SearchBot crawled
35,000 pages for every visitor it sent back. Amazon crawled 117,000
pages a day and sent none. Cloudflare's bot detector added 2.875
seconds and cut mobile Lighthouse to 58. Removing it restored 99, then
an Azure scraper pulled 23,000 pages in an hour. The founder now
judges crawlers by pages taken per visitor returned.

 INSIDE AN APPLIED AI COMPANY (7 MINUTE READ) [11]

 Applied AI companies sell completed work, making the harness, not the
model, the core product: tools, integrations, persistence,
orchestration, oversight, and evals close the gap from 70% to 98%+
accuracy. Durable advantage comes from compounding knowledge,
hardening, and evaluation loops.

 HOW TO KEEP THINKING (5 MINUTE READ) [12]

 One engineer now runs six or seven AI-agent sessions on the same
coding task. Some explore, others build competing versions, and more
review and test. Things got faster, but his job slowly became skimming
and judging answers instead of forming his own. So he added one
deliberately slow habit. He reads dense nonfiction and writes about it
without an LLM. As more research, code, and analysis move into agent
queues, keep one place where you still have to start with a blank page
and decide what you think.

 THE MOAT PARADOX (5 MINUTE READ) [19]

 Building a business moat involves solving the hardest core problems,
