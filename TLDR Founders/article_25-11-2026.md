 INSIDE THE PAY-PER-JOB AI BUSINESS MODEL (18 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Startups selling AI work have to decide who pays for retries and
unusable results. An operator building an AI research product compares
charging for effort against charging for a delivered output or a
successful outcome. Candor bills per synthetic interview while buyers
think in studies of 15 to 24 interviews, so the cost unit differs from
what customers buy. True gross profit per paid job must include
retries and free trials alongside model costs and human review. Repeat
demand follows a natural cycle, so teams should wait that full window
before judging a cohort.

 AI IS A CUSTOMER REPELLENT (16 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Companies have significantly raised AI spending in customer service
without much return. People are more likely to take their problems to
third-party AI providers than to talk to company-owned chatbots. Use
AI where it makes things better without asking anyone to talk to it.

 WHY CONFUSING ADS STILL WORK (2 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Ads and landing pages can still spark interest even when readers do
not fully understand them. A growth firm reanalyzed user-test
responses and measured whether people wanted to learn more. Actual
understanding explained 3% of the variation in interest, while
relatability explained 58%. Relatability was the strongest predictor
in these tests, so audit your copy by counting sentences about your
company against sentences about the customer's situation. Then show
the message to a small group of target customers and ask who they
think it is for.

 THE AI SLOP ERA REWARDS ONE THING… (3 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Picking creators by follower count often misses real engagement in
feeds full of AI content. Cal AI evaluated more than 10,000 creators
and signed over 300. The company looked for real conversations in a
creator's comments rather than follower count or emoji replies. Cal AI
paid creators fully upfront to earn their trust. Test hooks cheaply
with AI-generated creative and then film the winning concepts with
real people before scaling.

 SAAS THAT SURVIVES (6 MINUTE READ) [16]

 There's always been a category of software that existed to only serve
the niche of 'software skill gap'. People buy software to get a job
done when the ROI on figuring out the task themselves never really
pays off. AI now makes it possible for people to build their own
solutions. While the game has certainly changed, there's still plenty
of opportunity to build software companies.
