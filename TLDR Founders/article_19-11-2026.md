HEADLINES & TRENDS

 STATE OF PRE-SEED: Q3 2026 (1 MINUTE READ) [5]

 A SAFE puts off pricing a startup, but its valuation cap can already
decide what the investor pays. Carta's latest US data shows most SAFEs
converted at their cap among companies that went on to raise a seed
round. Seed valuations usually land above the cap, so negotiate the
cap as the likely price. Median caps rose across every round size
while total pre-seed dollars grew and the number of deals fell.

 WHY ARE ALL THE INDIE-FRIENDLY PRODUCTS DYING AT THE SAME TIME? (3
MINUTE READ) [6]

 More venture-backed startups have shut down this year than in any
year on record. Many of these companies were founded or fundraised
when raising capital was easy, and couldn't fundraise again this year.
The companies that are surviving tend to be bootstrapped, profitable
tools that don't need venture capital, local-first software where
people can self-host or 'bring your own cloud', and API-first
platforms that can be used as a primitive for custom things.

 HOW FAST DO YOU REALLY HAVE TO BE GROWING TO RAISE VENTURE CAPITAL
TODAY? $1M TO $100M ARR IN 5 YEARS, MAX. BUT VCS ARE HOPING FOR 2-3
YEARS (8 MINUTE READ) [8]

 A software company can beat median growth and still struggle to raise
venture money. SaaStr's proposed benchmark is reaching $100 million in
annual recurring revenue within five years of hitting $1 million.
Lower public-market revenue multiples mean a company needs more
revenue to reach the same exit value, so investors push that
requirement back to earlier rounds. Strong retention and large
infrastructure contracts can earn a company more time under the
framework. Private equity and growth equity remain options for slower
growers.

STRATEGIES & TACTICS

 THE B2B BUYER JOURNEY WITH AI (6 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Buyers increasingly ask ChatGPT or Claude which software to buy
before they visit a vendor's site. Growth Unhinged studied about
27,000 buying prompts to see what those buyers wanted to know. Most
prompts compared specific products, while only 15% involved early
research. Pricing was the biggest single topic, including early
questions about coding agents and website builders. Check what AI
tools say about your prices and features, since a wrong answer can
drop you from the shortlist.

 A PRIMER ON AI-ENABLED ROLL-UPS (5 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Selling software to service firms leaves most of the money in those
industries out of reach. US spending on accounting services is about
34 times spending on accounting software. AI-enabled roll-ups go after
that bigger budget by buying existing firms and running them on their
own AI tools. The primer recommends using the gains to serve more
clients with the same staff. Its industry screen favors fragmented
markets with cheap acquisition prices and more demand than firms can
staff.

MISCELLANEOUS

 THERE IS ONLY ONE COMPANY TO BUILD (6 MINUTE READ) [17]

 There's a single shape/direction emerging across all of the players
in enterprise AI. Every company across the training data supply chain
is becoming an AI transformation business. The only thing enterprise
actually wants to buy is a change in their business. Founders can
charge a lot more and have an easier time selling this if they just
say they will sell the change enterprises want to make.

 B2B PR THAT GETS RESULTS WITH MICHELLE GARRETT (23 MINUTE READ) [18]

 Michelle Garrett discusses effective B2B PR strategies, emphasizing
the importance of targeted media lists and ongoing engagement over
sporadic press releases. She highlights how building relationships
with trade editors and focusing on specific industry publications can
yield better results than a broad "spray and pray" approach. Garrett
also advises incorporating visuals in PR content and tailoring case
studies to address multiple stakeholders within B2B decision-making
processes.
