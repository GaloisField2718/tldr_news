HEADLINES & TRENDS

 2026: THE STATE OF ENTERPRISE AI (18 MINUTE READ) [8]

 The labs selling you model access are also competing for your
customers' software budgets. Menlo's yearly enterprise AI report
estimates the frontier labs now capture 69% of enterprise AI
deployment spending. Agents are growing the pie, though, since new
budgets funded 55% of the extra AI spend in Menlo's survey, and the
firm reads that as companies paying for work software couldn't do
before. Startups still take 61% of application spending once the labs'
own apps are left out.

 WHO WINS THE AI APP LAYER? (5 MINUTE READ) [9]

 AI labs are now shipping products aimed right at vertical AI
customers, like OpenAI's tool for lawyers that launched a week after
Harvey raised at $15.5B. Most of the value still lands with the apps,
as long as they own data the labs can't simulate or buy. Coding took
off because the labs can grade it automatically, so the moat is
outcome data that only exists when someone does the job, like whether
a loan defaulted or a claim got approved. A quick test is to list
every outcome your product touches and capture the job byproducts as
structured data from day one.

STRATEGIES & TACTICS

 HOW TO MAKE FRIENDS BEFORE FUNDRAISING (5 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Most founders start meeting investors the week they start raising, so
every coffee turns into a pitch. A former YC partner explains how to
build those relationships months ahead so the eventual raise is fast
and competitive. The list is bigger than founders think, since more
than 4,700 Series A rounds since 2023 were led by about 2,400
different funds. One tactic is to script every intro ("we're not
raising, and I want to meet you because of the company you backed") so
the meeting doesn't accidentally start a process.

 ANALYTICS HAS CHANGED. HERE'S WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT (6 MINUTE READ)
[11]

 Software stopped having near-zero marginal costs once AI features
started showing up on the bill. Analytics has to change now that
agents both run the analysis and use the product. Agents now create
about half of PostHog's dashboards and feature flags, so the team tags
every event with whether a human or an agent did it. The same tagging
shows a third of orgs query PostHog through agents, and 1 in 5 new
orgs signs up through one.

 PRESENTING AN ANNUAL PLAN (AI-ERA EDITION) (8 MINUTE READ) [12]

 AI can make an annual plan look leaner before any of the savings show
up. The fix is to put AI spend right beside the headcount plan and
show when avoided hires or extra output will hit the numbers. Revenue
needs the same split, because usage-based AI products can carry very
different margins from the subscriptions next to them. Give the AI
cost of free trials and proofs of concept its own line too.

 THE SAAS APOCALYPSE STARTED IN OCTOBER (5 MINUTE READ) [16]

 AI-built competitors are the easy explanation for any SaaS slowdown
this fall. Stripe benchmarks covering about 181,000 subscription
businesses show 29 of 48 groups above $100,000 ARR shrinking in
October, up from four in September. Customer churn went down over the
same stretch, leading the writer to suspect weaker new sales. Only
companies above $5 million ARR were still growing in October.

 WHO OWNS THE INTELLIGENCE ECONOMY? (4 MINUTE READ) [17]

 An AI that learns from your best salesperson can keep using their
methods after they leave. Companies need to decide who owns that
expertise before model providers end up controlling it. Keep workflows
portable between providers so switching models doesn't wipe out what
the company's AI has learned.
