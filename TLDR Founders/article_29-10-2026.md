HEADLINES & TRENDS

 THE FUTURE OF THE FIRM (22 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Managing people used to be something you earned after years of grunt
work. In this post, two people at OpenAI who were running teams of
agents at 24 walk through where AI could end up on a company's org
chart. In the simplest version, nobody changes seats, so everyone just
becomes a manager (an accountant still owns the accounting while
agents do the spreadsheet work). The accounting team doesn't shrink
either, because the rest of the company is getting more productive and
needs more accounting than before.

 DATABRICKS & THE FUTURE OF THE FIRM (29 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Most companies don't need smarter AI models to unlock major
productivity gains. The bigger bottleneck is context: models lack
access to internal knowledge, unwritten processes, and the judgment
sitting in employees' heads. Databricks is building a shared system of
record that gives agents this context, alongside permissions and
feedback on their work. This could reduce management layers devoted to
moving information around, while shifting employees toward defining
tasks, delegating execution, and judging results. The lasting
competitive advantage would come from capturing what a company learns,
so its AI systems improve even when it switches models.

STRATEGIES & TACTICS

 WHAT COMES AFTER SELLING WORK? (7 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Companies spend about $6 on services for every $1 on software, so a
lot of AI startups now want to be the law firm instead of selling it
software. Selling the work itself might only be step one. Big firms
are building their own AI (Kirkland & Ellis is putting $500M into its
stack), so give the service away and make money on the deals and data
that run through it. For example, Rockwell runs a free benefits
concierge for employees at mid-market companies, so it sits right in
front of their healthcare spending.

 DESIGN THE DINNER FOR THE FOLLOW-UP (2 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Most customer dinners are planned around the night itself, and the
"great meeting you" email afterward gets ignored. One conference
dinner turned into $4M in revenue for Recall.ai. The trick was to put
a rep at every table whose only job was seating customers next to
prospects, so prospects heard about the product from people who
already trusted it. Reps then sent each prospect an intro to the
customer they sat beside ("You two were talking about X at the
dinner"). That email is much harder to ignore than a thank-you note.

TOOLS & RESOURCES

 SMART REPORTS, SURVEYS, AND POOLED GROUP BUDGETS FOR CLAUDE
ENTERPRISE (3 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Claude Smart reports surface what a team gets done with Claude and
what that work costs. Admins can now create surveys to get feedback on
how the team is working with AI. Anthropic has introduced Pool group
budgets, giving teams a shared monthly budget. Member limits are still
available as a guardrail against unusually high individual use.

 INTRODUCING THE SLACK CODE API: YOUR AGENTS, YOUR TEAM, ONE CODE
CHANNEL (6 MINUTE READ) [10]

 The Slack Code API allows agents to start code channels, add relevant
people and files, post updates as they work, and archive channels when
the work is done. It enables teams to work together with agents on
tasks with the same shared context. Users can see exactly what agents
are doing and who approved it, and workspace controls and data
boundaries are built in. Users can choose what agents and models they
want to use to suit their needs.

 AI TRAFFIC INSIGHTS NOW AVAILABLE IN VERCEL OBSERVABILITY (TOOL) [11]


 Vercel now shows how AI bots use your site, including which pages
assistants fetch when someone asks them a question and which pages
return errors to those bots. Querying the numbers from the CLI
requires Observability Plus.

 HOW I BUILT THE MOST AI-PILLED COMPANY IN THE WORLD (20 MINUTE READ)
[12]

 Spawn is a company with 10 people that spends 30 times its payroll on
AI tokens. The company ships almost as much as Anthropic, with 77,000
substantial changes in the last 30 days. Its work is done by a fleet
of permanent AI agents that each own a part of the company. Each new
model improvement has made the team faster and more coherent, and
pointed the company's velocity more in the right direction.

 THE 90% GROSS MARGIN ERA IS OVER (2 MINUTE READ) [13]

 AI companies need a new way to pitch their economics. The cost of
serving customers is much higher with AI. Companies need to separate
gross margin by customer and gross margin by pricing cohort. When
changing pricing, you ideally want to show investors that customers on
the new model have higher margins than those on the old one, and that
the gap keeps widening.
