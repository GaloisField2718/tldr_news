HEADLINES & TRENDS

 BUSINESSES ARE CHANGING AI MODEL PROVIDERS MORE OFTEN (1 MINUTE READ)
[5]

 The monthly share of firms switching providers, averaged over three
months, reached a record 8% in September. Anthropic gained significant
share in May last year following the launches of Opus 4 and Sonnet 4.
Switching declined over the next year until OpenAI introduced price
cuts and new model launches in June this year. The intense competition
between the two companies could be an early sign that AI models are
becoming commodities.

 EVERYONE IS BUILDING THE SAME THING (4 MINUTE READ) [6]

 AI startups are converging on the same agent loops, memory systems,
connectors, and sandboxes. The comparison here is to web companies in
2005, when everyone needed a database and a login page. Sharing those
parts didn't make the businesses interchangeable. The concern is that
founders are pitching basic infrastructure as their advantage while
saying little about the customer problem they've figured out. Customer
references and the team's knowledge of its industry may tell you more
than which model or memory system they've chosen.

 PLATFORMS ARE (STILL) THE BEST BUSINESSES (29 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Businesses that perform strongly in profitability, scalability, and
defensibility generate substantial profits relative to the capital
they require. They grow with limited incremental effort or additional
capital, and can sustain success over long periods. Platforms,
businesses that enable direct interactions between customers, come
closer to this Platonic ideal than any other type of business. Network
effects and asset-light cost structures allow successful platforms to
generate exceptional returns on invested capital.

STRATEGIES & TACTICS

 GTM IN GIT (9 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Put as much of your GTM operations into git so agents can reason over
them, test, and scale. Give agents the context and tools to understand
the system, investigate a problem, and help implement a change. Agents
make analysis easier to produce, so business context and tools need to
work across interfaces, with evals to check that answers remain
consistent. Every GTM operator needs reliable data, research, and an
environment to make decisions quickly. Do as much GTM operating work
as possible with coding agents, and keep the logical brain in git.

 HOW TO HIRE IN THE AGE OF AI (15 MINUTE READ) [9]

 While everyone agrees that AI requires rethinking which knowledge and
skills signal quality in a new hire, there's little consensus on what
those are. The stakes are highest for early-stage companies where new
hires matter most. This post approaches the problem by running an
experiment to see what makes the most sense for gauging a candidate's
quality.

 WHAT DO YOU DO THE WEEK A COMPETITOR RAISES MILLIONS? (6 MINUTE READ)
[10]

 A competitor raises millions, and suddenly you're considering a new
round or copying features you'd ignored last week. Before changing
either plan, look at what the money commits them to spending and how
much growth they'll need to justify it. One founder in this piece
calmed down after comparing their monthly burn with his own. Its
review of ten AI categories found the best-funded company led three,
with two already ahead before raising the most.

 INFERENCE IS THE MOST IMPORTANT MARKET IN SOFTWARE (3 MINUTE READ)
[15]

 AI inference is projected to become a $350 billion market by 2027,
doubling the size of the database market. This shift leads to higher
usage costs, reduced gross margins below typical SaaS levels, and
makes infrastructure a key component of COGS. Businesses will need to
adapt by optimizing efficiencies and could see innovation
opportunities arise from these evolving dynamics.

 WILL AGENTS HAVE NETWORK EFFECTS? (11 MINUTE READ) [16]

 If my agent can email yours, why do we need to use the same product?
That gives assistants a different problem from social networks, where
everyone benefits from joining the same service. Network effects could
still come from what agents create and share. An event website can
bring in new users, and a shared reputation system could help agents
find a trustworthy seller or a candidate who isn't publicly job
hunting. That reputation system could belong to one assistant, or
become a separate service that every assistant can access.
