STRATEGIES & TACTICS

 THE CAP TABLE YOU DIDN'T NOTICE YOU WERE BUILDING (6 MINUTE READ)
[10]

 A SAFE is a promise where an investor gives a company their money,
then when the company finally sells stock at a real price, the
investor gets shares, usually at a discount or capped at some
valuation as a reward for going first. There's no interest, due date,
or anything owed if that day never comes. For founders raising a small
amount quickly, it's a good trade. For investors, the worst-case
percentage locks the moment they sign.

 MOMENTUM IS NOT A MOAT (15 MINUTE READ) [11]

 Momentum should not be confused with a moat, which offers lasting
defensibility against competitors. Startups can leverage execution and
distribution to drive early momentum but need clear paths to
defensibility, such as data effects, scale economies, or unique
processes. With the rapid pace of AI adoption, startups must focus on
building moats early to sustain growth and withstand competitive
pressure.

 THE CONSUMERIZATION OF HEALTHCARE (13 MINUTE READ) [17]

 People in the US pay far more for far worse coverage and lower life
expectancy. However, the system is functioning exactly as it was
designed. The employer-based system started as a way for employers to
attract workers after a wage freeze in World War II. It became a
system where employers gained significant tax benefits for buying
healthcare on behalf of their employees over decades of layered rules.
Healthcare is a super-regulated market, and it is intimidating for
founders.

 Startup fundraising is rewarding elite teams with high valuations,
rapid growth, and frequent M&A, but underlying risks are mounting.
Common issues include weak gross margins, inflated contracted ARR,
customer concentration, expensive compute, thin technical
differentiation, and intense competition.
