HEADLINES & TRENDS

 A SPOONFUL OF REALITY FOR 'BENT' VALUATIONS (7 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Airtable's acquisition by Bending Spoons forced the tech community to
confront the sobering reality that software companies must grow or
die. The acquisition didn't include Airtable's Hyperagent platform,
which was carved out prior to the transaction. Bending Spoons mainly
acquired Airtable's incumbent SaaS businesses. Airtable's Hyperagent
spin-out from the legacy platform shows how success in the AI era may
require a full bend away from the past.

 NO REVENUE IS ENOUGH REVENUE (4 MINUTE READ) [9]

 If there is no growth, then great topline is meaningless, and if
topline is near zero, great growth doesn't mean much. Complex stories
drag on momentum. You've probably already lost as soon as you find
yourself justifying the size of your opportunity. Frame yourself as
simply as possible with the goal of being a financial earworm, meme,
or cognitive virus, and grow.

STRATEGIES & TACTICS

 HOW TO OPTIMIZE YOUR HOMEPAGE FOR AI TRAFFIC (15 MINUTE READ) [10]

 AI as a channel sends fewer people than search, but those people, on
average, spend more time on site. Treat the homepage as a front door
for visitors who already know what to do. AI systems cite knowledge
from deep pages, but they usually direct users to the homepage. Most
sites are handing their best new traffic to the one page still built
for a stranger.

 HOW NOTION HIT 100M+ USERS BY HIRING FANS INSTEAD OF MARKETERS (12
MINUTE READ) [11]

 Notion now has more than 100 million users and crossed $500 million
in revenue in 2025. It's always good to see what a company like that
looked like in the beginning. Notion once cut the team to two,
borrowed $150,000 from its founder's mother, and rebuilt the product
in Kyoto. One of its best early growth hires was a fan whose template
site already brought in 20,000 to 40,000 visits a month. Look for the
people already teaching, supporting, or distributing your product.
They may have started the job before you posted it.

 YOU SHOULD DEPLOY DIRECTLY TO PROD (7 MINUTE READ) [12]

 Continuous deployment reduces release risk by keeping changes small,
observable, and reversible instead of batching weeks of work. Fast CI,
strong monitoring, feature flags, automated rollback, backward
compatibility, and staged rollouts make production the safest place to
validate changes.

 I FOUND THE AMERICAN DREAM… IN CHINA (9 MINUTE READ) [16]

 Investment casting is the kind of industry most software founders
never look at. A conventional part can require a $500,000 mold, eight
months of waiting, and up to 40 hours of labor. Rangeview says it can
make a pour-ready mold in about 12 days without changing the
customer's approved design. Its founder found the market by ranking
more than 100 manufacturing processes, then picking one where old
tooling and a shrinking supplier base had become the bottleneck. The
output stayed the same. Rangeview rebuilt how it gets made.

 GAME DISCOVERY LESSONS FROM 30 YEARS (!) IN THE BIZ (10 MINUTE READ)
[17]

 In 1995, only 262 PC games shipped all year. Today, you can buy a
complete Unity game template for $30 to $100. Making games got much
cheaper. Getting anyone to notice got much harder. MobyGames lists
about 1,100 PC releases in 1998, while the first PlayStation had
roughly 1,500 Western releases across its entire life. Software and
media are moving the same way. If customers can choose from thousands
of decent products, distribution may matter more than one more
feature.
