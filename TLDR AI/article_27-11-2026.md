 OPENAI MAY BE IN COST CONTROL MODE (2 MINUTE READ) [8]

 OpenAI's Astra 6.2 and Sol 6.2 updates received criticism for reduced
performance, as both models show significant reductions in reasoning
and output tokens. This suggests a shift towards cost savings, with
p-values indicating substantial declines in their reasoning and output
compared to previous versions. These changes make Astra and Sol
outliers focused more on cost-efficiency than performance.

DEEP DIVES & ANALYSIS

 MEASURING RSI (12 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Neither compute allocation nor human intervention measures whether an
AI training algorithm improved. Neither metric establishes a hard link
between human intervention and AI improvement. Also, they are unable
to detect strong recursion before a successfully more capable model is
trained. Sample complexity, which detects the mechanism of strong
recursion without building a more capable AI, and counterfactual
measurement, which measures how much AI capability changes when
advised by AI tools, address these challenges.

 YOUR MODEL IS A DEPENDENCY (16 MINUTE READ) [11]

 The large language model (LLM) you choose to use is a dependency.
Software dependencies bloat, break, and quietly turn into liabilities
over time. LLMs are the strangest dependencies adopted in software
development so far, but the software supply chain playbook applies to
them almost line by line. Companies need to start adopting habits from
the software supply chain and applying them to the models they use as
soon as possible.

 FROM MODEL COMPRESSION TO LOOPED TRANSFORMERS (22 MINUTE READ) [12]

 Model layers are not all equally sensitive to compression. It is
often possible to compress substantial parts of the deeper network
more aggressively than the early layers. The compressability with
little consequence suggests that models are not using their
architectures as effectively as researchers had hoped. Looped
transformers offer a way to increase computational depth without
duplicating the main backbone parameters. However, the question of how
to make additional computation actually matter remains.

 PLATO'S KEY (8 MINUTE READ) [15]

 The Platonic Representation Hypothesis posits that neural networks
converge toward a shared representation of reality. Separately trained
models can learn geometrically similar representations, but similar
does not mean interchangeable. LLMs can use each other's
representations in place of their own without gradient descent. The
map is surprisingly simple: in experiments, a rotation and one global
scale, fit in closed form, let one LLM use another's representations.

 LAYERSTREAM: FASTER MOE FINE-TUNING ON FEWER OR CHEAPER GPUS (10
MINUTE READ) [16]

 Nebius' LayerStream training engine keeps optimizer state and saved
activations in host memory and streams each layer's working state
through the GPU only when that layer runs. On 16 H200 GPUs, it
finished a pass over a 25.8M-token dataset in 7.1 minutes instead of
22.4, with 3.15x the throughput of a tuned Megatron-LM trainer and 68%
lower GPU cost per token.

MISCELLANEOUS

 DO SCALE-DEPENDENT ALGORITHMS CUT AGAINST A SOFTWARE INTELLIGENCE
EXPLOSION? (13 MINUTE READ) [19]

 Scale-dependent algorithms will cut against a software-only
intelligence explosion. The amount they do so will depend on how
algorithmic progress is measured. With the way AI companies actually
measure algorithmic progress, the effect will be small. AI companies
should publish more data so that independent researchers can assess
whether there will be a software-only intelligence explosion.
