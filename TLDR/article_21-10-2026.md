SCIENCE & FUTURISTIC TECHNOLOGY

 HOW CLAUDE DESIGNED PROTEIN BINDERS AGAINST SCORPION NEUROTOXINS (16
MINUTE READ) [9]

 Anthropic asked an internal research model to design mini binders,
small proteins that attach tightly to a target, against scorpion
neurotoxins as part of its work in experimenting with how Claude can
accelerate drug discovery. 17 of the 211 designs tested met
Anthropic's criteria. Anthropic has not confirmed whether these
binders neutralize scorpion venom in a cellular environment, so it is
not claiming to have produced an antivenom. However, the study is a
promising first step towards using AI to design credible binders
against hard-to-target toxins in days, with lab confirmation within
weeks. The challenge, as always, will be turning those binders into
safe, affordable, and widely accessible drugs.

 I PAID $60 FOR TWO HUMANOIDS TO CLEAN MY HOME IN SAN FRANCISCO (8
MINUTE READ) [10]

 You can now pay for human operator-controlled robots to clean your
house in San Francisco. This post describes the experience of hiring
Tau Robotics' robots to clean a home for an hour. It details what the
experience is like, examines the economics for the user and for the
company, and tries to predict what a future with humanoids and
advanced robots doing productive work would look like.

 HOW TO USE DECISION MODELS FOR EVALS (23 MINUTE READ) [13]

 Decision models are an appealing alternative to LLM judges for
automated evals. However, whether they're the right choice depends on
the cost, performance, and latency requirements of the use case. Many
people leave the default decision threshold at 0.50 without checking
whether that's appropriate for the task, leaving a lot of performance
on the table. This post shows how to tune thresholds to get the best
results from decision models, compares cost and performance between
models, and explores how to interpret model scores and how calibration
can make them more useful.

 30 MONTHS ON THE EXPONENTIAL (IN CHARTS) (7 MINUTE READ) [17]

 AI's gains have been so large that how we work with it has to change.
Before, the work was divided between AI and humans. Now, humans summon
agents when needed, assign them work, and evaluate what comes back.
While it is the most powerful way to use AI, it carries new risks as
we lose control over what AI is doing to get those results.
