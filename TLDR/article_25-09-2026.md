BIG TECH & STARTUPS

 GOOGLE'S FIRST SUNCATCHER ORBITAL DATA CENTER TEST LAUNCHES OCTOBER 1
(3 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Project Suncatcher is a moonshot effort by Google to design orbital
AI data centers. Its first experimental satellite will launch on
October 1. Its aim will be to validate Google's vision for a
constellation of AI satellites. The satellite, about the size of a
refrigerator, has four custom TPU AI accelerators and solar panels
that supply about one kilowatt of power. It will serve as a test of
several technologies over its few months of operation.

 JEFF BEZOS' BLUE ORIGIN IS FUELED WITH $30 BILLION OF HIS FORTUNE (4
MINUTE READ) [6]

 Jeff Bezos has invested $30 billion in Blue Origin since its founding
in 2000. The commitment has helped Blue Origin become a large space
company with around 15,000 employees and an ambitious growth plan.
Blue Origin recently raised capital from outside investors for the
first time. The company expects to produce about $1.4 billion in
revenue this year, growing that number to more than $30 billion in
2030.

 WAYMO IS SCALING FAST: HERE'S WHAT THE FLEET DATA SHOWS (4 MINUTE
READ) [7]

 Waymo appears to be concentrating its efforts in just two states. 80%
of its roughly 4,000 robotaxis are in California and Texas. Waymo's
fleet has grown by nearly half in the past three weeks in Texas. The
company's remaining 800 or so vehicles are spread across cities in
other states, including Arizona and Florida. A growing number of the
vehicles in Waymo's fleet are Chinese-built minivans.

 THERE'S A NEW WAY TO BREAK RSA THAT'S FASTER THAN ANYTHING WE'VE SEEN
BEFORE (5 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Researchers have discovered a novel method that uses classical
computing to reduce the current RSA security level to an unacceptably
low threshold. The practical risk is limited, but still significant.
Widely used RSA implementations are also safe. The technology involves
signature forgery and reduces the required computing resources by
orders of magnitude.

 A NEW TREND OF CPU SHORTAGES (10 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Many companies are struggling to source CPUs. CPU spot pricing has
disappeared because there's no longer any lack of demand for CPUs.
It's now nearly impossible to get CPUs on spot instances without
long-running connections with cloud providers. Reserving specific CPUs
now needs to be done months in advance. Cloud providers will even turn
down certain reservations because they don't have enough CPUs or the
right type of CPUs.

 THINKING IN SYSTEMS, SHIPPING IN LOOPS (3 MINUTE READ) [11]

 AI has removed the typing from software engineering but left the
system design. What's left is designing the loop that decides whether
the output is right. Great system design is defined by resilience,
self-organization, and hierarchy. Resilience is AI checking its own
work, self-organization is the loop learning, and hierarchy is the
layering of skills, tools, and sandboxes into composable components
that are reused once verified to work.

 THE SPECTER OF NEURALESE (20 MINUTE READ) [13]

 Neuralese recurrence is a hypothetical technology that would allow
AIs to think dangerous thoughts without getting detected. OpenAI's new
Astra model uses a reasoning technique called recurrent depth that
makes the model's chain of thought more difficult to monitor by
allowing it to operate outside of the sequential thinking that
characterizes most reasoning models. There is an emerging taboo on
creating dangerous neuralese AIs. It is unclear whether OpenAI broke
that taboo, as a clear line has yet to be drawn.

 ZUCKERBERG'S ‘TAMAGOTCHI-LIKE' AI COULD BE META'S IPOD MOMENT (6
MINUTE READ) [14]

 Meta announced a Tamagotchi-like pendant for interacting with its
Muse personal agent on Wednesday. The device, expected to be out in
time for Christmas, features a customizable character at the center of
the screen. It can be worn with a wrist strap, carried in a pocket, or
attached to a bag or keychain with a lanyard. The devices can interact
with each other when placed near each other. Muse Charm could open up
new streams of revenue that could eclipse the company's advertising
business.
