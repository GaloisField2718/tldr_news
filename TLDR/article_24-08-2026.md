BIG TECH & STARTUPS

 APPLE'S FOLDABLE IPHONE WILL HELP BRING SOME MAGIC BACK (12 MINUTE
READ) [6]

 Apple is set to introduce its foldable iPhone at a launch event on or
around September 9. Samsung has stolen some of the thunder from the
launch by introducing a similar design, but that just reinforces that
the new form factor is a winner. Apple's device reportedly excels as a
camera viewfinder, but it lacks a telephoto camera, which could be an
issue for early adopters spending over $2,000. The design also relies
on Touch ID rather than Face ID.

 NVIDIA CUSTOMERS NOTIFIED ABOUT AI-RELATED PRICE HIKES ABOVE 15% (2
MINUTE READ) [7]

 Some of Nvidia's biggest customers have been told that the price of
the AI servers containing its flagship Vera Rubin and Grace Blackwell
chips is going up by more than 15% due to the soaring costs of memory
chips. The price hikes will go into effect early next year. The
increases will depend on the generation of Nvidia chips and the memory
configurations. Nvidia has also raised prices for its gaming-oriented
PC graphics cards.

SCIENCE & FUTURISTIC TECHNOLOGY

 CHINESE HUMANOID ROBOTS SMASH HUMAN RECORDS IN 100M SPRINT AND HIGH
JUMP AT BEIJING ROBOT GAMES (4 MINUTE READ) [8]

 The opening day of the World Humanoid Robot Games in Beijing on
Saturday saw Chinese humanoid robots break records set by humans,
including Usain Bolt's 100-meter sprint world record. More than 2,000
humanoid robots participated in the event. There were 51 events and
more than 1,000 competitions, including running, table tennis, and
soccer. While the games showcased humanoid robots' rapidly evolving
abilities, experts say it will still take time to achieve mass
real-world deployment and that these robots are mostly still used for
demonstrations, performances, and research.

 AMAZON'S TOASTER-SHAPED ROBOTAXIS ARE HITTING THE ROAD (7 MINUTE
READ) [9]

 Amazon's all-electric toaster-shaped Zoox taxis are the first
robotaxis offering rides on the roads without standard driver features
like a dashboard or pedals. They can drive in either direction and
hold up to four passengers sitting facing each other. Amazon has
started rolling out the vehicles across San Francisco and Las Vegas.
Tesla's Cybercab lacks a steering wheel and pedals, but it has not
received the same regulatory approval as Zoox to begin offering rides
to the public.

 FABLE & THE END OF THE FREE LUNCH (2 MINUTE READ) [16]

 When Moore's Law was in full effect, it didn't make sense to
ruthlessly optimize code, as a new CPU would arrive in 18 months that
would double performance. Developers only started to have to think
about parallelization, architecture, memory locality, etc, after
Moore's Law started slowing down in the mid-2000s. Prior to Fable, it
felt like a waste to spend too much time improving coding harnesses or
context strategies, as a new model would arrive at the same price or
cheaper in just a few months that would cover most of the problem.
Fable is incredible, but the cost is so high that developers are now
starting to think about what work should go where and optimizing their
harnesses to provide weaker models with sufficient context to perform
well.

 ‘MAYBE I'M PSYCHO': WHY TECH FOUNDERS AND VCS ARE SO ONLINE (11
MINUTE READ) [17]

 New news is coming out so fast that it seems crazy for a founder not
to be fully plugged in. Being online can help build a presence and
keep people as informed as possible. Posting frequently can help drive
inbound interest. Tech Twitter can provide a view of emerging
consensus, but few immediately actionable insights. It's important to
actively maintain cognitive security and cognitive hygiene, as
spending too much time online can be dangerous.
