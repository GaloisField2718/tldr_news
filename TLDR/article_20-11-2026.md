 APPLE IS WORKING ON AI AGENT FOR DEVICE CONTROL, APPS, BROWSING AND
CODING (4 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Apple is developing an autonomous AI agent that can get things done
on behalf of users similar to offerings by OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta,
and others. The company is currently seeking a senior engineering
manager for a proactive, agentic AI assistant that can write code,
browse the web, control apps, and navigate devices on a user's behalf.
Apple is working on agents that can plan and carry out multi-step
tasks across apps, browsers, command lines, code, and APIs while
coordinating specialized subagents along the way. The company's App
Store has been dominated by AI agents, so it has introduced a new
warning in the upcoming macOS 27.3 update that prompts users to
reconsider granting third-party agents permission to control their
Macs.

 GOOGLE TURNS TO BLUE ORIGIN FOR A ROCKET, BUT ITS LONG-TERM PROBLEM
REMAINS (4 MINUTE READ) [11]

 Blue Origin's New Glenn rocket will launch data center satellites for
Google in 2029. Google's Project Suncatcher program is a long-term
moonshot that aims to scale AI infrastructure in space by tapping into
solar power. Its satellites each have the computing capacity of
roughly the scale of a data center rack. The mission on New Glenn will
deploy satellites into a Sun-synchronous orbit, where they will be
able to receive nearly continuous solar energy.

 SPACECRAFT REACHES MERCURY ON SECOND-EVER MISSION TO ENTER ITS ORBIT
(4 MINUTE READ) [12]

 The BepiColombo mission is now preparing to swing into orbit around
Mercury. The joint European-Japanese space mission aims to help
scientists understand how Mercury formed so close to the Sun, why it
has a magnetic field, and what makes up the frozen ice under the
planet's permanently shadowed craters. The spacecraft will separate
into two orbiters in early December. The orbiters will maneuver to
different polar orbits around the planet, and science operations will
begin in April next year.

 THE MOST IMPORTANT NUMBER IN AI (42 MINUTE READ) [19]

 The Epoch Capabilities Index (ECI) is a measure of AI progress
developed by Epoch AI, a nonprofit research organization. It provides
a single number that summarizes how well AI models do on benchmarks.
It is designed to be continuously updatable, and researchers can use
it to look at trends going back years and forecast things for future
models. This post covers how the ECI is constructed, the challenges of
benchmark selection, whether it makes sense to talk about 'model IQ',
whether static benchmarks stay relevant as models get better, and much
more.
