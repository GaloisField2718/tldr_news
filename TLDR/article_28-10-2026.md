BIG TECH & STARTUPS

 APPLE REFRESHES THE IMAC, ENTRY-LEVEL MACBOOK PRO WITH THE M6 CHIP (2
MINUTE READ) [6]

 The iMac and the entry-level 14-inch MacBook Pro are both getting the
M6 chip. The iMac will have two variants of the M6 chip, one with a
10-core CPU and a 10-core GPU, while the other will have 12 cores for
each. The MacBook Pro will only have the 12-core option. Both
computers will have the N1 chip for wireless connectivity, which means
they will have Wi-Fi 7 and Bluetooth 6. Pre-orders will start on
October 28, and they will start shipping on November 6.

 ANTHROPIC RAISES CONCERNS (21 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Anthropic's prospectus claims the biggest risks to its business are
risks the business poses to everyone else. All other human enterprise
will be eaten by Claude, so you might as well own some of it. The
company's governance structure is exactly what you would expect for a
company worried about existential AI risks. However, it is also the
same governance structure for a startup that wants to raise a lot of
outside capital without losing control.

 SUNLIGHT AT NIGHT: US STARTUP'S DEMONSTRATION OF LIGHTING EARTH FROM
SPACE DRAWS CRITICISM (6 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Reflect Orbital is a US-based startup that plans to erect thousands
of giant mirrors in space to reflect sunlight onto the Earth during
night-time. The Federal Communications Commission has approved the
startup's proposal to launch an initial demonstration satellite next
year. The test will reflect sunlight in 3-mile-wide beams in strengths
from moonlight up to the brightness of the Sun at noon. While the
technology could be helpful for search-and-rescue operations, among
other applications, critics say that it threatens a harmful change to
the separation of night and day, with a huge number of unresolved
questions and significant potential impacts.

 VIBE GENES? NATURAL(ISH) LANGUAGE COMES TO PROTEIN, GENOME DESIGN
SOFTWARE (13 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Researchers based in China have collaborated to create a tool that
can take natural language prompts and use them to produce proteins
with specified functions. This could enable anyone to design proteins
- if they have a lab that can sort the good designs from the failures.
The model's success rate is not great, but it can still get the job
done. The tool likely won't remove the need for biologists, but it
could help accelerate research.

 AMAZON'S ANTI-TOKENMAXXING MEMO SAYS ENGINEERS SHOULD BE PICKIER
ABOUT USING AI (4 MINUTE READ) [13]

 Amazon has released a memo to staff saying that token usage and code
generation should be considered with care and judgment. The company
has not instituted caps on employees' token usage, but it has
acknowledged that metrics like token usage, the rate of adoption of AI
tools, and code changes or reviews remain deeply flawed ways of
measuring AI usage, and that they can lead to higher costs and too
much complexity. The company is now looking at how many launches it is
doing and how many successful changes it is making in production to
measure how AI is improving engineer productivity.
