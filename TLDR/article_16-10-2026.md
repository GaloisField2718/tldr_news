BIG TECH & STARTUPS

 TESLA'S NEW ROADSTER TAKES FLIGHT AFTER NEARLY A DECADE OF DELAYS (4
MINUTE READ) [5]

 Tesla unveiled its second-generation Roadster sports car on Thursday
by having the vehicle launch itself up a curved ramp into a controlled
landing. It later leapt over five other Tesla vehicles while twisting
in the air. The car is scheduled to enter production in 2028. Tesla
has yet to release a price tag. The Roadster can go from a stop to 60
miles an hour in about one second, and it has a top speed of over 230
miles an hour.

 META UNVEILS MUSE SPARK OPEN, THE NEW TOP US OPEN WEIGHTS MODEL —
AND IT RUNS ON TWO PAIRED MAC DESKTOPS (18 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Muse Spark Open is an 816-billion-parameter open-weight model that
can run on relatively affordable consumer hardware. Meta's benchmark
tests used a pair of M5 Ultra Mac Studios with an unknown memory
configuration. The model uses a mixture-of-experts design and
activates 42 billion parameters for each token. It supports a context
of up to 1 million tokens and accepts text, images, video, and audio,
but only outputs text.

SCIENCE & FUTURISTIC TECHNOLOGY

 BRINGING THE MAGIC: GOOGLE BREAKS OPEN A BOTTLENECK IN QUANTUM
COMPUTING (3 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Google Quantum AI has demonstrated that 'magic state cultivation'
works in practice on its Willow processor. Magic states help
researchers create the operations they need for going beyond classical
computing. Creating high-quality, error-corrected magic states is
incredibly resource-heavy. This post details how Google Quantum AI
created a magic state cultivation protocol that achieved a 40-fold
reduction in errors. The breakthrough is a crucial step towards
scaling up quantum computing to practical use.

 HOW TO TRAIN YOUR ROBOT (22 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Training an AI model fundamentally requires lots and lots of examples
of something being done correctly. Creating this data for training
robots is expensive, and companies need a lot of data to train
competent models. Large language models showed the benefits of scale
when training models. Researchers are now working hard to gather
enormous amounts of robot task training data in the hopes of seeing
the same sort of trends with robotic capabilities.

PROGRAMMING, DESIGN & DATA SCIENCE

 NEW TREND OF BUILDING “INTERNAL VIBE-CODING APPS” AT TECH
COMPANIES (6 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Tech companies are building internal vibe-coding tools for
non-engineers. It's not that complicated, it gets higher adoption than
vendor products, and it helps build out more AI intra expertise
in-house. Building an internal vibe-coding tool is a tiny investment
that can result in better, more custom tools than with vendor
products, and companies don't have to wait on vendors to build what's
needed. It's also a lot safer to expose internal data sources to
internal tools.

 USING ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE TO REVERSE ENGINEER HARDWARE (10 MINUTE
READ) [10]

 Developers are using Claude to debug hardware issues. AIs can read
thousands of lines of USB capture, notice sequence repeats with one
byte changing, make suggestions on what that byte means, and write the
tests to confirm its suggestions. A human is still required to decide
what to build and check that the fix runs on real hardware, but AI
takes away a lot of the slog. The post includes a link to a harness
with a small set of tools that help with reverse engineering Linux
drivers.

 HOW CODEX HAPPENED (45 MINUTE READ) [11]

 Codex started as a scrappy effort to build an AI software engineer.
The team building it had launched a version in the cloud, but it
didn't find product-market fit. OpenAI took the project and made it
the centerpiece of its comeback. This post tells the inside story of
how Codex was created.

 COREWEAVE CREDIT DEFAULT SWAPS HIT DISTRESS LEVELS AS INVESTORS SOUR
ON AI DEBT (4 MINUTE READ) [12]

 CoreWeave has taken on more than $51 billion of debt and obligations
to finance its AI infrastructure buildout. Stock and bond investors
are now showing signs of being concerned about AI overspending and
debt levels. Yields on CoreWeave debt continue to climb. The spread on
its five-year credit default swaps has soared. No one is questioning
whether demand for CoreWeave's services is real, but the cash flow
needs to arrive to justify all of the capex spending.
