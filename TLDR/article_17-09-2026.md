 APPLE REPORTEDLY BUILDING SERVER PACKED WITH M-SERIES ULTRA CHIPS FOR
AI (2 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Apple is working on an AI server powered by its M-series Ultra chips.
It is expected to be released in 2029. The enterprise server will come
with either two or four M8 Ultra chips. The project, which received
support from John Ternus when it began a year ago, back when Ternus
led Apple's hardware engineering efforts, will be Apple's first server
to hit the market in nearly two decades.

 MICE WITH HUMAN BRAIN CELLS OFFER A TOOL TO STUDY DISEASE. ETHICISTS
ASK: WHAT'S NEXT? (7 MINUTE READ) [11]

 Researchers have developed a technique for installing millions of
human brain cells into mice. The scientists bred mice that were
missing most of their cerebral cortexes and then replaced a large part
of those missing mouse cells with lab-grown human brain cells. This
resulted in mice with human neurons that could be altered to
approximate human brain conditions. The technique could potentially
create better scientific models for studying neurodegenerative
disease.

 INTRODUCING HOME MCP: ENABLING YOUR AGENT TO INTERACT WITH YOUR HOME
(2 MINUTE READ) [15]

 Google has opened early access to Model Context Protocol for Google
Home in English for Google Home Premium Advanced users in the US. Any
agent that supports calling MCP tools can now interact with all of the
devices and event history in the Google Home ecosystem. The MCP allows
agents to analyze and answer questions about home activity, summarize
camera footage across rooms, manage devices, and create smart home
dashboards using everyday language. It contains safety protections,
such as prohibiting sensitive actions like unlocking doors, but
depending on the agent, using the MCP can result in unexpected or even
undesired behavior.

 TRAINING A 4B MODEL TO PRODUCE 81% FASTER QUERY PLANS THAN POSTGRES
(66 MINUTE READ) [16]

 Query optimizers continue to leave much to be desired despite an
enormous body of research. A Postgres database should know everything
about the stuff that lives in its tables, but the problem is
enormously hard. What's not as hard is verifying whether a query plan
an optimizer picks is good or not. Language models are particularly
good at learning how to do tasks with easily verifiable outputs. A
small, open-weights model can be post-trained via supervised
fine-tuning and agentic reinforcement learning to produce Postgres
query plans that beat Postgres' default plans.
