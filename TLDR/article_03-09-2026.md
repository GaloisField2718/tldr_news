 META RELEASES MORE POWERFUL AI MODEL, EDGING CLOSER TO RIVALS (3
MINUTE READ) [8]

 Meta has released a new model with capabilities close to top
competitors. Developers can now access and pay for Muse Spark 1.3. The
model will soon roll out to Meta's social media platforms. Meta has
yet to decide whether to release the model's weights. It still plans
to release weights for the prior version, Muse Spark 1.2.

 GOOGLE RELEASES GEMINI 3.8 FLASH, ITS THIRD FLASH MODEL IN SIX WEEKS
(3 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Google has released its third Flash model in just six weeks in two
variations. The standard model is good for agentic tasks and software
development, while the Cyber model has been tuned for vulnerability
detection and mitigation. The Cyber model is currently limited to
trusted testers and governments. API access to the model is available
at an introductory rate of $0.75 per million input tokens and $3.75
per million output tokens through until the end of the year.

SCIENCE & FUTURISTIC TECHNOLOGY

 THE RACE TO ENGINEER NEW KNOBS FOR THE HUMAN BRAIN (17 MINUTE READ)
[10]

 Chemogenetics is a powerful technique that modifies specific neurons
so they can be controlled remotely by normally inert drugs. There are
several ongoing clinical trials of chemogenetics in China. The
research could result in new ways of treating epilepsy, Parkinson's
disease, and pain. This post looks at chemogenetics, describes what
those first clinical trials are doing, and looks at how they could be
improved upon.

 ROBOTAXIS TRY THEIR LUCK ON LONDON'S MEDIEVAL ROADS (3 MINUTE READ)
[11]

 The arrival of 15 robotaxis in London is an important step for the
autonomous vehicle industry. Acceptance of the technology has been
slower in European cities, where the driving environment can be
complex, and regulation is stiff. The failure rate of a driver's
license test in London is just over 50%. 15 cars will be available for
ride-hailing through Uber's app during the safety testing period.

 HOW WE MAKE AI CODING MORE COST EFFICIENT WITHOUT SACRIFICING TASK
QUALITY (14 MINUTE READ) [15]

 Token count alone isn't a meaningful measure of efficiency. The goal
should be to use the right amount of context to move a task forward.
Concise tool responses can sometimes require additional calls or work
if they leave out information agents need. This ultimately makes tasks
slower and more expensive, which is why it is important to optimize
for the outcome rather than the tool call.

 AI PRODUCTIVITY DOESN'T MEAN WHAT I THINK IT MEANS (5 MINUTE READ)
[16]

 One-shot prompt templates often fail. The current best working
architecture is a closed-loop taste flywheel. AI raises the ceiling of
what the same effort produces. Users should push for the
higher-quality output AI now makes possible rather than just accepting
the pre-AI baseline.

 IN A BIG WIN, GOOGLE AVOIDS A BREAKUP OF ITS AD TECH BUSINESS (3
MINUTE READ) [18]

 A federal judge has ruled that Google must make changes to its
advertising business but will not have to break it up. The ruling will
ensure that Google's power over the internet will remain largely
unchanged. Google has been ordered to change its business practices to
benefit competitors, but no specific instructions have been provided.
The ruling is another sign that the federal government's attempts to
rein in the power of big tech have faltered.

 HOW TO PROTECT YOURSELF FROM WORKSLOP (3 MINUTE READ) [19]

 Workslop is when your co-workers or managers communicate via big
chunks of copy-pasted AI-generated text. The core problem with
workslop is that the effort involved is asymmetrical. It costs almost
no effort to produce slop, but it still costs effort to read. This
post looks at different ways to handle this workslop so that you don't
waste ten minutes of effort for every ten seconds of theirs.
