 TECH WORLD'S CALLS FOR JENSEN HUANG TO RETURN TO NVIDIA ARE GROWING
(7 MINUTE READ) [8]

 An online petition calling for Jensen Huang's reinstatement as CEO of
Nvidia has received more than 160,000 signatures. It has reportedly
attracted support from Tesla CEO Elon Musk, Epic Games CEO Tim
Sweeney, and Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan. Huang is currently under
investigation into how his actions may have damaged America's AI
dominance, the national security implications, and his close
relationship with President Trump and his Cabinet. Huang has discussed
the possibility of returning, but the investigations surrounding him
will likely prevent that from happening.

 APPLE BETS ON SIRI AI APPS AS THE MAIN DRAW FOR ITS SMART GLASSES (14
MINUTE READ) [9]

 Apple rolled out its new Siri AI earlier as it needs software makers
to begin preparing for its smart glasses, which it plans to ship in
2027. The company is counting on integration with apps people already
use on their iPhones as a key differentiator for the glasses. Its
large developer ecosystem could give it an advantage over Meta and
Google's offerings as it potentially provides countless third-party
integrations on launch. The hardware for the glasses won't be
dramatically different from rival offerings, which makes it more
crucial for Apple to nail the developer ecosystem.

 SPACEX CATCHES STARSHIP IN MIDAIR AT TEXAS SITE AFTER SPACE TRIP (4

 SpaceX successfully caught Starship using the vehicle's launch

captured both sections of the launch system during different missions.

 ONE PROTON AWAY (6 MINUTE READ) [12]

 Marathon Fusion, a San Francisco-based fusion startup, claims it has
created gold from mercury. The startup has not published how much gold
it made, and the process requires a fusion plant that does not exist
yet. Many fields currently rely on isotopes from aging reactors.
Marathon Fusion's process could unlock a world of abundant isotopes,
opening up many markets and possibilities.

 MAKING A PROGRAMMING LANGUAGE (7 MINUTE READ) [15]

 A compiler is a line of small programs. Each turns one shape into the
next. The programs transform text into tokens and tokens into a tree,
then the tree gets checked, and either an interpreter runs it or it
becomes a native binary. No single stage is hard - the work is in
making errors useful and keeping two backends in agreement.

 Always-on agents run indefinitely, managing context across sessions
and taking action without direct supervision. They have surged in
popularity lately, largely due to recent model advancements that have
made them more practical to run. Models now have better judgment and
are more reliable, and intelligence is becoming cheaper. This post
breaks down the primitives behind always-on agents and walks through
how they fit together.

 AI, WORK FROM HOME, AND THE YOUNG-GRADUATE JOB MARKET (15 MINUTE
READ) [19]

 New college graduates are employed less than a few years ago. While
AI may be a factor, there's growing evidence that work from home is an
important factor moving the needle on young-graduate employment. AI
exposure and teleworkability are highly correlated with each other,
making it hard to distinguish definitively between the two factors.
The full extent of AI's effects on the labor market might not manifest
until the next recession.

 THE AVALANCHE THAT HIT MATH IS COMING FOR AI DEVELOPMENT (50 MINUTE
READ) [20]

 AI's capabilities in mathematics exploded quickly. Current AIs can
now complete well-scoped problems in mathematics easily. This ability
to solve well-scoped problems is going to be an even bigger deal for
machine learning research than for mathematics. AI labs mainly care
about how any new algorithmic recipe empirically makes training more
efficient or elicits smarter models - they don't care about theory for
its own sake.
