BIG TECH & STARTUPS

 SPACEX GETS APPROVAL TO LAUNCH ONE MILLION SATELLITES FOR ORBITAL
DATA CENTERS (4 MINUTE READ) [8]

 The FCC has granted SpaceX approval to launch a million data centers
into space. Anthropic and Google have already committed to paying
billions of dollars in fees to rent capacity from the network.
Proponents of orbital data centers say offloading advanced computing
to orbit will allow developers of AI tools to avoid the headaches they
are facing on Earth. There are still numerous hurdles to overcome for
SpaceX to deploy data centers in space. The FCC authorization gives
SpaceX until 2033 to launch and operate the first satellite and until
2040 to launch the full constellation.

 NEURALINK RECEIVES FDA APPROVAL FOR 'PIVOTAL' BRAIN IMPLANT TRIAL IN
ALS PATIENTS (2 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Neuralink has received permission from the FDA to launch a trial for
its LINK brain-computer interface. The study will be open to a limited
number of patients with ALS in the US. It will evaluate patients'
ability to control digital devices through thought and assess the
system's long-term safety and reliability. If the technology proves to
be safe, the trial may allow Neuralink to apply for more advanced
trials and eventually release LINK onto the market.

 SPACEX IS NOT A MONOPOLY, TECH INCUMBENTS OBVIOUSLY LACK URGENCY, AND
OTHER HARSH TRUTHS (49 MINUTE READ) [10]

 The US space industry is generally unhealthy. The legacy industry
lacks vigor. SpaceX's announced intention to gracefully sunset Falcon
three years from now has induced a deep, spiraling panic among
industry insiders who understand that there is no alternative. The
company is uniquely positioned to put compute in space, and it has
been investing heavily in this direction for more than a decade. Other
tech giants have largely run out of ideas to invest for future growth.
Due to this lack of vision, some have resorted to dividends and stock
buybacks.

 THE SHORT AGE OF DRAGON RIDERS (10 MINUTE READ) [11]

 AI models available to the public have passed a threshold in August
this year. They are now stronger than most theoretical physicists at
most technical tasks. Models are now able to carry out complex
analytical calculations, work out lengthy proofs, and turn well-posed
problems into code with minimal input. There will still be joy in
discoveries even if it is not only humans that push them.

 LATE MID-GAME SOFTWARE ENGINEERING (7 MINUTE READ) [13]

 There is a growing frontier of work that can be fully black-box
automated. There is still some work that seems resistant to being
automated, but once you have a reasonably verifiable target, then the
work to get to that target largely falls to agent
teams/swarms/factories. Agents' ability to do problem selection,
intent, and taste is improving. This means a continuous rediscovery of
what pieces require human input, and what can be fully given over, is
required.

 Lumping AI-using engineers in with the executives hollowing out their

 THE BUSINESS OF ANTHROPIC (5 MINUTE READ) [16]

 Anthropic's business is incredibly simple. It points tens of billions
of dollars of compute toward persistently shifting goalposts to sell
frontier lead time (and performance). Anthropic is best understood as
a leveraged utility with an R&D department. It can maintain margins
while under pressure from near-frontier models because of an
increasing demand for frontier intelligence.
