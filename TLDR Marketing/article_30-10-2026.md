 GOOGLE AI MODE SENDS 57% OF PRODUCT LINKS TO GOOGLE SHOPPING (2
MINUTE READ) [7]

 Google's AI search experience keeps shoppers within its own
ecosystem, especially for fashion and beauty products. A study of
55,198 AI answers found that 57% of product links led to Google
Shopping and 46.5% of US product answers linked only to Google pages.
Brands may gain visibility in AI search without receiving website
traffic.

 WHAT MARKETERS SHOULD KNOW ABOUT THE LEGAL RISKS OF ALGORITHMIC
PRICING (5 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Dynamic pricing, where every customer sees the same demand-driven
price, is relatively safe, but pricing that changes based on the
shopper carries much greater legal risk. A promo code sent to one
segment or a retargeted offer that undercuts the public price can
count as personalized pricing. Dozens of bills are pending across more
than 20 states, and the FTC has drafted a policy statement on when the
practice is misleading or unfair. False-advertising laws also apply,
since a sale price only some shoppers see may misrepresent the
discount.

STRATEGIES & TACTICS

 DRIVE MORE APP DOWNLOADS FOR $0 (1 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Turn high-engagement UGC comments into app downloads by replying to
questions about problems your app solves. Read the comment on camera,
answer it, show the relevant app feature, then tell viewers where to
download it. One family meal-planning app used this tactic to generate
100K+ views on a reply to a post with 8M+ views and 16K comments.

 INSTAGRAM JUST LAUNCHED LONG-FORM VIDEO…BUT I DON'T THINK BRANDS
SHOULD USE IT (4 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Instagram's new long-form video format isn't a priority for brands
that haven't mastered short-form content. Brands should first create
Reels over 60 seconds that earn strong watch time and engagement, then
turn successful formats into consistent series that build an audience.
Long-form is worth exploring only when short videos can persuade
viewers to watch more. Create content that builds brand influence and
drives action, not just views.

 HOW TO IMPLEMENT & MEASURE VIRAL LOOPS: STORIES FROM SAAS GROWTH
EXPERTS (14 MINUTE READ) [11]

 The most sustainable viral loops in SaaS come from invites the
product already requires, not from asking users to refer someone. A QR
code generator labels every free code so each real-world scan exposes
the brand, while an assessment tool reaches new users whenever a
recruiter invites a candidate. K-factor sums every loop into one
growth rate, and the useful inputs are invite-accept rate and invitee
activation rather than opens or clicks. Gating rewards on activation
and fixing activation first keep the loop fueled, since users who
never reach value have nothing to share.

 AS SEARCH TRAFFIC DRIES UP, REDDIT ANNOUNCES IT'S SHIFTING TO A
LOGGED-IN EXPERIENCE (3 MINUTE READ) [14]

 Reddit is shifting toward a primarily logged-in experience as
AI-powered search reduces traffic from Google and other search
engines. The move aims to limit AI scraping and encourage
data-licensing deals while giving Reddit more user data to personalize
feeds and target ads. Search referrals fell by more than 1M per week
in October, while daily active users in the US dropped 2% year over
year to 50.7M. Reddit will test the change in select markets outside
the US in Q4.

 HOW BRANDS MAKE BORING INDUSTRIES DESIRABLE (5 MINUTE READ) [15]

 Categories where shoppers once bought whatever was cheapest or on the
shelf leave more room for a brand to change how the whole category
feels. Jolie positions its filtered showerhead against skincare rather
than plumbing, while Scrub Daddy's smiling face doubles as the
sponge's grip and its social personality. Making a commodity feel
premium, designing it to be left out, or turning its use into a ritual
gives buyers a reason to care which brand they pick in a category
where that choice barely mattered.

 THE HORROR MOVIE MARKETING HALL OF FAME (22 MINUTE READ) [16]

 Horror campaigns have to hook audiences without giving the scare
away, usually on small budgets. The Blair Witch Project and Longlegs
built worlds with fake websites and real-world touchpoints that turned
curiosity into word of mouth. Backrooms tapped a fandom that already
existed online rather than starting from scratch. Smile planted actors
with creepy grins at sports broadcasts and earned about 170 million
trackable impressions without buying airtime. Obsession made its
villain feel real with a text-me billboard and her own Letterboxd
account, turning a $750K budget into $522M worldwide.
