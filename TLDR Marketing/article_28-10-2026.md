NEWS & TRENDS

 GROWING SHARE OF AMERICANS SAY SOCIAL MEDIA IS AN IMPORTANT NEWS
SOURCE FOR THEM (2 MINUTE READ) [7]

 58% of US adults say social media is important for keeping up with
the news, up from 43% in 2022. Among adults ages 18-29, that figure
rises to 76%, including 22% who say social media is their most
important news source. 41% of adults 65 and older feel the same.

 DO HOLIDAY OFFERS BRING SUBSCRIPTION APPS MORE THAN A SALES SPIKE?
(10 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Subscription apps running Black Friday promotions get 20% more new
paying customers per day than their autumn baseline, while apps
without offers get 16% fewer. Each discounted buyer is worth 8% less
over six months, but the extra volume leaves sale-day revenue 12%
higher. About half the lift comes from free users who installed the
app more than three months earlier. Christmas-only offers show no
advantage, while New Year promotions drive 14% more new paying
customers, led by fitness and gaming apps.

STRATEGIES & TACTICS

 HOW TO TURN WEBINARS INTO PIPELINE BEFORE YOU EVER GO LIVE (4 MINUTE
READ) [9]

 After someone registers for a webinar, use the thank-you page to ask
5-6 questions about their role, challenges, and needs. This turns
registration into a lead qualification flow before the event even
happens. Use the answers to personalize follow-up offers and let
high-intent prospects book demos immediately. About 90% of registrants
complete the extra questions.

 ARE YOU TRACKING LINKEDIN TRAFFIC? (2 MINUTE READ) [10]

 George Terry, co-founder of Winbox, tracked website visits from
organic posts, his personal profile, the company page, and paid ads
using separate UTMs. 80% of trackable LinkedIn form submissions came
through his profile and the company page. Company page visitors spent
longer on site and viewed more pages, while organic post clicks kept
visitors engaged 12X longer than sponsored post clicks. He notes that
paid activity may also drive people to profiles and company pages, and
some visits go untracked as direct traffic.

 WATCH OUT, YOUTUBE - INSTAGRAM GOES FROM SHORT-FORM TO 90-MINUTE
VIDEOS (3 MINUTE READ) [13]

 Instagram is testing “Features”, allowing creators to upload
videos up to 90 minutes long as it looks to compete with YouTube and
keep audiences on its platform. It's also testing tools that turn long
videos into Reels that link to the full version. This could create new
ways for creators to earn money through ads and fan payments.

 HOW TO WORK BETTER WITH AI ON YOUR TEAM (2 MINUTE READ) [14]

 A study found that people working with AI and a colleague produced
higher-quality solutions than people working alone. Participants
performed 10.2% better when they worked with both AI and a colleague,
compared with 9.6% better with AI alone and 6.3% better with a
colleague alone. They were also roughly 3x more likely to rank among
the top performers according to expert evaluations. The benefits
disappeared when teammates had the same skills, suggesting that
complementary expertise helps people and AI work together more
effectively.

 OPENAI'S STRONGEST CHANNEL FOR SELLING CHATGPT TO BUSINESSES IS
IN-PERSON EVENTS (3 MINUTE READ) [15]

 OpenAI has more product awareness than any company in its category,
and yet it still relies on getting buyers into a room. In-person
events are its strongest channel for selling ChatGPT to businesses
because enterprise purchases require buy-in from multiple executives.
Product awareness alone doesn't address concerns about data security,
costs, employee training, and deployment across large organizations.
OpenAI also uses a fixed marketing calendar that incorporates new
product launches into recurring activities, including about two
webinars per month, rather than building a separate campaign for each
release.

 WHY BELLIWELLI'S CO-FOUNDER AND CEO IS FILMING SKITS IN WALMART AT
NIGHT (3 MINUTE READ) [16]

 BelliWelli's CEO treats short-form production as core to her job
because the skits drive so much of the brand's retail traffic. The
improvised Walmart scenes, staged with about 65 rotating actors, show
shoppers fighting over the fiber supplement so viewers wonder why it's
in such demand. The videos run organically, not as ads, and the brand
leans into cringe because it gets noticed. Filming in stores also
gives Wilson regular conversations with shoppers about what they reach
for in the category.
