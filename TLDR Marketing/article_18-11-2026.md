NEWS & TRENDS

 HOLIDAY ADS ARE STARTING EARLIER. SHOPPERS MAY NOT BE. (1 MINUTE
READ) [6]

 Retailers are increasing holiday ad spending earlier in the year, but
consumers are not necessarily starting their shopping earlier. Nielsen
found six times more holiday advertising in September than five years
ago, while historical data shows that holiday ads on connected TV can
generate up to 3.2 times the sales lift of non-holiday ads. The
earlier push gives brands more time to build awareness and
consideration, but it also risks ad fatigue before shoppers are ready
to buy. An earlier advertising schedule does not mean an earlier
customer journey, so brands need to build interest before introducing
timely offers that give consumers a reason to purchase.

 WHY SOME SHOPPERS SKIP COUPONS—EVEN WHEN THEY OFFER A DEAL (2
MINUTE READ) [7]

 Consumers who believe businesses use discounts for self-serving
reasons are less likely to redeem coupons. Research found that
shoppers with higher power distance beliefs often question whether a
coupon represents genuine savings or signals inflated prices or
product problems. You can improve coupon redemption by clearly
explaining the offer, addressing skepticism about pricing, and
offering price-matching guarantees. The research also found that
coupon campaigns may perform better in regions with lower power
distance beliefs, making cultural attitudes a useful factor in
audience targeting.

STRATEGIES & TACTICS

 SPEND 80% ON SEO AND 20% ON AI SEARCH (1 MINUTE READ) [8]

 SEO budgets shouldn't shift to AI search based on visibility metrics
alone. One founder's data showed Google organic search generated 146
qualified leads in 6 months compared to just 5 from AI search. The
recommended approach is to allocate roughly 80% of resources to SEO
fundamentals and 20% to AI search experiments. Track results and
prioritize the AI platforms your actual buyers use.

 JUST ADMIT “I DON'T KNOW” (2 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Admitting when you don't know an answer can increase customer trust
and purchase intent more than giving a vague response. A study found
that vague answers made customers up to 16% less likely to buy than
answers that acknowledged uncertainty and promised to find the
information. This effect was strongest when customers suspected
salespeople had a personal financial incentive, such as earning
commission. Explain how you'll get the answer rather than leaving
customers with uncertainty, since a clear and honest response makes
you seem more trustworthy and collaborative.

 YOU CAN NOW ADD AMAZON AFFILIATE LINKS TO YOUR INSTAGRAM REELS (3
MINUTE READ) [11]

 Instagram is expanding its shopping features by letting eligible US
creators add Amazon affiliate links directly to Reels and feed posts.
Creators earn commissions on qualifying purchases without Instagram
taking a cut. Product links also work in carousels and support
affiliate platforms like LTK and ShopMy. Creators can now turn entire
product collections and gift guides into shoppable content using a
single link.

 HOW GOOGLE REDIRECT URLS CAN DISTORT A RANK-TRACKING EXPORT (5 MINUTE
READ) [12]

 Google rank-tracking tools can record a result's position without
identifying the actual page it links to when Google redirect URLs are
not properly identified. In one November 2026 snapshot, 63 of 65
organic results contained these redirects, making URL comparisons
unreliable because different redirect links can lead to the same page.
Check where each link leads before analyzing keyword overlap, page
rankings, or site visibility. Keep the original URLs, provider, and
collection date so your SEO reporting remains accurate and easy to
verify.

 HOW (& HOW NOT) TO USE AI FOR KEYWORD RESEARCH (17 MINUTE READ) [13]

 AI is most useful for keyword research when it analyzes data a
business already has rather than generating keyword lists from
scratch. Customer emails and call notes can be turned into search
phrases, with the customer's original wording kept separate from AI
suggestions. A short brief listing services, location, and what the
business doesn't offer stops the model from suggesting irrelevant
keywords. Search Console exports and PPC search terms reports can then
be sorted into page assignments and negative keyword candidates, with
every figure checked in the source platform.

 SAME QUESTION, 1,000 TIMES (2 MINUTE READ) [14]

 A test comparing 1,000 ChatGPT responses found that model updates can
dramatically change which businesses get recommended, even when
nothing about the businesses changes. One model favored a single
directory in 82% of answers, while the newer model cross-checked
multiple sources, including licensing records and business websites.
The findings raise questions about the reliability of one-time
visibility checks.

 HOW I BROKE THROUGH MY AI PLATEAU (5 MINUTE READ) [15]

 After hitting an AI plateau, the author found new momentum by
building a personal app to surface overlooked photography and creating
a shared “marketing brain” in GitHub with Claude. The experience
reinforced the value of solving personal problems with AI and
exchanging practical learnings with peers. Rather than treating AI as
a fixed set of workflows, the approach is to keep experimenting while
protecting skills, like writing and critical thinking, that are worth
developing independently.

 WHAT WE LEARNED BY WATCHING CLAUDE READ ZAPIER.COM (5 MINUTE READ)
[16]

 Zapier built an experimental tool to test how Claude interprets
website content. When testing its Slack integration page, the team
discovered that Claude wasn't reading the modified webpage directly.
Instead, it retrieved a Markdown version and passed the content
through another AI model before the main model processed it. This
means changes to a webpage's HTML may not affect what AI agents
actually see. Testing how AI reads Markdown content is just as
important.
