NEWS & TRENDS

 META'S TIKTOK AD BAN TURNS PLATFORM RIVALRY INTO A TRAFFIC WAR (2
MINUTE READ) [8]

 Brands that find creators on TikTok and then use Meta's targeting to
reach a wider audience may lose that route, since Meta reportedly now
rejects third-party ads that send people to TikTok and other ByteDance
properties. The ban took effect October 8 in seven markets, including
the US and Canada, and also covers ByteDance's own ad buying on
Facebook and Instagram. Campaigns that run across both platforms may
now need Meta-native destinations, neutral websites, or separate
creative and measurement for each platform.

 YOUR ALGORITHM, YOUR WORDS: NEW WAYS TO SHAPE WHAT YOU SEE ON
FACEBOOK (2 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Facebook is using AI to let users shape Feed and Reels
recommendations by describing their interests in their own words. Its
new “Your” algorithm tool lets users add and manage topics, while
expanded feedback options let them explain why they want more or less
of certain content. Large language models combine this direct feedback
with existing engagement signals to update recommendations in real
time, including during the same session.

STRATEGIES & TACTICS

 HOW TO SPOT AND FIX CRAPPY CASE STUDIES (3 MINUTE READ) [10]

 B2B case studies often underperform because they document completed
projects instead of telling compelling customer stories. Audit your
last 3 by checking whether they explain the customer's original
problem. If the stories feel interchangeable, interview a customer
stakeholder for 30 mins to uncover what went wrong and what improved.
3-5 distinctive case studies can be more persuasive than 20 generic
ones. Use anonymous stories when needed and repurpose them across
newsletters and social media.

 SHOW AI INFLUENCERS WITH REAL PEOPLE (2 MINUTE READ) [11]

 AI influencers are seen as less trustworthy because they can't
actually use the products they promote. Showing them in everyday
moments with real people, like grabbing coffee or going for a run,
helps close that gap. In a study of 9,500+ posts and 4 experiments,
these scenes drove 20.7% more engagement and made people up to 17.5%
more likely to choose the product.

 SHOPPING IS PART OF THE PRODUCT (7 MINUTE READ) [15]

 AI is changing e-commerce discovery by presenting shoppers with
product shortlists instead of sending them to search results. A study
on AI Mode found that 74% of participants chose the top-ranked
product, while only 10% chose a product ranked third or lower. The
author outlines a five-stage organic growth loop: earn AI
recommendations through product feeds and third-party signals, convert
visitors with tools such as quizzes, encourage user-generated content
through packaging and product experiences, generate repeat purchases
through customer relationships, and build brand recall. Shopping
infrastructure and marketing automation are increasingly table stakes,
while customer relationships, creator partnerships, community,
first-party data, and brand recall create longer-term advantages.

 WHAT POKEMON POKOPIA TAUGHT ME ABOUT THE FUTURE OF MARKETING (5
MINUTE READ) [16]

 Creativity comes from experimenting and adapting rather than simply
generating a finished result. As AI makes content production faster,
marketers risk losing the problem-solving and creative judgment that
come from hands-on work. A study in 2024 found AI-assisted stories
were rated as more creative but also more similar to one another. To
stand out, teams should automate repetitive tasks while protecting
time for play and experimentation to develop distinctive taste.

 WHAT DOESN'T GET CHEAPER (4 MINUTE READ) [17]

 AI is making websites cheap enough to build for short-term
experiments and one-off events that previously weren't worth the cost.
However, enterprise websites still require complex integrations. AI
can automate production and support decision-making but cannot
eliminate the human effort needed to align stakeholders. Generative
tools also create collaboration challenges when multiple companies
need to edit the same work. As production costs fall, experimentation
will expand while complex projects remain expensive because
coordination still requires people.
