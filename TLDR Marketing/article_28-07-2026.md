NEWS & TRENDS

 THE OPEN INTERNET IS CLOSING THE GAP WITH WALLED GARDENS (4 MINUTE
READ) [8]

 Two-thirds of consumer attention sits on the open internet while only
one-fifth of ad spend follows it there, and advertisers often pay more
for equivalent reach inside walled gardens. The gap was never a supply
problem, since open publishers collectively reach massive audiences,
but fragmented technology and limited outcome visibility made that
inventory harder to value.

 RETURN OF THE HEARTTHROB: WHY THE MEN OF THE EARLY AUGHTS ARE BOOKED
AND BUSY IN ADVERTISING (4 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Brands are reviving early 2000s heartthrobs to tap into millennial
nostalgia and drive engagement, especially with audiences like
millennial moms who now have strong purchasing power. By recreating
teenage fantasies and cultural moments, these campaigns trigger
emotional memories that feel joyful and simple compared to today's
complexity. This “nostalgia talent” strategy also resonates with
Gen Z's interest in 2000s culture.

STRATEGIES & TACTICS

 USE THE STEEL MAN TECHNIQUE TO PERSUADE PEOPLE WHO DISAGREE WITH YOU
(4 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Steel manning improves persuasion by presenting an opposing argument
in its strongest form before responding. It emphasizes understanding,
validating concerns, and then offering a deeper perspective.
Techniques like feel-felt-found build empathy and trust. Research
shows addressing objections directly increases persuasion and
credibility. Limits matter - only raise objections that can be
answered and already exist in the audience's mind. This approach leads
people toward a conclusion without forcing them to reverse their
stance.

 OPTIMIZING CRAWL BUDGETS: WHAT'S NEW (2 MINUTE READ) [11]

 Google's updated crawl budget guidance explains how sites can help
Googlebot access and index important pages more efficiently. New sites
start with limited crawl capacity, which can expand as they grow and
maintain regular updates. The guidance covers improving site
performance and organization while reducing unnecessary crawling. It
also introduces 304 status codes as a way to save server resources on
unchanged pages.

 MARKETING TEAMS ARE STUCK IN SINGLE-PLAYER CLAUDE MODE. HERE'S HOW TO
GO MULTIPLAYER (21 MINUTE READ) [14]

 Most AI gains inside marketing teams stay locked to individual users
rather than compounding across the team. A shared system that keeps
context current and capabilities reusable turns one teammate's
improvement into gains for everyone else automatically. The hard part
is rarely the technology. It's deciding who owns the system, getting
teammates to actually use what already exists, and keeping
instructions from going stale as tools change weekly. Teams that skip
this step keep re-solving problems that were already solved somewhere
else on the team.

 SPOTIFY'S 265-MILLISECOND OBSESSION (3 MINUTE READ) [15]

 Spotify won by making legal music feel as fast as piracy instead of
competing on catalog or price. It treated speed as the product and
built playback that started in about 265 milliseconds by combining
local caching, prefetching, and peer-to-peer delivery. Only 8.8% of
audio came from Spotify's own servers because cached files and other
users handled most of the work. Find the biggest reason people choose
an alternative product, then remove that friction even if it means
building your product around customer expectations set by a
competitor.

 GOOGLE DOESN'T PUNISH AI CONTENT; IT PUNISHES BAD CONTENT (8 MINUTE
READ) [16]

 Google doesn't penalize AI content. It rewards quality. Fully AI
pages can rank in top positions, but most high-ranking results are
still largely human-written. Higher AI use trends with lower rankings,
indexing rates, and impressions, though there's no strict cutoff. Many
high-AI pages are still indexed, and performance remains stable over
time. The real issue is quality, as scaled, low-value content tends to
underperform regardless of whether AI was used.
