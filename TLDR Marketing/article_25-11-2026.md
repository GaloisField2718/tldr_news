NEWS & TRENDS

 ACCENTURE LIFE TRENDS 2027 (47 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Consumers are embracing innovation while seeking more human
connection and control. AI-generated content is driving interest in
human-made media and private communities. Concerns about technology's
impact on local communities are increasing demand for transparency and
accountability. Biotech breakthroughs are raising healthcare
expectations while AI and digital platforms help people build
independent income streams. Meanwhile, wellness culture is fueling
fatigue as constant self-improvement becomes exhausting.

 WHY MANY PEOPLE WHO CAN PAY UPFRONT ARE CHOOSING TO PAY OVER TIME (2
MINUTE READ) [9]

 Consumers increasingly use buy now, pay later products to manage cash
flow and preserve savings, even when they can afford to pay upfront.
In the survey, 58% of deferred-payment users had used these products
to keep money in savings or investments, while 47% of financed
transactions could have been paid upfront. Fixed payment schedules
also appeal to consumers, as 57% prefer predictable payments over
flexible, open-ended credit. These patterns show how credit products
can serve different financial needs, from avoiding credit card
interest to covering expenses while waiting for a paycheck.

 WIKIPEDIA EXPLAINERS LOST A MEDIAN 40% OF VIEWS SINCE 2022,
BIOGRAPHIES LOST 9% (4 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Explainer pages that an AI answer can cover in two lines lost the
most traffic, while pages about people held up. For instance,
photosynthesis fell from 79,000 monthly views to 45,000, and Napoleon
only slipped from 365,000 to 346,000. Nearly all of the topic decline
came in the two years after Google launched AI Overviews, and the
pre-2022 trend of about 5% drop a year explains only half of it.

STRATEGIES & TACTICS

 YOUR AI ALREADY HAS YOUR BRAND DOC. HERE'S WHY THE DRAFTS ARE STILL
GENERIC (4 MINUTE READ) [11]

 AI drafts often sound generic because brand guidelines rely on vague
adjectives instead of specific customer and product knowledge. Build a
one-page “Brand Brain” with customer pain points in their own
words and value propositions backed by proof. Include what your
product does and doesn't do alongside an unedited writing sample. Test
it by running the same prompt with and without this context. Compare
the results and refine the document wherever the output still sounds
generic or makes unsupported claims.

 GOOGLE'S ‘TOP 10' LINE FOR CMOS POINTS AT GENERIC TIPS POSTS (3
MINUTE READ) [12]

 Google is urging brands to replace generic ‘tips' posts with
content built on first-hand expertise. Its example contrasts a generic
“Top 10 Things to Consider” article with a detailed analysis of
why a specific customer's running shoes failed. The guidance also
encourages brands to optimize for conversational searches rather than
just keywords and measure performance through leads and sales instead
of proxy metrics. Google Search's own AI guide makes the same
distinction between common knowledge and original experience.

 “MADE WITH AI” ADS HURT YOUR BRAND (2 MINUTE READ) [14]

 AI disclosure labels can hurt ad performance and brand perception,
especially when brands explain why they used AI. Across three
experiments, 82% to 92% of people who noticed an AI label assumed the
company used AI primarily to benefit itself, such as cutting costs.
Explanations about improving quality or benefiting consumers did
little to change that perception and made people rate ads less
favorably. Avoid using AI for emotional brand messaging, where
consumers are 24.6% less likely to recommend the brand, and use it
instead to refine creative ideas or support post-production. If
disclosure is required, keep the label simple rather than explaining
your reasons for using AI.

MISCELLANEOUS

 CONSUMERS, OBLIVIOUS OF BEING TARGETED, FIND ONLINE ADS HELPFUL (4
MINUTE READ) [16]

 Telling people why they're seeing an ad made them feel more watched,
even when the ad wasn't retargeted at all. Retargeting itself mostly
landed as helpful, raising click intention as ads for a product viewed
earlier followed people across sites. The more sites those ads
appeared on, though, the stronger the sense of surveillance and the
worse people felt about ads. Offering controls over ad topics
alongside the explanation could offset that effect.

 CRAFT FIRST, AI SECOND (4 MINUTE READ) [17]

 AI should support design craft rather than replace it. Start by
sketching ideas and refining features before turning to AI for
building and iteration. Too many tools and constant reviewing can
create cognitive overload. A focused workflow with 2-3 tools is more
manageable. Better results also come from precise prompts instead of
vague instructions. Build a library of inspiring screenshots and
interactions. Save feedback and design decisions to help AI better
match your taste and reduce back-and-forth.

 WHY CONFUSING ADS STILL WORK (2 MINUTE READ) [19]
