NEWS & TRENDS

 BRANDS ARE ALL IN ON CREATORS. MEANWHILE IN REALITY, THE ECONOMICS
CRASH (5 MINUTE READ) [8]

 US brand spend on influencer marketing keeps climbing, but the
industry's own numbers are questionable. Its $5.78 return per $1 spent
is earned media value, not real revenue, based on estimated
impressions and rate cards. A third of followers on the average
creator account show signs of being fake or purchased, while more than
half of creators earn under $15,000 a year. As brands shift toward
nano-creators, Americans are also posting less than they did five
years ago. What's left is a reach channel, not the trust economy it
promised.

 SPOTIFY'S NEW ‘SKIP' BUTTON COULD END THE PODCAST BUSINESS AS WE
KNOW IT (4 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Spotify is testing a feature that lets some Premium users skip
podcast ads, promotions, and filler, raising concerns among publishers
that rely on direct-sold ads. The move could weaken podcast ad revenue
as audiences increasingly shift toward YouTube and video. Spotify says
the feature could keep listeners engaged longer, but publishers worry
that easier ad skipping could make their inventory less valuable.

STRATEGIES & TACTICS

 CLAY'S “AI WRITING POLICY” (1 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Clay's new AI writing policy requires employees to fully own
AI-assisted writing, treating writing as a way to develop
understanding rather than bypass thinking. It also discourages
generating long documents from short prompts, asking writers to
respect readers' time and cut unnecessary AI-generated filler.

 EMOTION-LED ADS CUT COSTS 30% (2 MINUTE READ) [11]

 A hop-infused tea brand initially focused its ads on being
non-alcoholic, but customer research showed that belonging mattered
more. It reframed the messaging around keeping the taste and social
experience of beer without alcohol, lowering acquisition costs by 30%
in two weeks. The approach also challenges excessive personalization,
which can raise ad costs by narrowing audiences around individual
profiles instead of broader emotional motivations.

 HOW TO GROW YOUR AI VISIBILITY WITH COMMUNITY SIGNALS (8 MINUTE READ)
[12]

 Community platforms act as a foundation for AI visibility across the
funnel. Reddit, Wikipedia, and LinkedIn account for 99% of UGC
citations in SaaS-related ChatGPT answers, while UGC sites hold 15-18%
of non-vendor cited domains throughout the journey, more than 4X
publishers' 4% share. Named authors and reps also tend to outperform
brand accounts because communities treat people as participants rather
than advertisers.

 HOW TO RUN CHATGPT ADS: A STEP-BY-STEP GUIDE FROM EARLY CAMPAIGNS (7
MINUTE READ) [14]

 ChatGPT Ads are still early, so advertisers need to approach them as
a testing ground. Start by confirming eligibility and setting up your
account, then build campaigns and use context hints to reach relevant
conversations instead of relying on keywords. Create your ads and
install conversion tracking before launch. Once live, focus on testing
creative and campaign structure since reporting offers limited insight
into which conversations drive performance.

MISCELLANEOUS

 MRBEAST HIRED A HEAD OF TIKTOK. THE STRATEGY IS ALREADY PRODUCING
RECORDS (3 MINUTE READ) [15]

 Platform-specific creative teams could become the next stage of
social strategy. MrBeast created a dedicated TikTok team led by a Head
of TikTok and specialized creative talent to develop content for the
platform. One of its first successes was a simple dance video that
became TikTok's most reposted video ever. The result shows how content
built for a specific platform can outperform content adapted from
other channels, even with simpler production.

 HOW TO CREATE VIRAL ADS FT. HARMON BROTHERS (4 MINUTE READ) [16]

 Harmon Brothers makes viral ads by building every creative decision
around the sale. It starts by deeply understanding the product, then
turns awkward or unsexy problems into entertaining stories that
explain the product and reduce purchase friction. Its ads hook viewers
within the first 5 seconds and test multiple variations before scaling
spend. Longer 3-6 minute videos help build purchase intent.

 HOW THREE PRODUCT MARKETING LEADERS SET UP THEIR AI WORKFLOWS (11
MINUTE READ) [17]

 AI workflows work best when they're built around how a company
actually works, not copied from generic Skills or prompts. Three PMM
leaders use connectors, reusable Skills, and company context in
different ways. Mara Taylor uses AI to cut context switching, Natalie
Marcotullio systematizes recurring marketing work, and Mike Hetrick
builds guardrails for quality. Start with a repeatable task, test it
on real work, add human checks, then refine it.
