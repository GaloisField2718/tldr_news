 HOW TOPICAL AUTHORITY SPREADS (AND WHERE IT DOESN'T) IN CHATGPT (5
MINUTE READ) [4]

 Topical authority works best when brands build deep expertise in a
core area before expanding. ChatGPT cited brands in 74% of closely
related categories compared to 50% of distant ones, but overall brand
mentions declined from 44% to 25%. Consistent coverage within a
category drove stronger visibility than spreading content across
unrelated topics. Brands should expand into adjacent areas gradually,
as the impact of broader coverage varies by industry.

 WHAT 14,473 HOLIDAY SHOPPERS TOLD US ABOUT HOW THE WORLD SHOPS (6
MINUTE READ) [5]

 Social has become a core part of the holiday shopping journey, moving
shoppers from discovery to purchase. 37% of shoppers use social for
gift inspiration, close to search at 41%. Social also drives
transactions, with 78% purchasing in-app. Creators play a central
role, with 94% of social shoppers turning to them for guidance. AI is
already used by 23% of shoppers, while messaging remains a key
touchpoint throughout the buying process.

 9 HIDDEN REASONS YOUR ONLINE COMMUNITY MANAGEMENT SERVICES ARE
UNDERPERFORMING (2 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Online communities often underperform because teams launch them
without a clear strategy for long-term engagement. The biggest issues
come from unclear goals, weak internal support, and community
operations that don't match member needs. Teams should define what the
community is meant to achieve and invest in the right infrastructure.
Members need to be given room to create valuable discussions instead
of relying on heavy moderation.

 WHY AUDIO MATTERS FOR INSTAGRAM REELS ENGAGEMENT, AND HOW SCHEDULING
IT ACTUALLY HELPS (5 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Instagram uses Reel audio as a distribution signal, so brands should
monitor rising sounds and publish during the early adoption window
before trends peak. Similar videos can see very different reach
depending on the audio choice, making real-time trend tracking more
effective than planning content too far in advance. Teams should leave
room in their calendars to adapt as new sounds gain momentum.

 3 NEW SPOTIFY UPDATES THAT ARE CHANGING MUSIC MARKETING (3 MINUTE
READ) [9]

 Spotify now rewards active fan engagement over automated growth
tactics. User Notes lets artists and playlist curators add context to
songs, which creates new opportunities for storytelling and music
discovery inside playlists. Campaign Lift provides a clearer way to
measure the impact of Discovery Mode by estimating results beyond
organic streams, which helps validate ROI and choose the right tracks
for promotion. Spotify also now requires fans to reconnect third-party
app permissions every 6 months, so artists need to build email and SMS
lists and keep a steady release schedule to maintain fan connections.

 THESE APP STORE HIDDEN GEMS PROVE THERE'S STILL ROOM FOR GREAT
SOFTWARE IN THE AI ERA (5 MINUTE READ) [10]

 The App Store is seeing a resurgence of niche apps. Rather than
replacing apps, AI is helping independent developers build focused
tools that solve everyday problems. Apps like Albo organize saved
content from across social platforms, PI.FYI brings back friend-driven
recommendations, and ThingsBook offers a personal space for tracking
interests and memories. Simple, human-centered experiences can still
stand out in the AI era.

 THE PERKS OF BEING A CREATOR EXECUTIVE (3 MINUTE READ) [11]

 Pie named creator Nadya Okamoto as co-founder and CMO as brands lean
more on individuals over traditional marketing. Okamoto built a large
audience while scaling her previous company and now uses her platform
to introduce Pie, gather feedback, and influence product decisions in
real time. The role focuses on testing ideas publicly and iterating
quickly, reflecting how marketing is becoming more creator-led and
experimental.

 WHAT 18 YEARS IN THE TRENCHES TEACHES ABOUT AGENCY VS. IN-HOUSE
MARKETING (5 MINUTE READ) [12]

 The agency vs. in-house debate is outdated as budgets stall and AI
turns execution into a commodity. Agencies still provide speed and
cross-industry exposure, but their reliance on utilization and margins
often leads to diluted ownership and limited visibility into real
business outcomes. Freelancers offer cost-efficient execution, but are
constrained by capacity and tend to diagnose problems through a narrow
lens. In-house teams align deeply with product and strategy, yet can
lose external perspective and default to safer decisions due to higher
personal stakes.
