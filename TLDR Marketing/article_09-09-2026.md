NEWS & TRENDS

 ATTENTION IS RENTED. MEMORY IS THE NEW MEDIA (2 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Reach and impressions show who saw a campaign, not what they
remember. 47% of industry leaders say experiential marketing's value
comes from long-term brand memory. Emotional intensity and physical
presence can help brands cut through crowded digital feeds. The
strongest activations create stories people keep talking about rather
than generating a short-lived spike in attention. Brands are
increasingly using physical experiences to turn campaigns into lasting
cultural moments.

 THE WELLBEING EDIT: HOW TEENS ARE USING PINTEREST DIFFERENTLY, BY
DESIGN (4 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Teens use Pinterest more intentionally than traditional social
platforms. Re-Pins per user are up 64%, searches are up 44%, and board
organization is up 68%. More than 77% proactively search to explore
interests, while teens use feed and settings controls nearly 2X more
than other age groups. Pinterest is positioning itself around
inspiration and identity exploration rather than social performance,
with art searches 1.26X higher among teens.

 HOW I BUILT AN SEO/AEO BLOG ENGINE (10 MINUTE READ) [10]

 A growth engineering intern at Browserbase built an AI engine to
automate its SEO and AEO workflow. 53% of search traffic came from
just five posts, while only 17 of 77 posts had been cited by AI
engines. BlogEO audits existing content, finds search opportunities,
generates drafts, and tracks AI citations. Human approval remains in
the loop. After 6 weeks, search impressions grew 5.8X, page-one
queries grew 9.8X, and average blog position improved from 10.9 to
6.6.

 HOW TO GIVE AWAY FREE PRODUCT AND MAKE MONEY DOING IT (9 MINUTE READ)
[11]

 Lovable treats free AI product usage as acquisition spend rather than
a margin drain, measured against a 3-month payback threshold weighing
conversion, retention, and expansion. That framing matters more now
that AI has pushed gross margins from the SaaS era's 80-90% toward 40%
or less, leading many companies to gate AI functionality behind
paywalls instead of letting users experience it first. Free-to-paid
conversion for Lovable runs 5-10%, and partner-driven giveaways with a
strong audience match can convert at 40-60%. The realistic alternative
to giving the product away is spending far more to acquire the same
customers through paid channels, not keeping the money.

 THE 11X ROAS ACCOUNT THAT LOST MONEY ON EVERY ORDER (5 MINUTE READ)
[15]

 An account reporting an 11x return on ad spend was losing money on
every order once returns, VAT, cost of goods, and fulfillment came out
of what the platform called conversion value. Much of that 11x came
from brand campaigns near 18x, largely shoppers who had already
decided to buy, blended with a real nonbrand return closer to 3x.
Correcting for margin alone created a second problem, since bidding
purely for efficiency starved an apparel SKU of volume, leaving more
stock to mark down below cost at season's end. The fix was rebuilding
conversion values around actual contribution and giving each SKU one
deliberate job, for profit, for volume, or for cash back before the
season closed.

 THE AFTERMARKET PREMIUM (6 MINUTE READ) [16]

 The aftermarket can become a major source of value around durable
products and platforms, so platform owners must decide whether to
coexist, fight, license, or buy the businesses that emerge around
them. AMG shows how licensing and acquisition can bring a successful
modifier inside the brand, while Rolex's service restrictions show
that fighting can leave substantial margin and customer demand to
independent players. In software, platform owners control whether an
aftermarket can legally form through decisions about open source,
licensing, and developer tools, which helped create businesses like
Counter Strike, Red Hat, and Cursor.

 SEEING LIKE A STATE (7 MINUTE READ) [17]

 Dashboards make marketing easier to measure, but they can also hide
the work that actually drives growth. Teams may optimize for traffic
or output while overlooking customer knowledge and
relationship-building. These efforts are harder to quantify but can
have a bigger long-term impact. Treat metrics as incomplete maps and
question what each dashboard leaves out. Regularly compare the data
with what customers and teams are seeing firsthand.
