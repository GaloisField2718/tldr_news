NEWS & TRENDS

 REPORT: AI SEARCHES WHAT IT REMEMBERS (9 MINUTE READ) [8]

 AI search visibility depends heavily on whether a model already
remembers a brand before it searches the web. A new report found that
remembered brands were searched 3.2 times more often than brands
outside the model's memory, with search rates of 55.7% versus 17.4%
across nearly 4,000 AI responses. Category authority built through
press coverage, analyst mentions, partnerships, and consistent brand
association appears to increase the odds that AI models will search
for and recommend a brand. Strong live content still helps newer
brands appear in AI searches, but building long-term brand recognition
gives a more reliable path to visibility across future AI answers.

 WHEN MARKETING RUNS ITSELF (23 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Self-improving marketing is already live, with AI systems running
continuous loops that execute and refine their own work. In one case,
an SEO loop ran every 2 hours for 9 weeks, while another saw organic
sessions jump 85% alongside more efficient paid performance. Each
cycle builds on the last, compounding gains over time. As execution
becomes automated, the role of marketing shifts toward setting
direction and judging outcomes, while the system handles ongoing
optimization.

STRATEGIES & TACTICS

 BRIBES WORK: THE BEST WAY TO GROW YOUR EMAIL LIST THAT'S STILL
UNDERUTILIZED (5 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Growing an email list starts with creating discovery content on a
consistent schedule and using lead magnets to capture subscribers.
Instead of lengthy ebooks or courses, businesses should create quick,
actionable resources like templates, swipe files, and checklists that
solve a specific problem. These can be repurposed from existing
newsletters, social posts, videos, or client materials. Testing new
lead magnets monthly and promoting them through social channels,
websites, and email flows helps find what drives conversions.

 CASE STUDY: HOW A WEBSITE REDESIGN DELIVERED THE BEST YEAR EVER FOR
CHURNET VALLEY RAILWAY (9 MINUTE READ) [11]

 A research-led website redesign can grow conversions without risking
revenue when every change is validated through testing before launch.
Churnet Valley Railway used user research to find specific friction
points, then rolled out A/B tested updates one page at a time. The
homepage redesign increased conversions by 5%, and reusing proven
design patterns helped lift Breakfast Train bookings by 10%, Midweek
Lunch Train bookings by 48%, and dining page conversions by 13%. The
program also showed that practical information, customer FAQs, and
richer visuals such as video can increase bookings, which helped the
railway sell out its Christmas event after a 7% booking increase.

 HOW TINDER IS GETTING PEOPLE OFF THE APP AND INTO THE WILD (3 MINUTE
READ) [12]

 Tinder is trying to get its users out of the swipe screen and into
cooking classes and mahjong nights instead. The feature grew out of
research into Gen Z's post-pandemic social atrophy, letting users see
who's attending an event beforehand and swipe on them afterward rather
than in the moment to reduce the pressure of in-person rejection.
Nearly 70% of eligible users engaged during a 60-plus event LA pilot,
and it's now expanding to 26 cities across the US and Europe by
September, one of Tinder's largest financial and engineering bets in a
decade.

 THE NO MARKETING PLG STRATEGY BEHIND CURSOR'S $60B ACQUISITION (4
MINUTE READ) [17]

 Developers distrust campaigns but trust each other, so Cursor built
its growth strategy around peer proof rather than paid promotion. It
removed setup friction to create an immediate "wow" moment, shipped
rough features in public instead of waiting for polish, and used a
free tier to drive word of mouth while paid tiers converted engineers
at companies like Shopify and OpenAI who started expensing it
themselves. Cursor also turned community into its marketing
department, paying 300 city-based ambassadors to run hackathons that
double as tutorials, content, and recruiting all at once.
