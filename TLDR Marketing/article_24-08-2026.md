 AMERICA'S MEETUP EXPLOSION (2 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Young people are trading apps and screens for in-person social
experiences. “Soft socializing” events built around activities
like trivia, mahjong, birdwatching, puzzles, and crafts are surging.
Puzzle events rose 151% year over year, while mahjong events rose 94%.
The trend also extends to dating, with people turning to
activity-based meetups and dinner parties to find both romantic and
platonic connections.

 CREATOR FIT BEATS FOLLOWER COUNT FOR BRANDS (3 MINUTE READ) [6]

 The creator economy is projected to reach nearly $500B by 2027, but
brand partnerships are shifting from reach to fit. Suitability is now
the top factor brands consider when choosing creators, while follower
count ranks eighth. Yet 67% of creators earn under $10K annually, and
53% say less than 25% of their income comes from brand partnerships.
Audience-brand tension is also growing, with 42% of creators reporting
it overall and 53% among creators with 500K+ followers.

STRATEGIES & TACTICS

 HOW TO AVOID WILL ROGERS' PHENOMENON IN A/B TESTING (1 MINUTE READ)
[7]

 Will Rogers' Phenomenon occurs when moving people between groups
makes the average performance of both groups improve, even though no
one actually improved. In A/B testing, this can happen when users
cross a threshold during an experiment and get reclassified from
casual to power users, which can make both segments appear to perform
better. This can create false conclusions about a feature's impact and
mislead marketing or product decisions. Freeze segment definitions
before the experiment starts and track segment migration separately to
avoid the problem.

 THE MOST CLICKED LINK TEASER (1 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Promote your newsletter by sharing its most-clicked link a few days
after each issue. Highlight that it was the edition's top link to
create curiosity and implied social proof, then invite people to
subscribe so they don't miss future issues. This tactic works best for
newsletters with curated links, but can be adapted to other formats.

 MICRO DRAMA: THE NEW CONTENT FORMAT MARKETERS CAN'T IGNORE (5 MINUTE
READ) [9]

 A micro drama is a serialized vertical video story split into 1-3
minute episodes, each ending on a cliffhanger that pulls viewers into
the next. The format is cheaper and faster to produce than traditional
TV, letting creators test hooks in real time and cut anything that
fails to hold attention. After years of brands chasing shorter cuts
and faster hooks, micro dramas reverse the model by asking audiences
to commit across multiple episodes.

 EVERYONE KNOWS YOU. DO THEY CARE? (4 MINUTE READ) [14]

 Brands can be widely known without being relevant to the audiences
they want to reach. This piece introduces an audience mapping approach
that analyzes who people follow and how those interests overlap,
revealing the cultural spaces a brand occupies, or misses. The goal
isn't to be everywhere, but to find the communities and cultural
worlds where the brand can meaningfully belong. Brands can then build
a more distinctive position instead of pursuing broad appeal that
resonates with no one.

 BRAND CONFESSIONAL: DENNIS WANG (8 MINUTE READ) [15]

 Dennis Wang, lululemon's former VP of Accessories Design, built its
accessories business from scratch to over $1B. His CPR framework
treats hit products as engineered outcomes, starting with a clear
Customer, solving a real friction with the Product, and building
Relevancy into the experience. He favors community and partnerships
over transactional influencer campaigns.

 HOW WE BUILT A RECOGNIZABLE, SCALABLE BRAND IN BROOKLYN'S CROWDED
FOOD SCENE (7 MINUTE READ) [16]

 Lucky Parlor's branding team started with strategy, not aesthetics.
They defined the brand's positioning before designing anything. The
identity then centered on distinctive memory hooks, including a gyoza
mark, custom lettering, colors, and illustrations. The flexible system
helped the brand stay recognizable across different touchpoints as it
scaled.
