NEWS & TRENDS

 AI AND GOOGLE UPDATES TO KNOW (5 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Google is reducing publishers' opportunities to earn search traffic
as AI features take up more space. New sports features bring
play-by-play updates and fantasy recommendations directly into Search.
OpenAI is reportedly restricting some competitor ads in ChatGPT.
Meanwhile, 41 of the 50 biggest US news sites saw traffic decline year
over year. Several publishers have linked traffic losses to AI
Overviews and AI Mode, while some are responding by investing more in
direct audiences and reducing reliance on search.

 WHY ACTIVITY WINS CUSTOMER ENGAGEMENT (4 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Traditional ads are losing attention as consumers tune out
repetitive, sales-heavy formats. 88% of Gen Z and millennials ignore
most ads, while 85% stay loyal to brands that feel genuine.
Activity-based campaigns offer a different approach by motivating
people to participate rather than simply buy. 65% of consumers say
they're more likely to engage with or purchase from brands that
motivate people to get active.

 AI SEARCH IS NOT THE SAME AS SEO (1 MINUTE READ) [11]

 Instead of chasing high-volume keywords, map prompt journeys around
the final recommendation a buyer wants, then create content around the
decision factors that inform it. LLMs can route users from broad
questions to specific recommendations, so owning the highest-volume
prompt matters less than being cited for the factors behind the
purchase decision. Start with bottom-funnel prompts, map adjacent
questions, and build content that answers them.

 THE DATA CENTER WATER MYTH (3 MINUTE READ) [15]

 Americans are increasingly hearing that AI data centers are draining
the country's water supply, but the national scale of the problem is
far smaller than viral claims suggest. US data centers consumed about
228 billion gallons of water in 2023, yet that amounted to only 0.015%
to 0.2% of total US water withdrawals, compared with 43% for
agriculture. A viral claim that every ChatGPT question consumes a
bottle of water also misrepresented research that estimated 500
milliliters could be consumed across roughly 10 to 50 GPT -3
responses. Water use can still create serious local concerns, but
misinformation and misleading comparisons are turning a site-specific
resource issue into a broader narrative about AI infrastructure
threatening the US water supply.

 GRAPHIC DESIGNER ≠ ART DIRECTOR ≠ CREATIVE DIRECTOR (12 MINUTE
READ) [16]

 Job title confusion runs in both directions. Companies without a
creative background borrow a senior-sounding title off a template
instead of defining the actual work, while designers themselves
inflate titles because freelance clients rarely check credentials.
Underneath the confusion, the jobs barely move: a designer executes
one piece of visual communication, an art director keeps a whole
project's output feeling like one brand, and a creative director sets
the point of view behind it. Only company size decides how many people
that work gets split across.

 INSIDE THE CREATOR ECONOMY'S AI RECKONING (7 MINUTE READ) [17]

 Creator-economy sentiment toward AI has shifted in about a year from
dread to a hunt for leverage, with most creators now treating AI
output as a rough starting draft rather than a finished product. That
shift is showing up in contracts: some brands now ask for access to
creators' AI prompts, while creators are starting to write their own
AI-use clauses back to protect their voice. Adoption itself splits by
gender, since women are 22% less likely than men to be regular AI
users at work and more likely to worry it looks like cutting corners.
The recurring conclusion is that audiences still connect to the person
behind the work, not the tool that helped make it.
