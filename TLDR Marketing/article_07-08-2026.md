NEWS & TRENDS

 HOW TO LAUGH (AND WIN) WITH NEXT GEN (10 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Gen Z and Gen Alpha use humor as a way to build identity and navigate
culture. Snapchat's study found 74% are more likely to consider buying
from funny brands, while 83% pay more attention to them. Brands should
understand the context behind internet humor before joining trends.
The best brand content adds to existing conversations through irony
and formats audiences can remix and share.

 WHY ADVERTISERS SHOULDN'T GET TOO EXCITED ABOUT MICRODRAMAS (2 MINUTE
READ) [6]

 Microdramas attract strong audience attention but current platform
economics limit the advertising opportunity. ReelShort users now spend
35.7 minutes a day on the app, yet the platforms rely on subscriptions
and in-app purchases because standard video ads take up too much of a
two-minute episode to work well. Brands can still use the format
through branded entertainment, as shown by Nivea Malaysia and KFC, but
those campaigns require high-quality storytelling that is difficult
and expensive to repeat at scale. Watch the format for future growth,
but treat it as a niche content strategy instead of a new advertising
channel.

STRATEGIES & TACTICS

 WHY NO ONE'S CLICKING “SEND VIA DM” (2 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Low “send via DM” activity usually signals content isn't built to
be shared. Make posts more “sendable” by delivering standalone
value someone would pass along. Bring back high-signal formats like
original data, strong insights, or visual breakdowns that feel useful.
Narrow the POV to focus on core audience needs instead of covering too
many topics. Layer in deeper formats like video to increase engagement
and make ideas easier to understand and share.

 LINKEDIN IS CRACKING DOWN ON AI SLOP. HERE'S WHAT THAT MEANS FOR YOUR
STRATEGY (5 MINUTE READ) [8]

 The AI writing tools LinkedIn embedded in its own product helped
produce the flood of generic content the platform is now suppressing.
Posts flagged as AI slop keep their visibility to direct connections
but lose recommendation-engine distribution, so higher posting
frequency now consumes slots without delivering reach. The line is
whether a post contains something only its author or their
organization could provide, a client outcome, a first-party data
point, or a specific internal observation. AI used for research and
drafting is fine. AI used for the thinking is what gets caught.

 OPENAI BRINGS PRODUCT CAROUSELS TO CHATGPT ADS (2 MINUTE READ) [9]

 OpenAI is rolling out a product carousel format for ChatGPT ads,
letting multiple products from a single retailer appear in one
placement rather than the single-product unit that's run for five
months. The system pulls product data directly from retailer feeds,
similar to Google's Shopping feed, and OpenAI's own platform decides
whether to serve a single ad or a carousel rather than leaving that
choice to advertisers. The mechanics are already built to support
competing retailers in the same carousel.

 WISPR FLOW LAUNCHES A GRANOLA-STYLED MEETING NOTETAKER (2 MINUTE
READ) [10]

 Wispr Flow launched a Mac notetaker that captures meetings using
system audio without joining the call. It transcribes conversations in
real time, cleans up notes, generates summaries and action items, and
lets users query past meetings with AI. The move expands Wispr Flow
beyond dictation into meeting intelligence.

 STUDY: DOES TOPICAL FOCUS MAKE YOUR BRAND MORE VISIBLE? (6 MINUTE
READ) [11]

 Owning a topic helps AI search recommend your brand, but broad
content only works when you build deep authority in each category
instead of spreading shallow coverage. The study found brands earned
citations in 50% of distant categories but mentions in only 25%, which
suggests AI will cite broad expertise but reserve recommendations for
brands that consistently cover core topics. Repeated visibility across
related categories improved future brand mentions, while shallow
presence across many categories reduced them. The effect varies by
industry, so finance and real estate brands can test adjacent category
expansion using citations as an early signal, while legal and
healthcare brands need repeat brand mentions before treating a
category as established.

 COPYWRITING FRIDAY: STEVE JOBS LAUNCHES THE IPHONE (7 MINUTE READ)
[12]

 Steve Jobs made the original iPhone launch memorable because it
followed a clear sequence that built curiosity, solved a problem,
answered objections, and proved value one step at a time. The
presentation delayed the reveal, framed existing smartphones as
frustrating, and turned every feature into a customer benefit instead
of listing specifications. Jobs also used specific proof points such
as a 160 pixels per inch display and Apple reaching its 100 millionth
iPod to make claims feel credible. Strong product marketing works best
when each message has one purpose, every claim has proof, and every
page or presentation moves the audience to the next idea instead of
showing everything at once.

 CREATORS ARE QUIETLY BECOMING MEDIA CONGLOMERATES (7 MINUTE READ)
[13]

 US brands will spend at least $21 billion on creators in 2026, nearly
double 2022, while publishers' revenue advantage over creator earnings
has collapsed from 44% to roughly 26% over the same span. The biggest
names, led by MrBeast's Beast Industries, are restructuring into
parent companies spanning chocolate, toys, financial services, and a
planned mobile carrier, hiring CEOs pulled from traditional media
rather than running a single YouTube channel. The diversification
hedges against any one line failing and reduces the key-man risk that
makes a business hard to sell, since buyers want a team that can run
without its founder.
