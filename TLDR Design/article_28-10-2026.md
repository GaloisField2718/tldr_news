 ACCESS VIDEO AND IMAGE MODELS FROM FIGMA WEAVE VIA THE FIGMA MCP (1
MINUTE READ) [4]

 Figma Weave's full lineup of video and image models, including
Seedance, Kling, and Nano Banana, can now be used inside external AI
agents through the Figma MCP. Users need a Figma Weave account
connected to their Figma account to generate media mid-conversation.
Each video or image generated with a Weave model consumes Weave
credits.

 FRANKI BRANDING: INSIDE KOTO'S NEW IDENTITY (3 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Koto's identity for restaurant discovery app Franki turns finding
places to eat into the visual language of an insider's club, centered
on bright yellow, oversized rounded typography, candid photography,
and conversational copy. Diatype Rounded and a soft geometric symbol
create a friendly foundation, while phrases such as “If we know, you
know” reinforce Franki's positioning as a knowledgeable friend
rather than a conventional recommendation platform. Motion extends
through typography, UI, and content to suit the video-first product,
while the same system carries into Franki Social Club and physical
experiences.

 NEW REPORT SLAMS MICROSOFT FOR LEVERAGING DARK PATTERNS TO FORCE
PEOPLE TO USE EDGE (4 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Microsoft continues using dark patterns, including misleading
banners, default-browser restrictions, and forced actions, to steer
Windows users toward Edge. Users outside the European Economic Area
encounter more interference when downloading, setting, and keeping an
alternative default browser, demonstrating how regulatory requirements
can directly affect interface design. Windows features such as Search
and Widgets can also bypass a user's chosen default and open links in
Edge, reinforcing concerns that Microsoft's design decisions undermine
meaningful browser choice.

 3 DESIGN PRINCIPLES IN THE AI AGE (5 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Three enduring principles remain essential to good design despite AI
dramatically accelerating creative work: iteration, feedback, and
connecting the dots. Faster generation makes exploring alternatives
cheaper, but quality still requires repeated refinement, diverse human
perspectives, and judgment about which feedback matters rather than
accepting AI's first answer or treating it as the only critic.
Designers also need time away from production to process accumulated
knowledge and recognize larger connections, because AI can generate
faster than humans can meaningfully reflect.

 DESIGN AS A GARDEN (2 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Creative work can be approached like tending a garden: cultivate
favorable conditions, return consistently, encourage unexpected
cross-pollination, and protect fragile ideas before judging them too
quickly. Pruning unnecessary work and deliberately leaving room for
play can be as important as producing more, while not every experiment
needs an obvious payoff. Ideas also benefit from periods of inactivity
and patience, making it useful to distinguish between moments that
require greater effort and those that simply require more time.

 NINE CHECKS BEFORE SHIPPING A PRODUCT EMAIL: A WORKFLOW FOR
DEVELOPERS AND DESIGNERS (7 MINUTE READ) [11]

 A product email can look right in a design file yet fail its reader
through a vanishing action, a surviving staging link, or a duplicate
send. Using a workspace invitation as the example, nine checks cover
the message's behavioral contract, client compatibility,
accessibility, captured application output, link validation, and retry
handling. Delivery evidence, such as authentication and bounce
handling, is reviewed separately after the template passes.

 MICROSOFT'S IDENTITY FOR COPILOT POSITIONS IT AS A TOOL FOR WORK (7
MINUTE READ) [12]

 Microsoft's evolving Copilot identity positions AI as a practical
tool embedded in everyday work rather than an abstract futuristic
technology. Its animated two-part mark, paired sans-serif and serif
typography, gradients, and flexible graphic system create continuity
across Microsoft's ecosystem while giving Copilot a recognizable
presence of its own. The system is designed to scale across product
interfaces, campaigns, and motion, balancing the expressiveness needed
to represent generative AI with the clarity expected from a
productivity tool.
