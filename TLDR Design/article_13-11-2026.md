 EUROPE WANTS TO BAN 'DARK PATTERNS' IN APPS AND LOOT BOXES FOR MINORS
(2 MINUTE READ) [4]

 The EU's proposed Digital Fairness Act would ban dark patterns and
hard-to-cancel subscriptions, and restrict loot boxes and other
pay-to-win tactics that push minors to overspend. Companies in breach
could face fines of up to six percent of their global revenue.
Enforcement would also shift from member states to the Commission,
which gains direct powers to investigate large-scale cases.

 GOOGLE IS GIVING AI VIDEO CREATORS LONGER CLIPS, BETTER EDITING, AND
FEWER FRUSTRATING MISTAKES (2 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Google's Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash generates AI video clips of 3 to 15
seconds, up from a 10-second ceiling in the previous version. Clips
can be refined through multi-turn chat, swapping backgrounds, weather,
or aspect ratio without regenerating everything. Pricing drops 30% to
$0.07 per second of 720p video, with less visual drift and clearer
lip-sync.

 WHATSAPP MAY SOON REPLACE THE COMMUNITIES TAB ON IOS (2 MINUTE READ)
[6]

 WhatsApp is testing a new Contacts tab on iOS that replaces
Communities in the bottom navigation bar, making it easier to find
people and start conversations. The redesigned hub displays favorite
contacts, highlights online users with green indicators, and lets
people sort contacts alphabetically, by recent activity, or by
upcoming birthdays. Communities will remain accessible through a
filter in the Chats tab, while the new Contacts experience is
currently limited to selected beta testers.

 DO AI MODERATORS UNCOVER AS MUCH AS HUMAN MODERATORS? (11 MINUTE
READ) [7]

 In a randomized trial with 20 participants, human moderators surfaced
more themes than an AI moderator built on Gemini 3 Flash, averaging
6.9 of eight versus 5.0 in Claude's coding. The widest gap was sleep
climate, which humans uncovered in six to nine of ten sessions by
probing beyond the discussion guide. Where the guide covered a topic,
AI came within about eight percentage points, so a strong script
closes most of the gap.

 DESIGN SHOULD MAKE IT EASIER TO SHIP (4 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Good design should improve the product without unnecessarily
increasing the difficulty of building and maintaining it. Designers
need to consider implementation costs, explain why proposed changes
justify the work, and collaborate with engineers when technical
constraints require compromises. Design engineering reduces the
overhead of iteration by allowing designers to implement their own
adjustments, while design systems should help teams ship consistent
interfaces rather than create obstacles they constantly work around.

 WHY A BUTTON TAKES A MONTH (9 MINUTE READ) [9]

 DS Contracts is an experimental system that converts React components
into editable Figma components and back using deterministic JSON
contracts that describe layout, tokens, properties, and visual
appearance. A test with IBM's Carbon design system generated 84 button
variants in roughly 30 seconds, but only 40 passed validation,
exposing subtle differences in icon dimensions, font naming,
rotations, and layout behavior. Rather than relying on AI-generated
approximations, the project uses strict visual comparisons, explicit
failure reporting, and planned held-out tests to measure whether
conversions genuinely preserve design intent.

 SYSTEM COLORS NEED TO WORK TOGETHER (5 MINUTE READ) [13]

 Chrome's dark mode on Android pairs HighlightText with Highlight at
just 1.98:1 contrast, making active buttons unreadable despite system
colors being named to work together. The System Color Study compares
resolved values and intended pairings across browsers, operating
systems, and preferences, checking each against five WCAG thresholds.

 DOES A PORTFOLIO NEED TO FOLLOW UX CONVENTIONS? (5 MINUTE READ) [14]

 Creative portfolios don't always need conventional navigation and
predictable layouts, particularly when expressing personality and
encouraging exploration are central to their purpose. Five designers
demonstrate alternative approaches, including interactive bookshelves,
chronological timelines, and unconventional visual structures that
make browsing feel like discovering someone's creative world. These
choices introduce usability trade-offs, but a portfolio can prioritize
curiosity, memorability, and artistic expression when those goals are
more important than helping visitors reach specific information as
quickly as possible.

 STUDIO BRIGHT GREEN'S QUIETLY CONFIDENT IDENTITY FOR LEVRANT (5
MINUTE READ) [15]

 Studio Bright Green has rebranded UK heritage architecture practice
Heritage Architecture as Levrant, using internal workshops and client
research to create an identity that better reflects its broader
architectural and consultancy expertise. The restrained visual system
combines a custom geometric logotype, Klim Type Foundry's Söhne,
stone-inspired neutrals, Venetian Red accents, and architectural
photography emphasizing material, light, and proportion. Flexible
templates and a consistent art direction extend the identity across
the website, reports, presentations, and everyday communications,
allowing the practice to present its expertise with greater clarity
and confidence.
