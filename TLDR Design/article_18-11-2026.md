 PENTAGRAM DRAWS ON ANCIENT GREEK COINAGE FOR ALPHA BANK IDENTITY
REFRESH (5 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Pentagram has refreshed Greek financial institution Alpha Bank's
identity, drawing inspiration from an ancient silver stater of Aegina
that originally influenced the bank's 1972 logo. The redesigned emblem
preserves the coin's distinctive geometry and subtle asymmetry,
accompanied by a custom Greek and Latin typeface called Alpha Sans, an
expanded color palette, and patterns derived from the symbol.
Developed around the positioning "Building progress, together," the
system extends across digital products, communications, and redesigned
physical branches, balancing the bank's cultural heritage with a more
contemporary identity.

 NEW CAMERA SENSOR COMING TO THE IPHONE 20 PRO'S MAIN CAMERA, SAYS
LEAKER (3 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Apple's rumored iPhone 20 Pro could introduce a new main camera
sensor manufactured using a 17-nanometer logic process. Previous
reports have suggested that Apple is exploring larger camera sensors
for future iPhones, including a possible 200-megapixel telephoto
sensor, although the latest leak doesn't confirm whether the main
sensor will grow in size. A larger sensor could improve low-light
photography and overall image quality, but the specifications and
potential benefits of the new component remain unknown.

 AGENT HARNESS: DEFINITION (6 MINUTE READ) [9]

 An agent harness is the software that runs an AI model's execution
loop, controls context, tool access, and limits. Two assistants built
on the same model can differ sharply in capability, so UX teams should
test whole tasks, not just model output.

 HOW TO RUN A UX POSTMORTEM: A PRACTICAL SIX-STEP FRAMEWORK (8 MINUTE
READ) [10]

 Design decisions should be treated as hypotheses, so teams ought to
review shipped work against what users actually did. This six-step
postmortem covers documenting decisions, revisiting assumptions,
examining outcomes, spotting surprises, extracting principles, and
storing the learning.

 GOOGLE'S YI XIANG ON HOW TO BUILD A DESIGN CAREER ACROSS GLOBAL
BORDERS (8 MINUTE READ) [11]

 Google senior UX designer Yi Xiang has built an international career
spanning China, Canada, Hong Kong, and the United States, learning to
adapt to different creative cultures without abandoning his own
perspective. His experience highlights the importance of seeking
honest feedback, understanding local communication styles, and
developing confidence through experimentation and continuous learning
rather than trying to fit a predetermined professional identity.
Creative coding and personal projects also provide opportunities to
explore new technologies, develop distinctive skills, and demonstrate
capabilities beyond conventional portfolio case studies.

 ACCESSIBILITY AND THE EU AI ACT (4 MINUTE READ) [15]

 The EU AI Act is already in force, with obligations phasing in
through 2028, and it overlaps with accessibility law. Providers of
Annex III high-risk AI must meet applicable accessibility requirements
from December 2027, and people must be told, accessibly, when they
deal with AI.

[16]

 Singapore-based product designer April Zhu, trained in architecture
and creator of the Colorfigures and Tinycharts AI skills, explains how
she designs with AI. She left slow-moving architecture for tech's
faster feedback loops, grew Colorfigures from a hand-drawn washing
machine, and won't outsource her taste and judgment.

 WHY ENTERPRISE DASHBOARDS FAIL EVEN WHEN THEY'RE WELL DESIGNED (8
MINUTE READ) [17]

 Enterprise dashboards can fail despite excellent visual design
because adoption depends on organizational trust, shared definitions,
and established workflows rather than charts alone. When different
departments interpret the same metrics differently, even accurate
dashboards become unreliable, while competing priorities and requests
for additional features can undermine their usefulness. Establishing
governed metric definitions, involving stakeholders in decisions, and
addressing the social dynamics surrounding data can improve adoption
more effectively than repeatedly refining visual layouts.
