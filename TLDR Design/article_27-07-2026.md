NEWS & TRENDS

 AMAZON IS REDESIGNING PRIME VIDEO WITH MORE AI, BUT WILL IT FIX WHAT
FRUSTRATES VIEWERS? (3 MINUTE READ) [4]

 Amazon is overhauling Prime Video's home screen through an internal
project called Lighthouse, personally driven by Jeff Bezos, replacing
the static tile grid with AI-generated, personalized recommendations.
The redesign follows a tense meeting last fall where Bezos pushed
executives to lean harder into AI. It remains unclear whether
AI-driven rankings will genuinely reduce paid placement bias or simply
shift it into the algorithm, leaving real benefits to viewers
uncertain until testing expands beyond the current small user group.

 HYUNDAI MOTOR GROUP OPENS UX RESEARCH HUB IN SHANGHAI (1 MINUTE READ)
[5]

 Hyundai Motor Group has opened UX Studio Shanghai, a dedicated user
experience research hub in Jing'an district, completing its four-city
network alongside Seoul, Frankfurt, and Irvine facilities. The
three-story studio splits into a public Open Lab, with Explorer, Test,
and Archive zones, and an invite-only Advanced Lab housing a
simulation platform for concept-to-validation co-development.

 WHATSAPP TESTING NEW MESSAGE BUBBLES ON IOS (1 MINUTE READ) [6]

 WhatsApp is testing redesigned chat bubbles on iOS that feature
significantly rounder, softer corners, bringing them closer to
iMessage and better aligning with its recent Liquid Glass redesign.
The update also removes the green outlines around photos, videos,
GIFs, and link previews for a cleaner, more integrated appearance. The
changes are currently limited to select TestFlight users, with no
confirmed date for a wider rollout.

 IOS 27'S NEW SIRI DELIVERS IN EVERY SINGLE WAY I'D HOPED FOR (2
MINUTE READ) [7]

 iOS 27 introduces a major AI-powered Siri upgrade that addresses
three key weaknesses: world knowledge, conversational interactions,
and consistency across Apple devices. Siri can now answer broad
questions, maintain conversations with follow-ups and chat history,
and provide the same capabilities across iPhone, Mac, Apple Watch,
iPad, and, eventually, Apple TV and HomePod. Deep integration with
Apple apps also enables personalized assistance based on messages,
notes, and email, potentially giving Siri a significant advantage for
everyday Apple users.

 A SIDE PROJECT IS THE FASTEST WAY TO UPSKILL IN THE AGE OF AI (5
MINUTE READ) [8]

 Building small side projects is one of the fastest ways for UX
professionals to learn how AI is changing software development, with
tools like Claude Code, Codex, and Cursor dramatically lowering the
technical, time, and cost barriers to creating real products. Rather
than relying on highly abstracted tools like Lovable, working closer
to the actual development process—doing discovery and design,
learning concepts such as version control, and asking AI to explain
unfamiliar technology—builds much deeper practical knowledge. The
key is to build with AI rather than have AI build everything for you,
gaining technical fluency and end-to-end product-building skills
through hands-on experience.

 WORKING BACKWARDS (5 MINUTE READ) [9]

 A visit to an art school studio shows how painting habits mirror the
judgment senior designers build over time. Design differs from art in
serving someone else's outcome, yet deliberate practice—training the
eye as art students do—builds taste no tool can replace. As prompts
turn ideas into finished apps within days, skipping the slow,
layer-by-layer deconstruction that art students practice may cost
designers the deeper judgment such practice builds.

MISCELLANEOUS

 ANATOMY'S CAMPAIGN FOR THE HORNIMAN MUSEUM IS BUILT ON A BESPOKE
TYPEFACE FORAGED FROM ITS COLLECTIONS AND GARDENS (2 MINUTE READ) [12]


 The Horniman Museum has partnered with London agency Anatomy on
‘It's in our Nature', a vibrant campaign celebrating its 125th year
and new nature-themed outdoor space. Instead of using an existing
typeface, Anatomy created a bespoke alphabet from shapes, textures,
architecture, plants, and specimens found across the museum and
gardens, balancing playful character with readability while leaving
room for future community contributions. Individual letterforms were
given distinctive, handcrafted animations, complemented by a warm
color palette and community-led photography to create an identity that
reflects the Horniman's eclectic mix of nature, culture, and
curiosity.

 LEARNING BEFORE LEVERAGE (5 MINUTE READ) [13]

 A design team's rollout of Claude Code in February revealed pressure
to appear competent before actually mastering the tool, echoing a
similar pattern at Slack. Drawing on researchers and practitioners,
the piece argues AI adoption should be treated as reversible
experimentation built on candor, shared vocabulary, and coordinated
workflow changes across design and engineering. Survey data showing
most designers feel overwhelmed and pressured to do more underscores
the case for making uncertainty visible rather than hidden.

 WHY I DON'T USE INTERRUPTION PAGES (3 MINUTE READ) [14]

 A UX reviewer argues against using interruption pages, a GOV.UK
Design System pattern meant to pause users with warnings mid-journey.
Interruption pages offer multiple actions, use unconventional button
labels, and look like dismissible banners rather than part of the form
flow. A standard question with radio buttons is recommended instead,
since it forces conscious decisions without needing a visually
distinct screen.
