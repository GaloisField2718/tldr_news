 INSTAGRAM'S EDITS LANDS ON IPAD WITH A LARGER CANVAS, AND A NATIVE
THREADS APP IS ROLLING OUT SOON (2 MINUTE READ) [4]

 Instagram's Edits video editor, previously limited to phones and Mac,
now runs on iPad with a larger canvas for the timeline and tools, just
over two weeks after its Mac launch. The iPad version adds trimming
before import, background caption generation, custom .cube LUT support
in Labs, three new filters, and more than 1,600 new stickers. A native
Threads app for iPad is coming soon, according to Instagram's @clips
account.

 DON'T LOOK NOW BUT ANTHROPIC JUST RELEASED AN ‘I'M FEELING LUCKY'
BUTTON FOR VIBE CODERS (2 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Anthropic's new Claude Ship bundles three AI agents that take a pull
request from review to fixes to approval, working like an "I'm Feeling
Lucky" button for vibe coders. Code Review hunts for problems such as
security holes, PR Steward applies the flagged fixes, and Claude
Approvals checks the code against a rules document. Merging can then
happen with no human involved if an organization's GitHub rules allow
it, and Anthropic suggests starting in observe-only mode.

 IOS 27.3 INTRODUCES DESIGN CHANGES FOR THESE APPLE APPS (2 MINUTE
READ) [6]

 Apple is introducing design updates to four built-in apps with iOS
27.3, scheduled for release in December. Health will receive the most
significant overhaul, with new Insights, Longevity, and Browse tabs,
while Calendar will move key controls to the bottom of the screen for
easier one-handed use. Safari will introduce optional vertical tabs,
and Apple Music will allow animated album artwork to blend seamlessly
into the scrolling lyrics interface in landscape mode.

 BEYOND THE PROMPT: DESIGNING AI INTERFACES FOR CREATIVE TOOLS (5
MINUTE READ) [7]

 Creative AI tools that confine visual, spatial, or sound work to a
text box create a gap between what people imagine and what they can
express. Visual cards, sketches, sliders, and painting directly on
objects let users show intent instead of spelling it out, while
language still wins for negation, comparison, and exception. Seemingly
simple interfaces risk automation bias and all-or-nothing results, so
tools should reveal their assumptions and allow small tweaks, rather
than demanding better prompts.

 FUTURES THINKING AS AN EVERYDAY DESIGN PRACTICE (14 MINUTE READ) [8]

 The accelerating pace of AI-driven change is making strategic
foresight an increasingly important part of everyday design practice
rather than an occasional innovation exercise. The Three Horizons
framework helps organizations understand the relationship between
established systems, emerging approaches, and possible future
operating models. Design leaders can use futures thinking to challenge
organizational assumptions, experiment with new working relationships,
and develop human capabilities alongside technological advances.

 WHO DRAWS THE IN-BETWEENS? (17 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Controversy surrounding Sylvain Chomet's animated holiday film for
Dior, which used automated interpolation for some frames, highlights
the difficulty of defining creative authorship in the age of AI.
Animation has historically involved delegating repetitive execution to
assistants, with creative ownership resting on decisions about
movement, timing, and expression. As AI lowers technical barriers to
creative production, judgment, taste, and intentionality become
increasingly important differentiators.

 CAN AI GENERATE GOOD DESIGNS FROM RAW USER DATA? (6 MINUTE READ) [14]


 AI-generated designs met 77.9% of scorecard criteria from synthesized
findings and recommendations, versus 69.5% from raw user comments, an
8.4-point gap. Raw comments describe problems but rarely say what to
build, while synthesis adds the interpretation and design decisions AI
tools struggle to make alone. Teams should not assume raw research
data is enough context, and the data here was small and cleaner than
typical feedback.

 BASKERVILLE: THE TYPEFACE THAT WAS TOO PERFECT FOR ITS TIME (5 MINUTE
READ) [15]

 John Baskerville revolutionized eighteenth-century typography by
developing a typeface with pronounced stroke contrast, refined serifs,
and vertical stress, supported by innovations in ink, paper, and
printing techniques. Initially criticized by English readers for its
excessive sharpness, Baskerville eventually became an influential
transitional serif, inspiring numerous revivals, including Zuzana
Licko's Mrs Eaves. Its longstanding association with scholarly
publishing and established institutions gives the typeface a sense of
authority, with a 2012 experiment even suggesting that it can
influence readers' perceived credibility of written statements.
