 JONY IVE AND MARGARET CALVERT CREATE POSTERS TO CELEBRATE DECADE OF
DESIGN MUSEUM IN KENSINGTON (3 MINUTE READ) [6]

 London's Design Museum has commissioned ten prominent designers to
create posters celebrating the tenth anniversary of its move to
Kensington in 2016. Jony Ive and LoveFrom contributed a minimalist
gray-and-white composition resembling the number 10, while Margaret
Calvert created a road sign-inspired design referencing the museum's
former location at Shad Thames. Other contributors include Tim Burton,
Hella Jongerius, Paul Smith, Marina Willer, and Tom Dixon, with
designs spanning illustration, typography, textiles, and graphic
design.

 GOOGLE WANTS ALL APPS TO BE FOLDABLE-READY, AND THE SHIFT GOES INTO
EFFECT NEXT YEAR (3 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Google Play will require Android apps to meet baseline foldable
compatibility standards starting in April 2027, with noncompliant apps
potentially losing the ability to publish updates. Apps must fill the
available screen without letterboxing, preserve user activity when
devices fold or rotate, and support multi-window operation. Developers
will also need to provide foldable screenshots, while more advanced
guidelines encourage responsive multi-pane layouts, external input
support, and interfaces that adapt to different device postures.

 INTRODUCING STITCH LOOP: A NEW WAY TO BUILD AND EVOLVE PRODUCTS (3
MINUTE READ) [8]

 Google Labs' Stitch Loop is a long-running product agent that
connects to a codebase and live metrics to keep improving software
after launch. It suggests fixes, writes code in a cloud sandbox, and
opens GitHub pull requests, with nothing merging until a person
approves. Built-in loops cover security, latency, and accessibility,
and a global waitlist is open with priority for eligible Google AI
Ultra users.

OPINIONS & TUTORIALS

 AI MADE US FASTER. SO WHY DO DESIGNERS HAVE LESS TIME? (5 MINUTE
READ) [9]

 AI has dramatically reduced the time required to generate creative
work, but designers often face shorter deadlines, more revisions, and
an overwhelming number of options to evaluate. A study by METR in 2025
found that experienced developers took 19% longer to complete tasks
with early-2025 AI tools despite believing they were working faster,
illustrating the difference between perceived and actual productivity.
The problem stems partly from adding AI to unchanged workflows, making
deliberate constraints, faster selection methods, and stronger design
fundamentals essential for realizing meaningful time savings.

 DESIGN LEADERSHIP IN 2026 IS MOSTLY SAYING NO TO GOOD IDEAS (5 MINUTE
READ) [10]

 AI has made prototyping inexpensive enough that designers, product
managers, and engineers can generate numerous viable solutions,
shifting design leadership toward evaluating ideas and deciding which
ones deserve to ship. Leaders increasingly spend their time reviewing
prototypes, protecting product priorities, aligning teams, and
rejecting attractive features that would introduce unnecessary
complexity or distract from the roadmap. As design and engineering
roles converge, understanding business context, exercising judgment,
and maintaining high standards become more important than simply
producing polished interfaces.

 SEND THE FIREFIGHTERS TO THE FIRES (13 MINUTE READ) [11]

 Enterprise interfaces frequently bury urgent problems inside enormous
tables, forcing users to search through hundreds of records instead of
immediately seeing what requires attention. Management by exception, a
principle dating back more than a century, suggests that operational
software should prioritize abnormal conditions and actionable issues
while keeping healthy systems in the background. Designing around
exceptions rather than exhaustive inventories can reduce cognitive
load, accelerate problem resolution, and make dashboards more useful
for the people responsible for fixing things.

 LONDON FILM SCHOOL: REBELLIOUS BRANDING BY RABBITHOLE (3 MINUTE READ)
[16]

 Rabbithole has redesigned London Film School's identity to reconnect
the institution with the independent, anti-establishment spirit that
defined its founding in 1956. The visual system combines Kimera's
condensed Waldenburg typeface, a vivid red palette, and bold framing
bars that evoke cinematic composition without relying on familiar
film-related symbols. Modular layouts, animated typography, and stark
contrasts extend across print materials, merchandise, and a redesigned
website intended to communicate more clearly with prospective
international students.

 ON DRAWING ICONS (12 MINUTE READ) [17]

 Pierre has rebuilt its icon library from the ground up, creating more
than 1,200 icons across three sizes and multiple variants to establish
a more consistent visual language. The process involved refining
stroke weights, optical alignment, proportions, and reusable shapes
while developing custom Figma plugins to automate repetitive
operations such as swapping layers, generating previews, and adjusting
arrow geometry. The redesigned library also required a new pipeline
connecting Figma, SVG, and React, demonstrating how careful visual
craftsmanship and purpose-built tooling can work together to maintain
consistency at scale.

 WHY I DON'T LIKE (AND DON'T RECOMMEND) WEBSITE CHECKLISTS (1 MINUTE
READ) [18]

 Website checklists are built for bulk, standardizing specifics
instead of tailoring them, and every one contains advice that doesn't
apply. A 2019 dissection of the front-end checklist ended with 41
thumbs-down and 13 raised hands. Quality websites are bespoke, so
understanding the problem and knowing the craft matter more than any
checklist, template, or framework.
