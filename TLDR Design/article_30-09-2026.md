 OPENAI LAUNCHES DOTS, ITS BUBBLY AGENTIC AVATAR (2 MINUTE READ) [4]

 OpenAI has launched Dots, always-on personal agents powered by GPT-6
Astra that pursue user-defined goals in the background rather than
staying tied to a single interface. Available to ChatGPT Pro and
Business Premium users in eligible markets, Dots can be launched from
ChatGPT or Codex and contacted through platforms such as Slack and
Teams, with text messaging planned. Their floating, cartoon-like
avatars give autonomous software a more approachable identity, while
OpenAI envisions specialist Dots working together with their own tools
and permissions.

 REPLIT ACQUIRES ATTA TO BRING AI BUSINESS ANALYSIS INTO ITS PLATFORM
(2 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Replit has acquired Atta, an AI-powered business analysis workspace,
bringing its data-analysis and charting technology directly into
Replit's platform. Users can upload or connect a dataset and ask
Replit Agent to investigate a question or generate a visualization
without writing SQL. The move reflects a broader shift toward folding
business analysis into the same environment used to build and operate
software.

 YOU'LL EITHER LOVE OR HATE THE FORMULA E LOGO – AND THAT'S THE
WHOLE POINT (3 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Formula E's new identity by MOX centers on a deliberately divisive
logomark that combines an F and E at a 30-degree angle to convey speed
and momentum. A bespoke type family based on AP Gavel expands the
system with condensed italics for energy and a monospaced style for
technical data, complemented by Inter for longer copy. Surge Purple,
Signal Orange, and Electric Lime add a distinctive palette designed to
give the championship a more expressive identity as it enters its GEN4
era.

OPINIONS & TUTORIALS

 DON'T FORGET TO DESIGN (4 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Design isn't something that happens after fully understanding a
problem. Making sketches and prototypes is itself a way to discover
what the problem really is. Rough ideas expose gaps, tensions, and
requirements that research, data, and discussion alone may never
reveal. An excessive focus on outcomes can discourage designers from
making anything, even though reaching a good outcome often requires
producing and evaluating lots of imperfect work along the way.

 SKIP THE TOOLS, MAKE THE OUTCOMES (3 MINUTE READ) [8]

 AI creates opportunities to skip traditional tools and deliver the
outcomes those tools were originally designed to help people achieve.
An AI-powered newsroom called Exposit discovered that users
overwhelmingly preferred asking for a topic and receiving a
personalized, up-to-date report instead of navigating headlines,
categories, and search interfaces. This “outcome first, tool
second” approach suggests a useful product design question: whether
a new AI experience needs another interface at all, or can simply
produce what people came for.

 THE FASTEST GUN IN UX: WHY YOUR TEAM IS TELLING THE WRONG STORY (5
MINUTE READ) [9]

 Designers are rushing through their process to prove value through
speed, but the real contest is winning stakeholder alignment, where
they are both vulnerable and well positioned. The "Fastest Gun in the
West" hijacks planning by anchoring early on an idea, using vague
agile language, and pressuring designers to produce polished mockups
that lock in unexamined assumptions while research is dismissed as
slow. Since designers can't outrun someone shooting from the hip, they
should use low-fidelity tools like scenario storyboards and PRFAQs to
engage stakeholders on mental models and outcomes.

 CRAFTING WCAG 3 FOR MORE ACCESSIBLE USER EXPERIENCES (5 MINUTE READ)
[13]

 WCAG 3's September 2026 draft sets a single conformance level, called
core requirements, built on WCAG 2.2's Level A and AA success
criteria. Supplemental requirements, assertions, and recommended
practices cover accessibility needs that can't be objectively measured
or don't apply everywhere, encouraging organizations to go beyond the
baseline. Tags let regulators tailor requirements to specific
situations and let websites report progress toward and beyond
conformance, with multiple reporting tiers built in.

 BEST AI IMAGE MODELS 2026: TESTED BY HUMAN EVALUATORS (10 MINUTE
READ) [14]

 Everypixel's blind evaluation of 863 pairwise judgments across 48
commercial scenes found no universal winner among 2026's leading AI
image models. GPT Image 2 showed the strongest evidence for
high-precision commercial generation and controlled photorealism,
though its direct comparison against real photography wasn't
statistically conclusive. Qwen Image 2 suits controlled editing, Nano
Banana 2 fast iteration, Nano Banana Pro consistency, Grok Imagine
creative interpretation, and FLUX.2 an explore-then-finish workflow.
