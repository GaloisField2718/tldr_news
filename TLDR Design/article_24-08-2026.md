 APPLE LAYS OFF 200+ PEOPLE ACROSS VISION PRO AND SIRI TEAMS (3 MINUTE
READ) [4]

 Apple has laid off more than 200 employees across its Vision Pro,
Siri, and software engineering teams as part of a broader
restructuring effort that includes scaling back Vision Pro gaming and
immersive video initiatives while reaffirming its long-term commitment
to visionOS and future hardware. The company is also reorganizing Siri
and AI-related teams around its new Siri AI platform, eliminating some
roles, creating new ones, and shifting resources to accelerate
development of AI-powered features across its products.

 CHATGPT FOR IPHONE NOW LETS USERS GRAB RECENT PHOTOS WITH A LONG
PRESS (2 MINUTE READ) [5]

 OpenAI has added a new iPhone shortcut that lets ChatGPT users
long-press the + button to instantly access and attach their four most
recent photos, streamlining a process that previously required several
taps through the Photos menu. The update reflects the growing
importance of image-based interactions with AI and joins other recent
improvements aimed at making photo uploads, visual questions, and
longer conversations faster and more seamless.

 SLACK HAS (OF COURSE) LAUNCHED A VIBE CODING TOOL (2 MINUTE READ) [6]


 Slack Code is a feature that lets teams tag AI coding agents like
Claude Code, Devin, or GitHub Copilot directly in group chats to build
and ship software fixes. Available now on all Slack plans, it requires
human approval for high-stakes actions like production merges and
archives the channel once work is approved. The launch continues Slack
and Salesforce's broader push to market AI agents as autonomous
digital coworkers, following prior products like Slackbot and
Agentforce Coworker.

 FIGMA IS THE NEW DREAMWEAVER: HOW MODERN PROTOTYPING TOOLS ARE
TRAPPING US IN 2018 (6 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Figma excels at visual design and collaboration, but treating it as a
faithful representation of the web can lead designers to overlook how
interfaces actually behave in real-world conditions. Static artboards
and Auto Layout can mask challenges such as responsive behavior,
loading performance, accessibility, unpredictable content, and dynamic
states. As products become increasingly adaptive and AI-driven,
designers need to think more like systems architects, testing ideas in
code, designing for edge cases, and grounding design systems in
production-ready components rather than purely visual artifacts.

 HOW TO CHECK YOUR AI SYSTEM STILL MATCHES YOUR VALUES (8 MINUTE READ)
[8]

 As AI becomes more deeply embedded in products and services,
designers have a growing responsibility to help ensure that technology
reflects human values, ethics, and societal needs. Building
trustworthy AI requires close collaboration between design, research,
engineering, and governance, with values such as privacy,
transparency, and safety translated into concrete decisions throughout
the development process. A human-centered approach treats design as
the connective thread that helps teams align strategy, implementation,
and outcomes while continuously evaluating whether systems deliver on
the promises made to users and society.

 ONE AI OUTPUT IS AN EXAMPLE, NOT AN EVALUATION (11 MINUTE READ) [9]

 A single AI output only demonstrates a system can perform a task
once—it cannot show how reliably that performance holds across
different situations. Rigorous evaluation, much like quantitative UX
research, requires testing multiple representative inputs across
repeated runs, then reporting averages with confidence intervals to
capture both test-input and run-to-run variability. Systems with
identical overall scores can fail in very different
ways—consistently on specific inputs versus unpredictably across all
inputs—so understanding failure patterns matters as much as the
aggregate score itself.

 A SLOPPY INTERFACE IS A SECURITY LIABILITY (2 MINUTE READ) [13]

 AI now makes it trivial to fake polished interfaces, as seen in
phishing attacks like the one behind the Axios npm incident, raising
interface craftsmanship into a security consideration. High-fidelity
design details—micro-interactions, loading behavior, UI voice,
edge-case handling—are hard and costly to replicate, making sloppy
products easier targets for imitation-based attacks. Investing in
above-average craft therefore serves as both a brand asset and a
practical layer of defense against exploitation.

 VIBE CODING AND QUALITY (2 MINUTE READ) [14]

 Vibe coding was once dismissed as producing only prototype-quality,
"duct-taped" results unsuitable for reliable software. A Mac app built
with Codex reportedly outperformed existing human-made alternatives
within a day, with clear room for further refinement. This experience
led to the conclusion that achieving exacting standards of quality is
realistically possible only through vibe coding, not conventional
programming.
