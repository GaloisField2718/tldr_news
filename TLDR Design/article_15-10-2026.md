 GETTY IMAGES TO GO PRIVATE AFTER RESTRUCTURING DEAL (1 MINUTE READ)
[4]

 Getty Images is planning a prepackaged Chapter 11 filing and will go
private, backed by up to $533 million from the Getty family and Koch
Equity Development. The deal follows a collapsed $3.7 billion
Shutterstock merger and leaves the company, carrying over $1.3 billion
in debt, wholly family- and Koch-owned. Generative AI image tools keep
squeezing the stock-licensing business of both Getty and Shutterstock.


 AI AGENT DEVELOPER MANUS RAISES $500M+ AT REPORTED $4B VALUATION (2
MINUTE READ) [5]

 Manus has raised more than $500 million in a round led by Boyu
Capital, with Tencent participating, at a reported $4 billion
valuation. That figure roughly doubles Meta's reported offer, an
acquisition Chinese regulators blocked in April. The newly released
Manus 2.0 cuts token use by 23.2% on some tasks and adds Cloud
Computer and Cue.

 IOS 27.2 BETA 4 REMOVES APPLE'S REDESIGNED HEALTH APP AND BRINGS BACK
THE OLD DESIGN (2 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Apple has removed its redesigned Health app from iOS 27.2 beta 4,
restoring the existing interface after allowing testers to try the
overhaul in earlier betas. The redesign introduced features including
Insights, Longevity, Health Age, and readiness metrics, but Apple had
already indicated that it would arrive separately later in 2026. Data
collected while testing the new version remains available through
Settings, suggesting the redesign has been delayed rather than
abandoned.

 ON WHAT A CLEAN INTERFACE HIDES (5 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Minimal interfaces can reduce visible complexity while quietly
transferring more work to users, who must discover hidden controls,
interpret ambiguous icons, and infer what happened after an action.
Comparing a hidden bookmark icon with an explicit Save button
demonstrates how adding labels, confirmation, and an Undo action can
make an interface visually busier but cognitively simpler. Good
interface design therefore requires balancing visual restraint with
enough visible information to support newcomers, while potentially
reducing that guidance as familiarity grows.

 EMERGING PATTERNS: DESIGN LEADERSHIP IN 2026 (15 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Research involving 119 design leaders found 93 distinct routes into
the profession, with fewer than 30% having studied in dedicated design
programs and nearly 40% beginning their careers elsewhere. Traditional
career ladders are giving way to more diversified portfolio careers,
while 94% of respondents expect their tool stacks to change
dramatically over the next five years as AI transforms design work.
The qualities that appear most durable amid this uncertainty are not
technical skills but intuition, navigation, adaptability, resilience,
and stewardship, including a growing commitment among established
designers to mentor the next generation.

 WHEN CODE IS NO LONGER THE CONSTRAINT: INSIDE ANTHROPIC'S DESIGN TEAM
(12 MINUTE READ) [9]

 AI has accelerated engineering at Anthropic to the point where
deciding what deserves to be built is becoming a greater constraint
than implementing it, pushing designers closer to production code and
working prototypes. Designers routinely build their own tools,
generate interactive variations using Anthropic's design system, and
collaborate through smaller, more frequent critiques and working demos
rather than lengthy specifications. Technical fluency is increasingly
useful, but Anthropic also values designers who continually reinvent
their workflows, have a point of view about where software is heading,
and can move fluidly between strategic direction and hands-on making.

 THUMBNAILS GIVE A FAST OVERVIEW (15 MINUTE READ) [13]

 Thumbnails let people choose by looking rather than reading, because
a scene's gist registers in about a tenth of a second. They fail when
neighbors look identical or a preview misrepresents the click, so the
two rules are: differ from your neighbors, and resemble your
destination. Fourteen guidelines follow, along with the risks AI
brings when it selects, personalizes, or synthesizes previews.

 EXPERIENCE PLANES – THE BIG 4: WHO, WHAT, WHERE, AND WHY OF
EXPERIENCES (10 MINUTE READ) [14]

 Picking a single lens such as customer, shopping, or brand experience
quietly shapes a design conversation before any design begins. Four
experience planes widen the view: who is involved, what they are
doing, where it happens, and why the organization offers it. Five
examples, from license renewal to food delivery, show frustration
often sitting in the connections between places.
