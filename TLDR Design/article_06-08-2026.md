 LEAK CONFIRMS PIXEL 11 ‘GLOW' IS CALLED ‘HILIGHT' AND WHAT IT CAN
DO (1 MINUTE READ) [4]

 Google's upcoming Pixel 11 will reportedly introduce HiLight
(formerly known as Pixel Glow), a color-changing LED that replaces the
traditional camera flash and discreetly notifies users of calls from
favorite contacts or interactions with Gemini when the phone is face
down. Leaked marketing materials confirm the new branding and suggest
Google is positioning HiLight as a glance-free notification system.
More details are expected at the Pixel 11 launch event next week.

 ARTISTS ARE LAWYERING UP AGAINST AI SLOP, AND SOME ARE EVEN WINNING
(7 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Illustrators, authors, and musicians are increasingly suing AI
companies like Anthropic, Meta, Google, and Suno over unauthorized use
of their work in training models. Some have already secured wins,
notably a $1.5 billion settlement in the Bartz v. Anthropic case over
pirated ebooks, though a judge ruled that training on legally acquired
scanned books counted as fair use. Despite mixed rulings, several
plaintiffs and lawyers say the legal and public-opinion tide is
shifting toward artists, even as concerns persist about creators'
long-term ability to earn a living from their work.

 AWS IS HELPING VIBE-CODING STARTUP SUPERBLOCKS, AND THE IMPLICATIONS
ARE BIG (3 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Superblocks, a vibe-coding startup, signed a multiyear marketing deal
with AWS to embed its tool inside enterprise customers' private
clouds. Under the partnership, apps built with Superblocks will use
Amazon Aurora databases and integrate with Amazon Bedrock, keeping
data internal and under IT's security controls. The deal reflects a
broader push by cloud providers like AWS and Microsoft to convince
enterprises to source AI orchestration and scaffolding tools from them
rather than frontier AI labs.

 HEDONIC ADAPTATION: THE SAME DESIGN SCORES LOWER EVERY YEAR (5 MINUTE
READ) [7]

 Users grow accustomed to good design just as they do to sudden
wealth, so satisfaction scores on an unchanged product tend to slip
roughly 2–5% each year as expectations rise. Chasing novelty through
redesigns, gamification, or variable-reward feeds backfires, since
delight fades quickly while recurring irritations remain persistently
noticeable. The recommended strategy is to fix intermittent
annoyances, benchmark against competitors rather than past scores, and
treat gradual reliability gains as the lasting payoff, not fleeting
delight.

 DESIGNING WITH CODE (5 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Code has become a creative medium at Cursor, where AI agents now
handle implementation while designers focus on directing and iterating
on ideas. A gear-graphic case study shows the process: refining
shapes, spacing, motion, and looping through iterative prompts within
Cursor. Despite AI's speed, initial concept sketching remains a
distinctly human step, with designers retaining responsibility for
taste and judgment.

 I REBUILT OUR PRICING PAGE USING NOTHING BUT CUSTOMER COMPLAINTS (14
MINUTE READ) [9]

 A review of more than 200 pricing-related support tickets revealed
that users weren't frustrated by prices—they were confused by plan
names, feature descriptions, and unclear recommendations. By treating
complaints as research rather than noise, the team identified
recurring patterns, rewrote the pricing page around users' actual
questions, simplified decision-making with clearer plan guidance and
outcome-focused copy, and significantly reduced confusion without
changing pricing. The key lesson is that support tickets often contain
the most valuable UX insights: complaints reveal mismatches between a
product's mental model and users' expectations, making them a powerful
foundation for design decisions.

 BATTLING AI FATIGUE AS A DESIGNER AND DEVELOPER: A PRACTICAL GUIDE (8
MINUTE READ) [13]

 A UI designer and developer noticed AI fatigue while vibe-coding a
personal expenses app, prompting reflection on healthier AI habits.
Key recommendations include limiting AI use outside work, keeping
enjoyable tasks and personal writing human-made, and resisting
pressure toward extreme productivity or constantly adopting new tools.
Stepping away from AI in personal time feels liberating. Chasing 100x
productivity or fear of "falling behind" are misconceptions
unsupported by reality.

 AI FLUENCY ISN'T THE FINISH LINE (4 MINUTE READ) [14]

 Product builders increasingly value AI tool skills, but Figma's 2026
AI report argues collaboration and facilitation matter just as much as
tool fluency. Three practices stand out: building shared internal
tools so individual AI knowledge benefits whole teams, actively
guiding stakeholders toward decisions amid unlimited AI-generated
options, and openly sharing experiments—including failures—to
close AI adoption gaps between colleagues. Tool proficiency remains
necessary but insufficient. The surrounding human skills of building,
deciding, and sharing determine team success.

 APPLE PHOTOS IN IOS 27 GETS CAMERA ROLL FEATURE IT'S LONG NEEDED (1
MINUTE READ) [15]

 iOS 27 adds a new "Captured by Me" collection to the Photos app that
shows only photos and videos taken with your iPhone's Camera app.
Unlike the Recents album, it excludes screenshots, saved images, and
shared media, and it's also available as a filter in the main library.
This finally gives users a true camera roll focused solely on their
own photography.
