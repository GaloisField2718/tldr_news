 CANVA TO CUT MORE THAN 1000 JOBS IN ‘HARDEST MOMENT' OF ITS HISTORY
(4 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Canva is restructuring 1,271 roles, affecting 22 percent of its
workforce, after its growth slowed this year for the first time in a
decade. Chief executive Melanie Perkins called it the hardest moment
in Canva's thirteen-year history, announcing a merged product
organization with fewer management layers. Departing staff receive at
least six months' salary, and the cuts follow Canva slashing its own
valuation to US$31 billion.

 ADOBE PREMIERE MOBILE APP IS GETTING LICENSED MUSIC FROM UNIVERSAL
AND WARNER MUSIC (2 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Adobe is bringing licensed tracks from Universal Music Group and
Warner Music Group into its Premiere mobile app, searchable by genre
and duration. Videos using them can be shared on social media without
copyright strikes, though only for personal use. Each video can
include no more than 60 seconds of a song.

 IOS 27.3 AND MACOS 27.3 ADD VERTICAL TABS, ADAPTIVE DARK MODE, AND
MORE TO SAFARI (3 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Apple is introducing vertical tabs in Safari's developer betas across
iPhone, iPad, and Mac, with tabs displayed as compact icons in a left
sidebar that can expand to show website titles. A new Adaptive Dark
Mode can automatically generate dark versions of websites that lack
native support, either on demand or through a persistent preference.
Siri AI can also organize, sort, move, and close tabs, with broader
awareness of webpage content planned for a future update.

 WHY 10,000 EXPERIMENTS WORK BETTER THAN 10,000 HOURS FOR DESIGNERS (6
MINUTE READ) [9]

 Repeatedly practicing the same design skills can make someone better
at their current job without developing the problem-framing,
influence, and decision-making abilities required for more senior
roles. Instead of relying on experience alone, designers can break
career goals into microskills and run small experiments, such as
opening every design review with a specific decision or presenting
research findings themselves. A weekly reflection can help turn these
experiments into deliberate professional growth without requiring
organizational approval or major changes to existing workflows.

 AGENTS STRUGGLE WITH THE SAME BAD UX WE DO (7 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Computer-use AI agents frequently fail at the same confusing
interfaces that frustrate humans, including ambiguous buttons, hidden
menus, unexplained disabled states, and disruptive overlays. People
often compensate for these problems by guessing, backtracking, or
relying on experience, while agents expose the underlying design debt
through repeatable failures that are easier to observe. Making
interfaces clearer for agents through explicit labels, visible states,
predictable interactions, and semantic structure can also improve
accessibility and usability for people.

 EVERYONE IS BUILDING AI AGENTS. WHO'S DESIGNING THE STOP BUTTON? (17
MINUTE READ) [11]

 AI agents introduce unpredictable behavior into products
traditionally designed around explicit rules and known transitions,
making it essential to distinguish where autonomy is useful from where
reliability is mandatory. Products can let AI interpret intentions and
construct workflows while using deterministic execution, visible
uncertainty, confirmation steps, and clear recovery mechanisms to
protect consequential actions. Designing effective agent experiences
increasingly means defining boundaries around what AI may decide, when
humans must intervene, and how users can understand, correct, or stop
an autonomous process.

MISCELLANEOUS

 BAD AI DOESN'T SMELL (4 MINUTE READ) [15]

 Like a bad survey, bad AI output gives no sign that it's wrong, but
authority bias, truth bias, and algorithm appreciation make people
trust it anyway. In user research, mistranscriptions, missed body
language, misread sarcasm, and hallucinations can turn into confident,
wrong insights. Someone who ran the research has to check any AI
analysis, ideally used only as a sense-check after doing their own.

 TOKENIZER MONO (30 MINUTE READ) [16]

 Tokenizer Mono is an experimental typeface based on IBM Plex Mono
that visualizes how language models split text into tokens, using
OpenType features to highlight boundaries directly as someone types
without running a tokenizer in JavaScript. Its 28 styles represent
different tokenizers from OpenAI, Meta, Google, DeepSeek, and others,
combining contextual glyph substitutions with built-in color palettes
to approximate token boundaries. Most styles achieve roughly 98%
agreement with real tokenizers in testing, demonstrating how advanced
font technology can perform surprisingly complex text processing while
revealing its limitations around line breaks, uncommon words, and
tokenization algorithms.

 HOW AFTERNOW PUT KINETIK'S IDENTITY AND FOUR PRODUCTS INTO ONE SYSTEM
(5 MINUTE READ) [17]

 Afternow redesigned healthcare transportation platform Kinetik around
painterly city landscapes inspired by the view from a car window,
replacing conventional clinical imagery with a warmer visual language
built from purple, deep greens, gold, salmon, and lavender. GT Alpina
provides expressive editorial typography, DM Sans supports accessible
product interfaces, and AI-generated landscapes are carefully
art-directed with documented prompts to maintain consistency. The
identity extends across four products through a shared design system
containing more than 300 components and 2,000 screens, using tokens to
adapt spacing, density, and accessibility for administrators,
transportation providers, and elderly patients.
