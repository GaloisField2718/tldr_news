 XBOX REDESIGNS ITS PC APP AND EXPANDS ITS WEB REFRESH (1 MINUTE READ)
[6]

 The refreshed Xbox PC app reorganizes the library, adds friends to
navigation, and widens search across games, add-ons, and people. On

Game Pass titles, joined by a desktop mode and five high-contrast

 APPLE TV GETS SIGN LANGUAGE INTERPRETATION, AND IT'S A HUGE
ACCESSIBILITY WIN (3 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Apple TV is introducing sign language interpretation for selected
films and shows starting November 13, with American Sign Language
(ASL) and British Sign Language (BSL) support. Viewers can activate
interpretations through a dedicated playback button, identify
compatible titles through visible labels, and discover them using
natural-language search or a curated collection. Each interpretation
is developed and tested with Deaf and native signers, offering an
alternative to captions across Apple devices and supported third-party
platforms.

 ADOBE'S GOOGLE PARTNERSHIP HINTS AT A RADICALLY DIFFERENT FUTURE FOR
PHOTOSHOP (3 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Adobe and Google DeepMind are exploring a customized AI image model
that could enable near-real-time editing in Photoshop, allowing users
to make changes through text prompts and switch between variations
without interrupting their workflow. Adobe is also integrating its
creative tools into Google's AI Mode in Search, potentially connecting
inspiration and content creation without requiring users to open
separate applications. Expanded Gemini and Gemini Enterprise
integrations will support conversational image editing, design
customization, and PDF workflows, although the new Photoshop
capabilities remain experimental.

OPINIONS & TUTORIALS

 COMPLEX ENTERPRISE UX AND UI AT SCALE: THE CASE FOR DESIGN
INFRASTRUCTURE (10 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Enterprises scaling UX and UI should stop asking which design tool to
buy and ask what design infrastructure they need instead.
Machine-readable components and tokens, open standards, and
self-hosting connect design to code, repos, and CI, so one change
propagates everywhere. Closed platforms, by contrast, carry per-seat
costs, manual workarounds, and lock-in, which can force a full rebuild
when switching vendors.

 I LET AI BUILD. I DON'T LET AI DECIDE (9 MINUTE READ) [10]

 AI gets the building work — research, first drafts, prototypes, and
repeat tasks — because each has a check outside itself that exposes
errors within minutes. Deciding stays human: financial choices,
sensitive data, psychological advice, and messages sent in one's name
either lack that check or can't be undone. Three questions sort any
task: name the guideline, check who wrote it, and find whose signature
is on the result.

 WHERE THE DISABLED BUTTON GOT ITS RULE (9 MINUTE READ) [11]

 The familiar convention of graying out unavailable controls traces
back to Apple's Lisa interface guidelines from 1980, when dimmed menu
items could still be selected even though they performed no action. By
1983, Apple had changed the rule so disabled commands could no longer
be activated, a behavior later inherited by Macintosh interfaces and
HTML's disabled attribute. Comparing eight modern design systems
reveals how this convention creates accessibility problems,
particularly when disabled buttons cannot receive keyboard focus or
explain why an action is unavailable, making focusable alternatives
such as aria-disabled useful.

 ON BORROWING APPLE'S TYPE SCALE (7 MINUTE READ) [16]

 Apple's macOS and iOS type scales now drive a personal site's
typography, with interface text at 13 on desktop and 17 on compact or
touch layouts. Numbers outside a four-pixel grid are deliberate, since
a spacing grid can't decide the relationship between kinds of text.
Article bodies keep the larger 17 on desktop too, and a Dynamic Type
fallback lets text scale with system settings on older iPhone and iPad
browsers.

 KOTO POINTS GIFFGAFF TOWARD ITS NEXT CHAPTER WITH AN ARROW-LED
REBRAND (5 MINUTE READ) [17]

 Koto has refreshed UK telecom provider Giffgaff's identity around the
idea of helping customers decide their next move, introducing an arrow
integrated into the final letter of its wordmark. The arrow becomes a
functional graphic device throughout the system, accompanied by acid
green, a custom condensed typeface called Giffsans, and candid
flash-lit photography that reinforces the brand's community-focused
personality. Built on Giffgaff's existing design tokens, the identity
extends across its website, app, packaging, and campaigns while
preserving the challenger brand's recognizable character.

 HOW+HOW CREATES UNIFIED SUB-BRAND SYSTEM FOR PET RETAILER PETS (5
MINUTE READ) [18]

 HOW+HOW has developed a connected brand architecture for UK pet
retailer Pets, bringing its growing services together under a more
coherent identity system. The project establishes distinct
positioning, verbal identities, and visual systems for Pets Insurance,
Pet Pals, and Pets Foundation, allowing each service to communicate
its purpose while maintaining a connection to the parent brand. The
approach addresses the fragmented nature of pet care, where retail,
insurance, veterinary services, and guidance often operate
independently, by creating a more recognizable ecosystem of related
offerings.
