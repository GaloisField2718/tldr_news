 WHATSAPP GROUP CHATS GET PERMANENT MESSAGE PINS, PRIVATE STATUS
UPDATES, AND MORE (3 MINUTE READ) [4]

 WhatsApp is adding four features designed to make busy group
conversations easier to manage, including permanent message pins and
Group Status updates visible only to members of a particular group. A
new filter collects unread conversations containing mentions, direct
replies, or @all messages so users can quickly find messages that need
their attention. Recent message history can also now be shared with
someone after they have joined a group, helping new members catch up
without exposing older conversations automatically.

 THE IPAD IS FINALLY GETTING THE AFFINITY APP CREATIVES HAVE BEEN
ASKING FOR (2 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Affinity's all-in-one creative app is finally coming to iPad, nearly
a year after its desktop debut, answering long-standing demands since
new-format files can't open in the older V2 iPad apps. It combines
photo editing, vector design, page layout, and sketching, with Apple
Pencil support for pressure, tilt, and barrel roll, plus RAW editing
and formats like PSD, AI, INDD, and SVG. The app is free for
individuals despite its “pre-order” label, though Canva AI Studio
features require a premium plan.

 META QUIETLY LAUNCHES AN AI FASHION APP CALLED STYLE SKETCH (2 MINUTE
READ) [6]

 Meta's Style Sketch is an AI app that builds a virtual closet from a
user's Instagram photos and suggests outfits from clothes they already
own. Virtual try-ons use a model reflecting the user's body shape and
skin tone, and trip plans pack outfits around destination, activities,
and weather. The app launched on iOS and Android in Indonesia,
Singapore, and Thailand, with no word on a wider rollout.

OPINIONS & TUTORIALS

 THERE WILL ALWAYS BE SOMEONE FASTER, CHEAPER, AND BETTER (8 MINUTE
READ) [7]

 AI intensifies designers' fears about competing with people who can
work faster, cheaper, or better, but those pressures have existed
throughout the history of professional design and every major shift in
production tools. Faster tools become threatening when designers price
their time rather than outcomes, while “better” extends beyond the
quality of an artifact to the experience, perspective, judgment, and
relationships a designer brings to a client. As AI makes production
faster, deliberately taking time to gain experience, make mistakes,
and develop a singular way of working may become more valuable than
racing to master whichever tool is currently dominant.

 “THIS WASN'T A THOUGHTFULLY DESIGNED MENU.” (4 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Hawaii's 2018 false missile alert demonstrates how poor information
architecture, ambiguous labels, and insufficient friction can turn an
interface mistake into a real-world emergency affecting 1.4 million
people. The alert system reportedly placed confusingly named test and
real commands together without clear hierarchy or confirmation
proportional to the consequences, making a catastrophic selection
surprisingly easy. The same principles apply to everyday software on a
smaller scale: visually similar choices, weak labeling, and poorly
structured menus create countless small errors that gradually erode
usability and trust.

 DESIGN SYSTEMS SHOULD BE FAST BY DEFAULT (18 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Slow design system components make every app built on them slow, so
performance belongs next to accessibility on the team's priority list.
Timing ten popular systems on a 249-country dropdown and 100
checkboxes found Fluent and Carbon quickest to open the dropdown and
Ionic by far the slowest. Playwright tests with explicit budgets,
virtualized long lists, and published performance numbers keep
components fast by default.

 COPUS AND KONTRAPUNKT DESIGN JUNO THE BAKERY'S COOKIE BRAND AND SITE
(4 MINUTE READ) [13]

 Kontrapunkt extended Copenhagen's JUNO the bakery into a restrained
identity and packaging system built around white paper, subtle blue
accents, simple duotone illustrations, and the bakery's blue peacock
mascot. Its Danish Butter Cookies use the same quiet visual language.
COPUS and Tommy Campbell carried the identity into JUNO's first online
shop, using blue sparingly for highlights, product tiles, and the
add-to-cart button to make the digital experience feel like a window
into the physical bakery.

 WEBSITE DESIGN THAT CONVERTS: WHY GOOD-LOOKING SITES STILL FAIL (4
MINUTE READ) [14]

 Striking photography wins attention but fails when visitors cannot
tell what working with a studio involves. Dutch lighting consultancy
Eclectic Lighting bridges that gap with sector-based project filters,
a four-step process, specific client reviews, and a floor plan upload.
Asking for a floor plan visitors already have turns the contact form
into a concrete next step.

 I GAVE MY WEBSITE A BOSS FIGHT (11 MINUTE READ) [15]

 Missing the personality of the old internet, Paul Bakaus rebuilt his
personal website over ten days with Claude Code as a retro Amiga-style
world, using a 4,096-color palette, live-synthesized music, and a boss
fight against a monster called Jirasaurus. Each page became a themed
scene, from a rooftop panel and a speaking auditorium to a floppy-disk
office platformer, and three pages hide playable games. Claude wrote
the code, built the art pipeline, and composed the music, but
animation needed a leg rig and constant feedback, and the site stays a
fast, optional-to-play Ghost theme where reading still comes first.
