 FIGMA EMPLOYEE SUES, ALLEGING COMPANY FORCED HER TO CONFRONT
HARASSERS (5 MINUTE READ) [4]

 A Figma engineer is suing the company and CEO Dylan Field, alleging
managers and HR repeatedly told her to confront the people she accused
of harassment. The suit ties that response to Figma's culture of
"direct feedback" and also alleges racial and gender discrimination.
Figma calls the claims meritless, saying it tolerates neither
harassment nor extortion.

 GOOGLE BRINGS 15-SECOND CLIPS AND SCENE EXTENSION TO FLOW WITH OMNI
1.2 (2 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Google has upgraded its AI filmmaking tool Flow with Gemini Omni 1.2
Flash, enabling users to generate continuous video clips up to 15
seconds long. New capabilities include extending existing clips by up
to 10 seconds per prompt, introducing characters or props through
image references, and restaging scenes while preserving their
composition. Free users receive 50 daily credits with shorter
generation limits, while paid subscribers unlock longer clips,
extensions, and upscaling to 1080p or 4K.

 CARLING'S NEW IDENTITY IS A BADGE OF BELONGING INSPIRED BY ITS
FOOTBALL TIES (3 MINUTE READ) [6]

 BrandOpus has redesigned Carling's visual identity around a
football-inspired crest that celebrates the lager brand's brewing
heritage and longstanding connection to British football culture. The
badge incorporates illustrations of the historic Carling Brewery,
Burton Bridge, barley sprigs, and the signature Black Label banner. A
flexible visual system of illustrations, murals, and stadium
advertising adapts to individual football clubs, reinforcing Carling's
positioning around community, friendship, and belonging.

 TEACH: A PRACTICAL HIERARCHY FOR STYLING DESIGN SYSTEMS (7 MINUTE
READ) [7]

 TEACH orders design-system styling into five layers — tokens,
elements, attributes, classes, and high-level components — starting
with what the browser already provides. Native elements and attributes
like [aria-expanded] or :user-invalid can carry much of the look and
state before any custom class exists. Components sit at the top,
packaging the lower layers into reusable building blocks that need
considerably less custom CSS.

 CRAFT FIRST, AI SECOND (4 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Designers risk becoming reviewers of AI output unless they keep
sketching, iterating, and defining how things should look before
handing work to agents. Automating easy tasks makes the hard parts
harder. A Harvard Business Review survey found 14% of AI-using workers
reporting "AI brain fry." Precise prompts, saved decisions, and a
personal collection of references help close the gap between taste and
execution.

 WHAT UX TASKS WILL AI BE ABLE TO DO, AND WHEN? (24 MINUTE READ) [9]

 AI will outperform humans at every UX task with a verifiable outcome
by 2030, including interface design, research, and usability
evaluation. AI masters tasks in order of how easily and inexpensively
their results can be verified, with UX development estimated to trail
coding by approximately one year. As design execution becomes
increasingly automated, human designers will focus more on identifying
worthwhile problems, setting direction, aligning stakeholders, and
validating outcomes with real users.

MISCELLANEOUS

 THE ERA OF PERSONAL SOFTWARE (7 MINUTE READ) [12]

 A custom health web app, first deployed in about 30 minutes and now
used daily, shows how accessible personal software has become. Hourly
exports feed a PlanetScale Postgres database, while a Grok Bot agent
researches and logs meal calories through an endpoint. Building for
yourself changes the process, since decisions no longer need to work
for anyone else.

 HOW SOMEONE REIMAGINED THE CO-OP BRAND IDENTITY (3 MINUTE READ) [13]

 SomeOne has refreshed Co-op's brand identity by breaking its
recognizable cloverleaf logo into five versatile Membershapes that
symbolize the organization's member-owned structure. The visual system
introduces Co-op Headline, a bespoke typeface developed with F37,
alongside textured letterpress-inspired graphics and expressive icons.
Designed to work across Co-op's food, insurance, funeral, and legal
services, the identity connects its heritage with an ambition to grow
membership from five million to eight million by 2030.

 INTERSTATE UNVEILS “UNAPOLOGETICALLY BRITISH” REDESIGN FOR BYRON
BURGERS (4 MINUTE READ) [14]

 Interstate Creative Partners has rebranded Byron Burgers around a
distinctly British personality, moving away from the American-inspired
conventions dominating the burger restaurant category. The redesign
combines expressive typography, hand-drawn illustrations, bold
blue-and-white graphics, and locally adaptable packaging with a
conversational tone of voice. The repositioning extends to a
simplified menu inspired by British culinary traditions, supporting
the company's ambition to expand from seven to 25 UK locations.
