 ADOBE LIGHTROOM MOBILE CAN NOW OPEN CLOSED EYES IN PHOTOS WITH A
SIMPLE TAP (1 MINUTE READ) [4]

 Adobe's Lightroom mobile app now opens closed eyes with a single tap,
surfacing an Open Eyes Quick Action whenever it detects closed eyes in
a photo. Generative AI matches lighting and pose, drawing on other
photos of the same person in the library, unlike Photoshop Elements,
which borrows eyes from another shot. Free users get two tries before
Adobe prompts them to subscribe to a Creative Cloud plan with more AI
credits.

 GEMINI NOTEBOOK ADDS REVIEW BEFORE GENERATING SLIDES AND VIDEOS (1
MINUTE READ) [5]

 Gemini Notebook now lets users review a slide outline or video
producer notes and request changes before anything renders. With the
optional Review toggle on in the Studio panel, users can ask for edits
like an executive summary or reordered scenes, then select Proceed.
Google announced the update on October 19 and is rolling it out
globally to all users over the following week.

 WHY WE REFRESHED THE N8N BRAND (4 MINUTE READ) [6]

 n8n has refreshed its identity with a new logo, typeface, and refined
color system to reflect its evolution from workflow automation into a
platform for building interconnected workflows and AI agents. The new
mark shifts attention from connected nodes themselves toward the
activity happening between them, while the typography balances
technical precision with the human character of n8n's builder
community. Rather than rebuilding the brand from scratch, the system
preserves recognizable elements such as its signature pink while
creating more flexibility for an expanding product ecosystem.

OPINIONS & TUTORIALS

 THE NEW AGE OF SOFTWARE (2 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Much software remains unbuilt for construction, manufacturing,
logistics, agriculture, and healthcare administration, which still run
on spreadsheets, paper forms, and systems installed around 1999.
Best-paid designers and engineers went to social and ad networks,
leaving industries too small, unsexy, or expensive for traditional
software economics. Small teams can now build specialized tools in
months and proofs of concept in days, and falling switching costs
loosen vendor lock-in from legacy enterprise software.

 HOW META LETS DESIGNERS PROTOTYPE WITH AI IN FORUM (1 MINUTE READ)
[8]

 Meta's designers prototyped Forum, its standalone Facebook Groups
app, with AI in a separate experimental build that used production
components and excluded prototype code from release. The sandbox drew
on the production codebase, frameworks, and design system, so approved
work transferred without rebuilding from scratch. Agents ran build
checks, automated tests, and visual comparisons before engineering
review, and every shipped change required an engineer's approval.

 YOU CAN'T HILL CLIMB YOUR WAY TO "GOOD EXPERIENCE" (6 MINUTE READ)
[9]

 Coding agents excel when success can be evaluated with deterministic
tests, but good user experience cannot be reduced to a metric that can
simply be optimized upward. Interfaces depend on nonlinear
relationships between hierarchy, wording, context, attention,
expectations, and human limitations, meaning hundreds of individually
reasonable changes may still produce something confusing. AI can test
whether an interface technically works, but its ability to read
everything and perceive a whole page at once makes it a poor
substitute for the distracted, imperfect humans who actually need to
use it.

MISCELLANEOUS

 FAST IS A DESIGN DECISION (8 MINUTE READ) [13]

 AI has made websites dramatically easier to build, but generated
experiences can still impose substantial costs on users through slow
servers, excessive JavaScript, poorly prioritized media, ineffective
caching, and other performance problems. Many of those costs originate
in design decisions such as autoplaying video, multiple font weights,
media-heavy carousels, and animations, making performance something
designers should actively budget rather than leave entirely to
engineers. Understanding a few fundamentals around rendering, CDNs,
caching, loading priority, JavaScript, and real-world device
performance lets designers and AI agents make deliberate tradeoffs
between visual ambition and speed.

 SUNLIFE'S NEW LOGO DESIGN IS "OPTIMIZED FOR A DIGITAL WORLD", BUT
DOES IT TAKE MINIMALISM TOO FAR? (4 MINUTE READ) [14]

 Canadian financial services company Sunlife has dramatically
simplified its 25-year-old identity, turning Sun Life into a single
word and reducing its detailed sun symbol to a simple yellow disc. The
redesign aims to create a more contemporary, consistent identity
optimized for digital environments while reflecting the company's
expansion beyond life insurance into health, asset management, and
other services. Its extreme simplicity has renewed debate around
branding minimalism, with the lighter serif wordmark retaining some
distinction while the stripped-back symbol risks the homogeneity
associated with “blanding.”
