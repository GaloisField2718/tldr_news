NEWS & TRENDS

 AT META CONNECT, THE COMPANY'S SMART GLASSES WERE EVERYWHERE (4
MINUTE READ) [4]

 At Meta Connect, hands-on demos showed how Meta's smart glasses could
support everyday tasks through its upcoming Muse integration, from
sending emails to reading to-do lists aloud. The assistant still
struggled to distinguish commands from nearby conversation,
highlighting a challenge for voice-driven interfaces. Meta also
demonstrated a separate $150 pair designed for people with hearing
loss, with adjustable amplification that focuses on the person ahead
or picks up sound from all directions.

 LOVABLE'S ANNUALIZED REVENUE CROSSES $600M AS VIBE CODING TAKES OFF
(2 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Lovable's annualized revenue has crossed $600 million, up from
roughly $500 million in June. Two-thirds of Fortune 500 companies now
use the vibe-coding platform, whose customers include Microsoft,
Nvidia, and Deutsche Telekom. Apps built on Lovable draw nearly a
billion monthly views, and two funding rounds totaling over $700
million lifted its valuation to $13.3 billion.

 APPLE ADDS RAW SUPPORT FOR 11 DIGITAL CAMERAS ACROSS IPHONE, IPAD,
MAC, AND VISION PRO (2 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Apple has expanded RAW image support across iOS, iPadOS, macOS, and
visionOS, adding compatibility for 11 more Fujifilm, OM System,
Panasonic, and Phase One cameras, bringing the total number of
supported models to 562.

OPINIONS & TUTORIALS

 CODE AS THE SOURCE OF TRUTH (2 MINUTE READ) [7]

 With AI tooling, treating code as the shared source of truth lets
design and engineering function as one real team rather than handing
work off in sequence. Designers can prototype real coded components
before engineers finalize them, while engineers push directly into
Figma for designers to refine, provided a subject-matter expert
reviews every step. Pairing engineers and designers to teach each
other's practices speeds up delivery, and, over time, the title lines
between them begin to blur.

 BRAND AS SOFTWARE (19 MINUTE READ) [8]

 In-house brand teams have become service desks: at Ramp, a Slack help
channel logged more than 375 requests in six months. Brand as software
carries a company's point of view, product truths, and design
decisions into the work itself, with artists setting the bar and
builders codifying it. A Slack agent there already turns an attached
doc into a near-final deck in one pass, so designers spend time on
work no system can predict.

 APPLE'S PERFECT IPHONE DUO TIKTOKS NAIL FOLDABLE PHONE MARKETING (2
MINUTE READ) [9]

 Apple is leaning into playful, social-first marketing for the iPhone
Duo with a series of TikTok sketches that showcase the foldable's
versatility in everyday scenarios, from a mini laptop to a portable
movie screen. Unlike Apple's typically minimalist campaigns, these ads
spend more time explaining the product's use cases, reflecting the
challenge of introducing its first foldable into a market where rivals
like Samsung, Huawei, and Honor already have years of experience.

MISCELLANEOUS

 THAT CRISP INTERFACE IS GOING TO DATE (5 MINUTE READ) [13]

 Today's crisp interfaces, with generous white space and a single
decisive CTA, will date the way drop shadows and gradients did.
Software has never been cheaper to ship, yet it keeps yielding
identical landing pages, and AI models serving up the center of the
distribution make it worse. Gumroad, Are.na, Substack, and Nothing
show the alternative: someone had a default available and chose
something else, from oversized type to deliberate friction.

 SHOULD UX DESIGNERS LEARN TO CODE? (11 MINUTE READ) [14]

 Whether UX designers should learn to code depends on context: knowing
a product's technical materials speeds up workflows and suits lean,
bootstrapped teams. Yet coding can invite scope creep, narrow thinking
toward technical constraints, and waste time on languages that quickly
become obsolete. Teams with more T-shaped members, deep in one field
and broadly aware of others, communicate more easily and ship better
products faster.

 FROM YWCA TO HOTEL WILLO: HOW RETHINK SOLVED ONE OF BRANDING'S
TRICKIEST PROBLEMS (5 MINUTE READ) [15]

 Hotel Willo rebranded a Vancouver hotel to overcome outdated
perceptions tied to its former YWCA identity, replacing the name with
a fresh, approachable brand centered on a simple willow-inspired logo.
The identity uses restrained design, playful messaging, and consistent
applications to communicate an affordable, welcoming boutique hotel
while clearly distancing it from past misconceptions.
