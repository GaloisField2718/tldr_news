 WEBFLOW IS NOW AVAILABLE IN CODEX AND CHATGPT (2 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Webflow is now available in Codex alongside built-in Skills for both
ChatGPT and Codex that automate common Webflow workflows. The Skills
package tools, context, and best practices so users describe an
outcome rather than choosing APIs or prompt sequences. Access is
currently free and does not consume AI credits.

 CHATGPT NOW LETS USERS CREATE CUSTOM IMESSAGE AND WHATSAPP STICKERS
(1 MINUTE READ) [6]

 ChatGPT can now create custom sticker packs from photos or text
prompts, allowing users to generate, remix, and edit stickers with
transparent backgrounds before exporting them directly to iMessage or
WhatsApp. Users can also apply styles from reference images, remove or
add elements, and save stickers to their photo library. Once created,
stickers can be added to supported chat apps with a single tap, making
it easier to turn photos, ideas, or inside jokes into shareable
sticker packs.

 CARD SORTING AND TREE TESTING: GETTING YOUR INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE
RIGHT (4 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Card sorting and tree testing are two UX research methods for
building and validating information architecture using observed user
behavior. Card sorting reveals how people naturally group and label
content, while tree testing checks whether they can find things in a
proposed structure. Run in sequence, they turn navigation decisions
into evidence rather than internal debate.

 BUILDING MAY BE CHEAP NOW. BEING WRONG STILL ISN'T (6 MINUTE READ)
[8]

 AI has collapsed the cost of building software but left the cost of
being wrong untouched, making knowing what to build the constraint.
Discovery research answers whether something should exist at all, a
question experiments and rapid prototyping are not built to address.
Use discovery when uncertain about the problem and prototyping when
uncertain about the solution.

 NO SHORTCUTS: WHY AI THREATENS CREATIVE INSTINCT (6 MINUTE READ) [9]

 As AI takes on more of the execution and ideation involved in
creative work, the most valuable skill for designers is shifting from
technical proficiency to judgment—the ability to make thoughtful,
informed decisions about what should be created and why. While new
tools have always changed creative practice, AI differs in its ability
to generate complete outputs with minimal human involvement, making it
easier to bypass the learning experiences that traditionally helped
designers develop taste and critical thinking. The challenge for
educators, practitioners, and organizations is to use AI as a tool for
augmentation rather than replacement, preserving human creativity,
agency, and decision-making while addressing broader concerns around
labor, ownership, and ethics.

 WHERE SHOULD AI GO IN YOUR UI? A UX GUIDE TO AI FEATURE PLACEMENT (8
MINUTE READ) [13]

 Placement shapes how users perceive and adopt AI features, and the
question is not only where AI sits but how prominently. Common
patterns include floating widgets, toolbars and action bars, sidebars,
dedicated hubs, and inline triggers, each signaling something
different. Five questions — centrality, user expectations,
frequency, scope, and whether a menu would do — guide the choice.

 FROM BARBIE LOGOS TO MEDIEVAL MANUSCRIPTS: STEP INSIDE TEDDY BLANKS'
FILM TITLE DESIGN PROCESS (8 MINUTE READ) [14]

 Teddy Blanks, co-founder of Chips, has built a career designing film
titles for directors like Lena Dunham, Greta Gerwig, Robert Eggers,
and David Lowery, treating title sequences less as branding systems
and more as emotional moments that serve the story. His work often
involves deep historical and visual research—from reconstructing
vintage Barbie logos and fonts for Barbie to studying medieval
manuscripts for Werwulf—but he sees titles as a subtle craft whose
value lies in how they feel in a specific cinematic moment rather than
in being noticed. For Blanks, successful title design is about
creating atmosphere, meaning, and connection, even when the audience
never consciously recognizes the work behind it.

 ONEMAY'S SURREAL VISUAL IDENTITY FOR SAM KERR'S WAWA SPARKLING WATER
(3 MINUTE READ) [15]

 OneMay developed the brand identity for WAWA, a sparkling water brand
aiming to make healthy beverages feel exciting rather than
restrictive. Built around the concept “Water With Aura,” the
identity uses a playful wordmark, vibrant color palettes, and surreal
collage-style illustrations to give each flavor its own personality
and emotional appeal. Designed to stand out in a category dominated by
minimalist health cues, the flexible system extends across packaging,
digital content, merchandise, and advertising, positioning sparkling
water as a product people actively choose rather than settle for.
