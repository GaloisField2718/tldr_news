 PINTEREST RESTYLE REIMAGINES YOUR ROOM (2 MINUTE READ) [4]

 Pinterest has unveiled Restyle, an AI-powered feature that lets users
upload a photo of their room and explore how different design ideas
would look in their own space. Users can test furniture, decor, paint
colors, and lighting, remove or swap individual objects, or try a
completely different style. Launching in beta in the US and Canada,
Restyle brings Pinterest Intelligence directly into the consumer
experience, helping people compare options and turn inspiration into
more concrete plans for their homes.

 SAMSUNG STARTS ROLLING OUT ANDROID 17 WITH ONE UI 9, CONFIRMS INITIAL
LIST OF GALAXY DEVICES (2 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Samsung has officially begun rolling out One UI 9, based on Android
17, starting with the Galaxy S26 series after a four-month beta. The
update introduces new Galaxy AI features, including My FanCam,
Creative Studio, Now Brief enhancements, improved privacy and security
tools, and easier access to warranty information. Samsung says the
update will gradually expand to more Galaxy phones and tablets,
including recent S-series, Fold, Flip, and Tab models.

 PUBLISH WEAVE TOOLS TO THE FIGMA COMMUNITY (1 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Figma now lets users build custom Weave tools—generative AI
workflows created in Figma Weave—and publish them to the Figma
Community for others to use directly on the canvas in Figma Design.
These tools support repeatable image and vector generation at scale,
and people can browse the Community to see tools others have built.
While any output type, including video and 3D renderings, can be
published to the Community, only image and vector generation tools
work within Figma Design itself.

 SEVEN LINES OF CLIENT FEEDBACK THAT KNOCK YOU SIDEWAYS, AND HOW TO
STOP THEM LIVING RENT-FREE IN YOUR HEAD (6 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Clients often give confusing or unhelpful feedback because of
uncertainty, internal politics, or a lack of design knowledge rather
than problems with the work itself. Common situations include vague
requests like “make it look less designed,” Canva comparisons,
hidden budgets, feedback by committee, and indecisive comments such as
“I'll know it when I see it.” Responding with calm questions,
clarifying underlying concerns, setting expectations early, and
guiding clients through the design process can turn frustrating
feedback into more productive conversations.

 FIVE ERAS OF UX DESIGN AND THE LESSONS TO KEEP IN THE AI ERA (15
MINUTE READ) [8]

 User experience design has evolved through five major eras—from
Human Factors and the Web to Mobile, Design Systems, and AI—each
contributing a lasting lesson while leaving behind habits that later
became limitations. Designers should focus less on producing
interfaces and more on shaping systems, information architecture,
constraints, and the rules that govern intelligent experiences, as
these are becoming the most valuable skills in the AI era. Rather than
replacing UX, AI continues the historical pattern of making creation
cheaper while shifting designers' value toward higher-level thinking
and decision-making.

 THE INVISIBLE SIDE OF DESIGN ENGINEERING (2 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Design engineering has no fixed definition, and social media makes it
look like little more than building animated eye-candy components.
Day-to-day work leans heavily on invisible priorities like
accessibility, responsiveness, performance, and the edge cases only
real users surface. A great experience often comes down to unglamorous
effort, like spending an afternoon on an accessibility fix or making a
landing page load slightly faster.

 WHY CANVA HIT THE BRAKES ON ITS HUGE AI ROLLOUT (AND WHAT OTHER
BRANDS CAN LEARN FROM IT) (2 MINUTE READ) [13]

 Canva revealed that overwhelming demand for Canva AI 2.0 forced it to
pause the rollout, leading the company to re-engineer the platform and
reduce AI processing costs by nearly 90% while making it five times
faster. Rather than relying on a single AI model, Canva is investing
in a diverse AI strategy focused on delivering reliable, affordable,
and user-friendly experiences that can adapt as AI technology evolves.


 FITTS'S LAW: WHY THE EASY BUTTON WINS (7 MINUTE READ) [14]

 Fitts's Law, developed from 1954 cockpit-control research, holds that
reaching a target gets faster as it grows larger and slower as it sits
farther away. Screen edges and corners act as "magic pixels" that are
effectively infinite in size on desktop, while touchscreens replace
that trick with a thumb-reachable zone. The same principle for making
desired actions easy to click can be inverted into dark patterns, like
oversized subscribe buttons next to tiny cancel links.

 PLANNING WITH AGENTS: DIVIDED WORLDS, BOUNDARY OBJECTS, AND THICKER
INTERFACES (18 MINUTE READ) [15]

 Current agent-planning interfaces trap people in walls of Markdown
text, even though humans read roughly 240 words a minute against an
agent's 4,500. Chuukese wayfinding and Susan Leigh Star's
boundary-object theory frame a case for thicker, visual, interactive,
and social interfaces instead of thin text exchanges. A GitHub Next
prototype called Chopin explores multiplayer, real-time planning where
coworkers and agents share visual, editable plans rather than solitary
text approvals.
