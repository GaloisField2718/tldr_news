 PIXEL 11 PRO FOLD DESIGN LEAKS AHEAD OF GOOGLE LAUNCH EVENT (1 MINUTE
READ) [4]

 Leaked marketing renders from Evan Blass reportedly reveal the
unannounced Pixel 11 Pro Fold, alongside new Pixel Buds and a Pixel
Watch, ahead of Google's launch event. The foldable appears similar to
last year's model, keeping a tall outer screen and 8-inch inner
display, but with a redesigned camera array featuring a glowy light
teased for the Pixel 11 Pro. Google's Made by Google event is set for
August 12th, where these devices are expected to be announced, though
prices may rise.

 A PROPERTIES PANEL AND ANNOTATIONS, NOW IN FIGMA MAKE (4 MINUTE READ)
[5]

 Figma Make now includes a properties panel and annotations, letting
users edit designs visually or with in-context prompts instead of
relying solely on text. The properties panel adjusts spacing,
typography, and layout directly, while annotations let users mark
elements and describe changes like hover effects or animations for the
agent to apply. Both features roll out to all seats and plans, consume
credits, and currently only work with new Make designs, not previously
created ones.

 REDDIT IS TESTING A NEW WAY TO WATCH AND LISTEN TO ITS VIRAL POSTS (1
MINUTE READ) [6]

 Reddit is testing a new TikTok-inspired video experience that would
let users watch videos based on viral Reddit posts and listen to
narrated stories in the background. CEO Steve Huffman said the company
is responding to the popularity of Reddit-story content on platforms
like TikTok, where millions of videos already repurpose Reddit
discussions with text-to-speech narration. The feature is expected to
enter testing later this year, as Reddit continues expanding its video
offerings following the recent launch of video comments.

 JAKOB'S LAW: HOW TO APPLY IT AS AI COLLAPSES SURFACES INTO ONE CHAT
BOX (15 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Jakob's Law still applies in the AI era, but the reference point has
shifted from websites to AI assistants like ChatGPT, Claude, and
Copilot. As users increasingly start tasks through conversational
interfaces rather than individual apps, products need to be
discoverable, understandable, and usable through assistants by using
clear naming, structured data, standard UI patterns, and protocols
like MCP and A2UI. Rather than designing only for users who visit an
app directly, designers should also optimize for products that work
behind the scenes, with AI assistants becoming the primary interface
for many tasks.

 YOUR PEOPLE GET AI. GET OUT OF THEIR WAY (12 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Despite widespread investment in AI, 95% of corporate AI projects
fail, not because of the technology but because bureaucracy, excessive
approvals, and a lack of trust prevent capable employees from using it
effectively. Smaller, empowered teams can make fast decisions and
focus on solving real problems, while large organizations often reward
control over judgment, creating "organizational debt" that slows
innovation. The companies that will benefit most from AI won't be
those with the best tools - they'll be the ones that trust their
people, remove unnecessary layers of management, and let experienced
employees openly experiment, share what works, and make decisions.

 HOW TO REDUCE AI DRIFTING IN DESIGN (7 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Design systems drift when Figma components and front-end code fall
out of sync, and AI often widens that gap rather than closing it. A
proposed fix uses "component contracts" — plain-language specs that
become the shared source of truth — paired with harness engineering,
deterministic guardrails surrounding an AI model. Testing showed an AI
agent scored 100/100 building screens from a contract catalog, versus
69/100 when ungoverned.

MISCELLANEOUS

 FOR 100 YEARS NO ONE NOTICED IT, BUT THIS REBRAND FINALLY GIVES
GUNNERSBURY MUSEUM A REASON TO BE FOUND (6 MINUTE READ) [13]

 Gunnersbury Museum and Park has introduced a new modular identity by
Wiedemann Lampe to solve a long-standing visibility problem: most
people knew the park but not the museum. The system uses separate
symbols and colors—a green tree for the park and a purple building
for the museum—that combine into a yellow identity when representing
both together, giving each its own voice while maintaining a cohesive
brand. Supported by flexible illustrations, human-focused photography,
and intuitive yellow wayfinding, the rebrand is designed to increase
awareness, encourage exploration, and help the historic site become a
more recognizable cultural destination.

 BETHESDA'S NEW LOGO DESIGN BRINGS PEAK GAMING NOSTALGIA WITH A NOD TO
ITS ICONIC ORIGINAL BRANDING (4 MINUTE READ) [14]

 To celebrate its 40th anniversary, Bethesda has introduced a new
retro-futuristic logo that replaces its clean, corporate identity with
one inspired by the company's original 1980s and 1990s branding. The
redesign brings back the "Bethesda Softworks" name, uses an angular
cyberpunk-inspired typeface reminiscent of its early years, and aims
to better reflect the publisher's long history and personality. Fans
have overwhelmingly praised the change, seeing it as a welcome return
to Bethesda's roots and a reminder that distinctive branding can be
more memorable than generic minimalism.

 GOOGLE'S SYNTHID WATERMARK IS HARD TO BREAK, BUT IT DOESN'T SOLVE AI
DISINFORMATION (9 MINUTE READ) [15]

 Google's SynthID watermark survived 300 rounds of simulated
compression and resizing in testing, though cropping combined with
heavy compression eventually defeated it. Even so, watermarking can't
solve AI disinformation broadly, since fragmented detection systems,
usage limits, and unlabeled open-source models leave huge gaps.
Experts argue the better long-term strategy is authenticating genuine
content via cryptographic metadata like C2PA rather than trying to
flag every piece of synthetic media.
