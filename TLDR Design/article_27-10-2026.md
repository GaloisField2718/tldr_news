 FIGMA ADDS ENTERPRISE AI CREDIT CONTROLS AND ADMIN APIS (1 MINUTE
READ) [4]

 Enterprise admins in Figma can now set default paid AI credit access
per user, choosing a monthly limit, full access, or none across
organizations or billing groups. The limits cover purchased credits
only and reserve nothing, so shared pools can still run dry. New admin
REST APIs for teams, folders, and files, plus a block on employees
opening Starter or Professional plans with company emails, round out
the update.

 APPLE UNVEILS ALL-NEW IPAD MINI: OLED, THINNER DESIGN, MORE (3 MINUTE
READ) [5]

 Apple's biggest iPad mini update yet introduces a slightly larger
8.5-inch OLED display with 24% thinner bezels, up to 1,200 nits of
peak brightness, and an optional nano-texture finish. The 5.4mm-thick
design adds IP67 water and dust resistance, an A20 Pro chip, a
landscape 12MP Center Stage camera, Acoustic Display audio, faster
connectivity, and fast charging. Pre-orders begin October 28 ahead of
a November 6 release, with pricing starting at $699 for 128GB.

 THESE CLEVER APPS SHOW OFF THE IPHONE DUO'S POTENTIAL (9 MINUTE READ)
[6]

 Developers are creating playful apps built around the iPhone Duo's
folding design. Deck-Man turns albums into virtual cassettes, letting
users load a tape with the phone open and close it to listen, complete
with mechanical playback sounds. TypeDraw uses the fold as a notebook
cover, with customizable paper, drawing tools, and fonts based on the
user's handwriting.

 HOW TO PUT YOUR DESIGN SYSTEM TO WORK WITH THE FIGMA AGENT (5 MINUTE
READ) [7]

 A connected design library gives the Figma agent approved components
and tokens, while guidelines attached to it encode rules, exceptions,
and which variant to pick. Examples on an Examples page or attached to
a prompt show how pieces combine, and reusable skills like a
/design-system-audit handle repeat processes. Recurring agent mistakes
signal that guidelines, library, or examples need updating.

 ENTERING ATOMIC DESIGN FROM THE SCREEN (10 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Atomic Design does not have to begin with small reusable components
and work upward, as product teams can instead start with complete
screens and discover their underlying structure from user behavior. A
scope-first approach identifies what users can do, determines which
data or interface region each action affects, and assigns
responsibility for that logic before defining components. This helps
reveal relationships that a simple interface inventory misses,
producing components and templates based on behavioral boundaries
rather than visual proximity alone.

 AI UI DESIGN PROMPTS: DESCRIBE THE INTERFACE YOU WANT (10 MINUTE
READ) [9]

 AI coding tools produce more distinctive interfaces when prompts
describe not only a broad visual style but also typography, layout,
hierarchy, density, colors, borders, and how specific interface
regions should relate to each other. Applying Swiss-inspired,
editorial, developer-workbench, and neo-brutalist directions to the
same test-results viewer demonstrates how identical functionality can
produce radically different compositions while preserving content and
behavior. Effective iteration comes from evaluating the rendered
interface against the task, then giving concrete feedback about
problems such as oversized headings, misplaced controls, or wasted
space rather than relying on vague aesthetic instructions.

 OPTIONS ARE EXPENSIVE. NOT HAVING THEM HAS A COST TOO (3 MINUTE READ)
[14]

 Oxygen is adding a setting that lets KDE users choose between
symbolic and colorful action icons, but supporting that seemingly
simple preference requires redrawing hundreds of icons individually at
multiple sizes. Different pixel dimensions require distinct
proportions and levels of detail rather than straightforward scaling,
turning one interface option into thousands of assets that must be
designed, tested, and maintained. User choice carries a substantial
hidden cost, but removing choice also has consequences when people
have different visual preferences, habits, and accessibility needs.

 CONRAN DESIGN GROUP REBRANDS LUXURY ACCOMMODATION PROVIDER CHEVAL (4
MINUTE READ) [15]

 Conran Design Group has repositioned luxury accommodation provider
Cheval around “Generous by design,” shifting the brand from a
product-led proposition toward experiences defined by space, time, and
attention. A new elongated C containing two horses references Cheval's
Knightsbridge origins, while Season typography, deep teal, bespoke
property emblems, and flag-inspired signage replace more generic
black, white, and gold luxury conventions. The flexible identity
supports Cheval's expansion across serviced apartments and branded
residences while giving individual properties room to express their
distinct locations and character.

 MASANI PIZZA'S IDENTITY CELEBRATES THE FAMILY THAT OWNS IT (5 MINUTE
READ) [16]

 Saint-Urbain built Masani Pizza's identity around the personality and
family behind the restaurant, giving the brand a warm, characterful
presence rather than relying on familiar Italian restaurant
conventions. Expressive typography, illustrations, photography, and
playful graphic details create a visual language that feels personal
and rooted in the people running the business. The resulting system
turns family character and hospitality into the restaurant's primary
point of distinction across packaging, signage, merchandise, and other
touchpoints.
