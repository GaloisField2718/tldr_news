NEWS & TRENDS

 SAMSUNG MAY BE GOING IN FOR THE FULL IOS-ESQUE LIQUID GLASS LOOK IN
ONEUI 9.5 (3 MINUTE READ) [4]

 Leaks suggest Samsung is exploring a glass-inspired redesign for One
UI 9.5, featuring shinier edges, transparency effects, background
blurring, greater visual depth, and possibly a new App Lock feature.
While the update remains unconfirmed and likely won't arrive until
2027, it indicates Samsung may be pursuing a more visually expressive
interface rather than simply refining its current design.

 WHATSAPP WORKING ON CUSTOMIZABLE EMOJI REACTION SETS ON IOS (1 MINUTE
READ) [5]

 WhatsApp is testing a feature that would let users customize the six
default emoji reactions shown when reacting to messages, replacing
them with emojis of their choice and restoring the defaults at any
time. The feature is not yet available to beta testers but appears to
be in development for both iOS and Android, with a possible tie-in to
WhatsApp Plus.

 ADOBE'S AI COLLABORATORS TURN OUTSIDE AI AGENTS INTO GOVERNED
WORKFRONT TEAMMATES (4 MINUTE READ) [6]

 Adobe has made AI Collaborators generally available in Workfront,
letting users delegate tasks to outside AI agents from Microsoft
Copilot Studio, Anthropic's Claude, and Writer as governed,
permissioned teammates. These collaborators—task agents and
brand-compliance reviewers now live, with a project coordinator coming
soon—receive full project context automatically and return work for
human review and approval. Alongside this, Adobe also launched
Workfront MCP, enabling users to bring Workfront project data into
Claude, Copilot, Gemini, or ChatGPT.

OPINIONS & TUTORIALS

 GETTING STARTED IS NOT GETTING IT RIGHT (9 MINUTE READ) [7]

 AI excels at overcoming the blank-page problem and generating first
drafts, but significant human effort is still required to verify
outputs, catch hallucinations, and ensure quality, especially for
complex or specialized work. While it can accelerate some tasks, it
often introduces new costs through fact-checking, rework,
accessibility issues, and subtle inaccuracies that become harder to
spot as models improve. Organizations will get more value from AI by
defining where it is reliable, where human review is mandatory, and
where expert-led work remains the better option.

 RETHINKING DESIGN LEADERSHIP WITH SWARMS AND FLOCKS (10 MINUTE READ)
[8]

 High-performing teams work best when they combine local autonomy with
a small set of shared rules, allowing people to adapt quickly without
losing alignment or direction. Leadership should focus less on
controlling decisions and more on enabling communication, rotating
responsibility, protecting team health, and reinforcing a few core
principles that guide the work. Regularly pausing to improve systems,
reduce technical or design debt, and invest in learning helps teams
maintain speed and resilience over the long term.

 YOUR UX TEAM IS SET UP FOR THE WRONG JOB (6 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Everyone across an organization is now designing using AI tools,
which means UX teams need to shift roles rather than absorb more
direct production work. Instead of routing all design through a small
team, successful organizations move designers upstream to build design
systems, playbooks, research repositories, and AI-briefing standards
that guide everyone else's output. Cutting design teams to cut costs
saves money short-term but creates inconsistency, accessibility
issues, and rework, so success should be measured by adoption of these
resources rather than throughput.

 OTHER BRANDS ARE THROWING SHADE AT THE NEW INSTAGRAM LOGO (2 MINUTE
READ) [12]

 Instagram has replaced its 10-year-old wordmark with a cleaner, more
digital-looking version, a change that has sparked criticism for being
less distinctive and harder to read. The company says the update was
simply due to the logo's age, prompting jokes from other brands about
redesigning their own long-standing logos for the same reason. While
some users welcome the refresh, many see it as a cosmetic change that
does little to address broader perceptions of the platform.

 FINDING AND FIXING DESIGN SYSTEM DRIFT (7 MINUTE READ) [13]

 Design drift — the gap between Figma files and shipped product —
is inevitable in fast-moving teams and stems from entropy, not
negligence or failure. Screen drift, involving easily spotted
rendering differences, differs from component drift, which involves
underlying changes requiring human judgment. AI can assist with visual
diffing to catch the former. Code represents "now" while Figma
represents "next," and scheduled reconciliation between the two lets
Figma regain its authority for planning future changes.

 VIBE CODED APPS ARE THE NEW SHADOW IT (7 MINUTE READ) [14]

 AI-generated internal tools now create infrastructure with IAM roles
and no ticket, review, or security involvement, unlike traditional
SaaS-based shadow IT. A layered defense is proposed: platform controls
prevent risky defaults, while process controls require automated
baseline checks and security-informed human review before deployment.
Cloud security posture management tools and behavioral telemetry
signals serve as a detection safety net for tooling that slips through
preventive layers undetected.
