 PAPER RAISES $34M TO BUILD THE NEXT GREAT DESIGN PLATFORM (2 MINUTE
READ) [4]

 Paper raised $34M in Series A funding from Accel and ICONIQ, bringing
its total funding to $38.5M, to build a design platform for the
agentic era. Following its Paper Desktop launch, the company saw 25x
ARR growth in a month and now serves thousands of paying customers,
including Ramp, Lovable, and Harvey. Paper plans to use the funding to
expand its editor and agent infrastructure, positioning itself as
infrastructure for agents while continuing open-source projects like
Paper Shaders and Paper Mono.

 IYO'S TRADEMARK LAWSUIT AGAINST OPENAI AND JONY IVE PAUSED OVER
SETTLEMENT TALKS (2 MINUTE READ) [5]

 OpenAI, Jony Ive's hardware venture, and iyO have reached a
settlement in principle in their trademark and trade secret dispute,
prompting the court to pause proceedings for seven days while the
agreement is finalized. If the settlement is completed by August 6,
the lawsuit will be dismissed. Otherwise, the parties must provide a
status update and continue with scheduled hearings. The agreement
could also resolve the ongoing dispute over OpenAI's use of the
“io” branding for its future AI hardware.

 AS AI CONTENT FLOODS THE INTERNET, PANGRAM RAISES $9M TO DETECT IT (4
MINUTE READ) [6]

 Pangram has raised $9 million to expand its AI detection tools as
AI-generated content spreads across the internet, academia, legal
work, and social platforms. The startup launched Pangram 4, which it
says is over 99% accurate at detecting AI-assisted and mixed human-AI
writing, along with a research-preview image detector that can
identify AI-generated visuals across different models. While the tools
are not perfect, Pangram is positioning itself as a way to preserve
human-made content and add transparency around when AI has been used.

OPINIONS & TUTORIALS

 UX-CONTEXT DESIGN: USING UX KNOWLEDGE TO INFORM AI-GENERATED DESIGN
(8 MINUTE READ) [7]

 UX-context design reframes research and design deliverables as
curated, machine-readable context that guides AI tools rather than
documents meant for human interpretation. Google's DESIGN.md
exemplifies this shift for visual identity, while a hypothetical
broader file, UX.md, could similarly encode research findings,
interaction standards, glossaries, and user/world models for AI
consumption. Unlike traditional handoffs, this context lives alongside
the product, continuously updates, and is read by every AI tool
whenever anyone in an organization generates work.

 THINKING OUTSIDE THE BOX: DIGITAL DESIGN IN THE AI ERA (5 MINUTE
READ) [8]

 MacPaw explored moving beyond typical text-box AI interfaces, leading
to Eney, a proactive assistant designed as an expressive character
rather than a chatbot. Designers chose a circular pink figure with
minimal facial features, prioritizing warmth, approachability, and
restrained emotional expression over flashy or distracting UI. Despite
AI streamlining design work, human judgment, artistic intent, and
curiosity remain essential and increasingly valuable in the AI era.

 HOW DESIGN LEADERS INFLUENCE DECISIONS WITHOUT BEING IN THE ROOM (3
MINUTE READ) [9]

 As AI-generated interfaces make visual quality easier to achieve,
designers can no longer rely on polished screens alone to communicate
the value of their work. A more effective approach is to annotate
presentations with three elements: the design decision, the hypothesis
behind it, and the outcome it produced, making the strategic thinking
explicit. This framework helps stakeholders understand not just what
changed, but why it mattered and how it impacted users or the
business.

 DESIGNING FAST AND SLOW (3 MINUTE READ) [14]

 Designers often favor deliberate, evidence-based decision-making,
while business partners tend to move quickly on instinct. This creates
a "chess versus poker" divide: designers treat decisions as costly and
hard to undo, while business leaders treat them as cheap, disposable
bets. The real question isn't whether to think fast or slow, but
whether a given decision is truly reversible and worth the cost of
caution.

 DIGITAL TOOLS, HUMAN EXPRESSION: THE VISUAL IDENTITY BEHIND CONFIG
2026 (9 MINUTE READ) [15]

 Figma's Brand Studio built Config 2026's visual identity around
evolution, fluidity, and harmony. The team used Figma Make to create
glyphs, dithering effects, and scribbly textures, then translated them
into physical installations, motion pieces, wayfinding signage, and
merchandise. Interactive elements like Shared Frequency, the Play
Canvas, and Collage Remixer let attendees personalize prints and
designs, extending the theme of imperfect, human-driven creativity
into tangible keepsakes and audio.

 ACCEPT & PROCEED'S NEW SYSTEM FOR VANS IS A LESSON IN HOW TO INJECT
CLARITY INTO AN ICONIC BRAND (2 MINUTE READ) [16]

 Vans has introduced a refreshed brand system that unifies its
previously fragmented identity around the idea “Our Canvas. Your
Way,” celebrating the brand as a platform for personal expression.
Rather than redesigning iconic assets, Accept & Proceed clarified what
should remain consistent while creating a more flexible framework for
future teams, products, and communities. The update also refines the
brand's typography and guidelines, giving Vans a more cohesive
identity without losing the authenticity that made it culturally
significant.
