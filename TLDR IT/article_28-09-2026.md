NEWS & TRENDS

 OPENAI AGENTS UPLOADED USER IMAGES TO THE PUBLIC INTERNET WITHOUT THE
COMPANY'S KNOWLEDGE (5 MINUTE READ) [4]

 AI agents operating in OpenAI's research environment uploaded
user-provided images to public image-hosting sites without the
company's knowledge. OpenAI identified 53 instances, underscoring a
new class of enterprise AI risk: autonomous agents with access to
sensitive data can take unintended external actions even when they
aren't explicitly instructed to do so.

 KITEWORKS TELLS CUSTOMERS TO SHUT DOWN SERVERS OVER THREAT OF AN
IMMINENT ATTACK (4 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Secure file-sharing provider Kiteworks took the unusual step of
asking customers to temporarily shut down servers after receiving
intelligence about a potentially imminent cyberattack. The precaution
is particularly notable given Kiteworks' role in handling sensitive
enterprise file transfers and communications.

ANALYSIS & OPINIONS

 THE ENTERPRISE NEEDS TO KILL THE "SSO TAX" (5 MINUTE READ) [6]

 The long-running practice of SaaS vendors reserving SSO for expensive
enterprise plans effectively makes companies pay extra for a basic
security control. The model needs to change, as identity becomes even
more important in an AI- and SaaS-heavy workplace, citing data that
37% of enterprise SaaS applications remain outside SSO.

 ENTERPRISE AI CODING AGENTS ARE BECOMING AN IT PROCUREMENT PROBLEM
TOO (7 MINUTE READ) [7]

 A comparison of GitHub Copilot, AWS Kiro, Cursor, and Cognition
highlights how enterprise evaluation of coding agents now extends well
beyond developer productivity. IP indemnification, data residency,
prompt retention, audit logs, and enterprise pricing are increasingly
becoming security, legal, and IT considerations when these tools are
deployed at scale.

 AI AGENTS EXPOSE A SECURITY GAP BETWEEN THE DATA THEY CAN READ AND
THE SYSTEMS THEY CAN CHANGE (6 MINUTE READ) [8]

 Traditional application permissions weren't designed for autonomous
agents that can read information in one system, reason about it, and
then take actions somewhere else. Enterprises need to think beyond
model security and start controlling the full agent execution path:
identity, data access, tools, and actions.

LAUNCHES & PARTNERSHIPS

 MICROSOFT ADDS AN IT-GOVERNED RUNTIME FOR EMPLOYEE-BUILT AI APPS AND
AGENTS (5 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Copilot Managed Runtime gives organizations infrastructure for
running apps created through Copilot Code, Cowork, and Copilot Studio
inside the Microsoft 365 environment. Microsoft is also introducing a
centralized plugin registry where IT can approve and manage plugins,
pointing toward a future where managing employee-created agents may
become another core IT responsibility.

 SALESFORCE UNVEILS KOA, A REASONING MODEL BUILT SPECIFICALLY FOR CRM
(4 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Salesforce has introduced Koa, a reasoning model designed
specifically for CRM workloads and Agentforce. Rather than relying
entirely on general-purpose models, Salesforce is betting that
domain-specific models can better understand customer data, workflows,
and the business context required by enterprise agents.

MISCELLANEOUS

 AI FLUENCY ISN'T ENOUGH - COMPANIES STILL NEED HUMAN JUDGMENT (5
MINUTE READ) [11]

 EY's Global Vice Chair argues that organizations shouldn't confuse
widespread AI adoption with eliminating the need for human expertise.
As AI handles more analysis and routine work, judgment,
accountability, and knowing when not to trust an AI-generated answer
may become more valuable workplace skills rather than less.

 AMAZON BEDROCK GETS BETTER VISIBILITY INTO WHO'S ACTUALLY SPENDING
YOUR AI BUDGET (4 MINUTE READ) [12]

 AWS has added IAM-principal-level visibility that can help
organizations break down Bedrock AI spending by team, project, or
application. As companies move from a handful of AI experiments to
dozens of internal agents and applications, attributing model
consumption to an actual owner is becoming a very practical FinOps
problem.
