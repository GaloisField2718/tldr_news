NEWS & TRENDS

 OPENAI'S JALAPEÑO AI CHIP IS OPTIMIZED FOR INFERENCE EFFICIENCY (3
MINUTE READ) [4]

 OpenAI has revealed more details about Jalapeño, its first custom AI
accelerator built with Broadcom. The chip is rated at 700W but stayed
at or below 550W in tested workloads, as OpenAI pushes to reduce
inference costs and power use.

 OPENAI GPT-5.6 TERRA AND LUNA NOW AVAILABLE ON AMAZON BEDROCK IN AWS
GOVCLOUD (US) (1 MINUTE READ) [5]

 OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Terra and Luna models are now generally available
through Amazon Bedrock in AWS GovCloud (US-West) and (US-East). Terra
targets balanced performance while Luna emphasizes faster, lower-cost
inference. Both offer million-token context windows and support prompt
caching with a stated 90% discount for repeated context.

 CISCO ADDS SUPERMICRO SERVERS TO ITS NVIDIA AI FACTORY STACK (4
MINUTE READ) [6]

 Cisco is adding Supermicro's liquid- and air-cooled systems to its
Secure AI Factory with NVIDIA. The expanded stack targets enterprises,
neoclouds, and sovereign clouds deploying increasingly dense GPU
infrastructure.

ANALYSIS & OPINIONS

 MCKINSEY SAYS ENTERPRISE AI IS FINALLY MOVING TOWARD ROI (5 MINUTE
READ) [7]

 Enterprise AI investment keeps climbing, but measurable earnings
impact remains limited. McKinsey's latest analysis suggests companies
are beginning to find clearer paths to ROI as deployments move into
redesigned workflows.

 SAAS ISN'T DEAD. SAMENESS IS (15 MINUTE READ) [8]

 SaaS is not disappearing, but cheap AI-assisted software generation
will weaken the standardized applications and seat-based pricing that
defined it. Vendors may instead provide underlying services such as
authoritative data, compliance, security, integrations, networks, and
operational accountability, while customers create more tailored
applications for smaller internal audiences.

 PERPLEXITY AND NVIDIA LAUNCH AN AI AGENT THAT RUNS LOCALLY (5 MINUTE
READ) [10]

 Perplexity's new Portable Computer runs agent workloads locally on
Nvidia DGX Spark and RTX-powered Linux machines, avoiding token
charges for local work. Tasks remain on-device by default and can move
to cloud models when needed.

 ORACLE EXADATA DATABASE@AWS OFFERS ADVANTAGES, BUT TRADE-OFFS REMAIN
(8 MINUTE READ) [11]

 Oracle has made Exadata Database Service generally available through
Oracle Database@AWS, extending its high-performance database platform
into AWS datacenters. Licensing experts say the service can reduce
processor-license requirements and suit license-constrained customers,
but higher compute and storage costs, less granular capacity, and
Oracle sales claims may undermine its value compared with native AWS
options.

 ENTERPRISE-MANAGED AUTH FOR THE SUPABASE MCP SERVER (2 MINUTE READ)
[12]

 Supabase has generally released enterprise-managed authentication for
its MCP server on Team and Enterprise plans, built with Anthropic and
Okta. The feature lets an administrator authorize Supabase in Claude
once and manage access through the organization's identity provider,
while each employee retains the projects, role, and permissions they
already have in Supabase.

MISCELLANEOUS

 GOOGLE ADDS AI ASSESSMENTS TO ITS CLOUD MIGRATION CENTER (4 MINUTE
READ) [13]

 Google is adding AI-powered assessments to Migration Center to
automate parts of cloud migration planning and business-case
development. The tools aim to speed up early analysis before deeper
technical validation.

 OPEN-WEIGHT MODELS ARE GAINING GROUND IN ENTERPRISE AI (6 MINUTE
READ) [14]

 Open-weight models are moving from developer experimentation into
enterprise production workloads, according to usage data cited from
Vercel's AI Gateway. Cost advantage, growing role in multi-model
deployments, managed inference availability, developer adoption, and
suitability for agentic workloads could make open-weight models a
durable part of enterprise AI without requiring widespread
self-hosting.
