NEWS & TRENDS

 MICROSOFT AND INTEL DEEPEN COLLABORATION TO DELIVER DIFFERENTIATED
AZURE INFRASTRUCTURE (4 MINUTE READ) [4]

 Microsoft and Intel have announced an expansion of their
collaboration under a long-term agreement to advance Azure cloud and
AI infrastructure. Microsoft will become the first company to use
Intel Foundry's EMIB advanced packaging technology for its first-party
silicon and secure multi-year Intel Xeon supply capacity, including
the upcoming Diamond Rapids server CPUs. The partnership also covers
system-level optimizations using CXL memory, Intel QuickAssist
Technology, and Intel Trust Domain Extensions for confidential
computing.

 PALO ALTO NETWORKS STRIKES $4.5 BILLION DEAL FOR APPLIED COMPUTE (3
MINUTE READ) [5]

 Palo Alto Networks agreed to acquire Applied Compute for $4.5 billion
in stock, adding tools for training and serving customized open-weight
AI models. The deal is expected to close in its fiscal third quarter
of 2027 and will support deployments across public and sovereign
clouds.

 WORKDAY CREATES EUROPEAN ENTITY TO GOVERN SOVEREIGN CLOUD (3 MINUTE
READ) [6]

 Workday plans to establish an Ireland-based company with an
EU-citizen board to operate its EU Sovereign Cloud, keeping governance
and operational decisions in Europe. The service runs on AWS European
Sovereign Cloud, with the new entity and additional safeguards
expected to become operational in early 2027.

ANALYSIS & OPINIONS

 IT LEADERS ARE DECIDING WHICH DECISIONS MACHINES CAN MAKE ON THEIR
OWN (17 MINUTE READ) [7]

 IT leaders and chief information officers are navigating the
challenges of deploying autonomous IT and generative AI tools in
high-security environments. While rules-based automation and large
language models help synthesize large volumes of data and accelerate
cybersecurity defenses, agencies face significant hurdles regarding
data readiness, over-trust in AI results, and classifying sensitive
information. Organizations are addressing these challenges by
establishing localized large language models, data governance
structures, and strict human-in-the-loop checkpoints.

 LEGACY SOFTWARE MAY BE HOLDING BACK ENTERPRISE AI (4 MINUTE READ) [8]


 Google Cloud's Jeanette Manfra argues that maintenance burdens and
vendor lock-in are limiting organizations' AI ambitions. She cites
Google-commissioned research in which 78% of surveyed technology
leaders said maintaining legacy systems diverts resources from AI, and
advocates greater vendor choice and stronger security requirements in
procurement.

MISCELLANEOUS

 INTRODUCING AGENT CONTROLS: AN ENTERPRISE CONTROL PLANE FOR AGENT
BEHAVIOR (10 MINUTE READ) [12]

 Microsoft has introduced agent controls, a managed Azure service
designed to help enterprises centrally define, manage, and evaluate
policies governing AI agent behavior. Built on the open-source Agent
Control Specification and Agent Hooks, the service brings runtime
governance to heterogeneous AI agent fleets without requiring
organizations to build custom policy infrastructure. It evaluates
agent actions and content using content safety classifiers, LLM
judges, and deterministic Rego rules.

 SASHIKO: LESSONS FROM BUILDING AN AGENTIC CODE REVIEW SYSTEM FOR
LINUX (11 MINUTE READ) [13]

 Google's AI and Infrastructure team built Sashiko, an open-source,
agentic code review system powered by Gemini designed to help
maintainers review Linux kernel patches. The system separates bug
finding from verification using parallel specialist sessions and
per-bug deep checks. Data from seven months of production use on
kernel mailing lists show that Sashiko completes about 1,200 reviews
daily and correlates with a 35 percent drop in 60-day follow-up fixes
for merged commits.
