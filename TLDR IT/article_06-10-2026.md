 HUAWEI AND QUALCOMM ANNOUNCE BROAD PATENT LICENSE AGREEMENT (3 MINUTE
READ) [4]

 Huawei and Qualcomm announced a multi-year, broad patent license
agreement featuring cross-licenses across technology fields including
5G, compute, AI, and networking. Additionally, the agreement includes
Qualcomm's purchase of certain Huawei US patents in compute, AI, and
networking. The transaction is pending necessary regulatory approvals.


 REFLECTION UNVEILS 501B-PARAMETER AI MODEL BEAM (3 MINUTE READ) [5]

 Beam is a 501-billion-parameter model that Reflection says competes
with leading Chinese models while requiring substantially less
compute. Access initially runs through an early-access program, with
model weights, documentation, and fine-tuning tools planned for
release later this month.

 SCHNEIDER ELECTRIC AGREES TO ACQUIRE PTC FOR $22.6B (5 MINUTE READ)
[6]

 Schneider Electric agreed to buy PTC in an all-cash deal valuing its
equity at $22.6 billion, with closing expected by Q3 2027 pending
approvals. The combination would connect PTC's product design and
engineering data with Schneider's operational and energy systems,
providing broader context for industrial AI applications.

 REGISTERED BUT NOT ROUTED: WHAT A GLOBAL AUDIT OF IPV6 SAYS ABOUT
DEPLOYMENT (8 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Researchers conducted a global audit comparing IPv6 registration
records from Regional Internet Registries against live BGP routing
tables and the IPv6 Hitlist project. They found that while 92.5
percent of registered IPv6 netblocks appear in live routing, only 79.4
percent of the underlying address space does, revealing a large gap
concentrated in unannounced legacy allocations and carrier-grade NAT
deployments. Furthermore, cross-checking against the IPv6 Hitlist
project identified 28.1 million genuinely distinct, confirmed-live
hosts, highlighting a massive amount of unrouted and unmonitored
address space worldwide.

 AI PRODUCTIVITY NEEDS AN ORGANIZATIONAL REDESIGN (12 MINUTE READ) [8]


 McKinsey argues that individual productivity gains will struggle to
translate into business value unless companies redesign workflows,
decision rights, and career paths around human–agent teams. Its
proposed approach organizes teams around outcomes, reduces handoffs,
and explicitly assigns responsibility for agent actions and the
capacity automation frees up.

 HOW ORACLE GIVES AI AGENTS ACCESS WITHOUT HANDING OVER CONTROL (9
MINUTE READ) [9]

 Oracle details how it preserves employee permissions across MCP
connections and limits coding and operations agents through temporary
access, least privilege, and human approval for sensitive actions. The
deployment offers a practical example of enforcing authorization in
backend systems as assistants move from retrieving information to
executing work.

 APACHE ICEBERG 1.12.0 RELEASE (9 MINUTE READ) [11]

 The Apache Iceberg community has announced the release of version
1.12.0, featuring significant performance enhancements, expanded write
capabilities for Variant and geospatial data types, and new
maintenance tasks. Key updates include faster unshredded Variant reads
in Spark, multi-engine shredded Variant writes, and Flink-native
resolution of equality deletes into row-position deletion vectors. The
release also introduces Hilbert-curve clustering for multi-dimensional
sorting, expands REST catalog and OpenAPI protocol features, and lays
groundwork for Table Format V4.

 SERVICENOW FLOW BRINGS A CHAT-FIRST SERVICE DESK TO SMALLER BUYERS (6
MINUTE READ) [12]

 ServiceNow has introduced Flow, an internal startup product offering
a chat-first service desk that operates within Slack, Microsoft Teams,
and email. Priced at $2 per conversation and $1 per automation run
with a $10,000 annual minimum, Flow allows smaller companies and
existing customers to bypass traditional portals and ticket forms.
Flow's credit card accessibility and public pricing will open
ServiceNow to smaller buyers while challenging third-party chat layers
built on top of the platform.

 HACKING THE GO COMPILER TO EFFICIENTLY MAP IPV4 TO IPV6 (29 MINUTE
READ) [13]

 The Go compiler handles mapping IPv4 addresses to IPv4-mapped IPv6
addresses inefficiently: the idiomatic netip.AddrFrom16 and As16
pattern runs eight times slower than a native method because of
suboptimal compiler optimization. Standard library changes, an unsafe
proxy, and custom IR and SSA rewrite rules can eliminate redundant
byte-packing and unpacking overhead and achieve native-level
performance.

 AMAZON EXPLORES AN $8B NVIDIA CHIP SALE-LEASEBACK (4 MINUTE READ)
[14]

 Amazon is reportedly discussing a financing arrangement that would
sell roughly $8 billion of Nvidia chips into a special-purpose vehicle
and lease them back for continued use. The proposal highlights how
hyperscalers are seeking outside capital to fund AI expansion while
investors take on risks tied to hardware depreciation and future
compute demand.
