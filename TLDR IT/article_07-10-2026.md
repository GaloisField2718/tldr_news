NEWS & TRENDS

 ATLASSIAN WARNS OF CRITICAL FILE ACCESS FLAW IN ITS DATACENTER
PRODUCTS (2 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Atlassian has issued an urgent warning regarding a critical 9.3-rated
arbitrary file access vulnerability, tracked as CVE-2026-21589, that
affects multiple Data Center products, including Bitbucket and
Confluence. The flaw allows unauthenticated attackers to access
specific files within the web application root directory if they know
the exact filename and path. Atlassian has released fixed versions and
recommends immediately patching affected installations. Organizations
unable to patch should restrict external access or apply the published
mitigations.

 THE INTERNET'S DNS ROOT KEY CHANGES OCTOBER 11 (9 MINUTE READ) [8]

 The DNS root will switch to a new key-signing key on October 11, only
the second such rollover ever. Most organizations need to do nothing,
but anyone operating DNSSEC-validating resolvers should verify that
their infrastructure trusts KSK-2024 or risk DNS resolution failures.

ANALYSIS & OPINIONS

 INFERENCE IS THE MOST IMPORTANT MARKET IN SOFTWARE (2 MINUTE READ)
[10]

 AI inference could become a larger software market than databases,
with spending projected to reach $350 billion by 2027. Rising
inference costs will pressure software companies' gross margins,
forcing them to improve efficiency through smaller models, better
harnesses, and smarter routing techniques.

 INTEL CIO: DON'T JUST PUT CHATBOTS ON TOP OF BROKEN PROCESSES (5
MINUTE READ) [11]

 Intel CIO Cynthia Stoddard argues that companies should measure AI
through business outcomes such as cycle-time improvements and ROI
rather than tool adoption. Intel's approach combines employee training
and experimentation with permission controls, data governance, MCP
controls, and observability, while redesigning the underlying
processes AI is meant to improve.

 ENTERPRISE SECURITY NEEDS TO START GOVERNING WHAT AI AGENTS DO, NOT
JUST WHAT THEY CAN ACCESS (6 MINUTE READ) [12]

 Traditional access controls answer whether an identity has
permission, but autonomous agents introduce a second question: whether
a permitted action actually matches the user's intent. The piece
argues for behavioral governance, sensitive-data controls, audit
trails, and targeted human approval for high-impact actions rather
than treating AI security as another SSO problem.
