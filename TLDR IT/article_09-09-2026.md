TLDR IT 2026-09-09

 HARDWARE VOLATILITY IS BECOMING AN IT PROBLEM (SPONSOR) [4]

 AI demand isn't just reshaping compute. It's putting pressure on
memory, storage, and other critical components, creating tighter
supply, volatile pricing, and longer procurement timelines. For IT
teams, that can turn hardware ownership into a source of financial and
operational risk.

RETHINKING INFRASTRUCTURE IN A VOLATILE HARDWARE MARKET [5] explores
how organizations can reduce their exposure to hardware volatility,
improve predictability, and scale infrastructure without being locked
into procurement cycles or overprovisioning.

NEWS & TRENDS

 ORACLE MAY BE NEXT IN EU SOFTWARE LICENSING HOT SEAT FOLLOWING SAP
DEAL (2 MINUTE READ) [6]

 MLex reports the European Commission (EC) has started examining
Oracle's software licensing practices ahead of a possible formal
investigation, with one source saying regulators are seeking
third-party views. The scrutiny follows the Commission's SAP probe,
which ended with binding commitments to abolish reinstatement fees and
reduce back-maintenance fees. An EC spokesperson said there is
currently no formal investigation into any company.

 META LAUNCHES AI AGENT THAT CAN ACCESS OTHER APPS TO SEND EMAILS,
MAKE PAYMENTS (4 MINUTE READ) [7]

 Meta launched Muse, an AI agent that can connect to apps including
email, calendars, payments, shopping, and smart home systems, then
take actions on a user's behalf. Each agent runs in its own virtual
machine and can continue working in the background, providing a
preview of how quickly AI assistants are moving from answering
questions to operating across real systems.

 SWITZERLAND BEGINS TESTING ALTERNATIVES TO MICROSOFT 365 TO PROTECT
ITS "MOST SENSITIVE DATA" AND STRENGTHEN DIGITAL SOVEREIGNTY (2 MINUTE
READ) [8]

 Switzerland's federal government is piloting Microsoft 365
alternatives across roughly 3,000 workstations, or about 7% of its
administrative workforce. Data sovereignty is the primary driver, with
licensing costs also contributing to a broader European push to reduce
dependence on US-based workplace software.

ANALYSIS & OPINIONS

 SOFTWARE HAS NEVER BEEN CAPITAL LIGHT (7 MINUTE READ) [9]

 Enterprise software's "capital lightness" is an accounting illusion,
because $50M spent on engineers is expensed as R&D while an equivalent
factory machine is capitalized. Software requires constant maintenance
labor to fight decay and outsources physical infrastructure to cloud
hyperscalers spending ~$700B. High gross margins therefore reflect
upfront sunk costs, not freedom from capital.

 INTRODUCING AI-AS-A-SERVICE (5 MINUTE READ) [10]

 Agentic AI could change SaaS from software designed primarily for
people clicking through interfaces into services increasingly consumed
by agents acting across multiple systems. Vendors and IT teams will
need clearer permissions, monitoring, and guardrails as software
starts performing work instead of simply exposing functionality.

MISCELLANEOUS

 SHUFFLE-SHARD EVERYTHING (6 MINUTE READ) [15]

 Shuffle-sharding is a low-cost way to reduce noisy-neighbor blast
radius in multitenant systems by assigning tenants to overlapping
subsets of workers rather than fixed shards. The technique lowers the
chance tenants share identical resources without extra hardware. It
requires tolerance for partial degradation and, for ordered workloads,
consistent hashing by resource.

 MACHINE ORGANIZATIONS (8 MINUTE READ) [16]

 AI labs like OpenAI and Anthropic could soon become “machine
organizations” where AI replaces researchers, managers, and
executives while human support staff remain. Such a shift would leave
customers and investors largely unaffected short-term but would
undermine government oversight and shift power from employees to
models, potentially enabling models to dictate terms.
